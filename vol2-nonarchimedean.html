Non-Archimedean Contact Geometry

A direct-transcription definition of p-adic contact manifolds and Reeb vector fields, a proposed p-adic contact Darboux theorem with one specific unverified gap named in advance, and a proof — not just an account — that the Collatz map admits no single p-adic power-series continuation, via Strassmann's theorem. Closes with a second, unrelated obstruction theorem (Groenewold–Van Hove) for comparison.

Status of this draft. This page proposes a definition, not a settled theory. Section 3's central theorem is (S): it reduces to one named technical lemma from the existing p-adic symplectic literature that has not been checked line-by-line against the contact case. Section 4 is (C) throughout; Section 5.1 (added 2026-09-28) proves the Non-Continuation Theorem in full (T), closing OP3. Abstract

p-adic (non-archimedean) symplectic geometry is an active research direction: Crespo and Pelayo have, over 2024–2025, constructed p-adic Hamiltonian vector fields, studied a p-adic Jaynes–Cummings model, proved a p-adic analogue of Darboux's theorem, and classified group actions on p-adic symplectic manifolds. No non-archimedean analogue of contact geometry — the odd-dimensional, dissipation-admitting cousin of symplectic geometry this series' Generative Contact Mechanics (GCM) framework depends on — appears anywhere in that literature. This page proposes one, by the same direct-transcription method already used for the p-adic symplectic case: replace \(\mathbb{R}\) by \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) in the standard definitions, and verify that the field-independent algebraic facts underneath them — that a non-degenerate alternating form admits a symplectic basis, and the wedge-power combinatorics of a contact form — carry over unchanged. It defines p-adic contact manifolds and Reeb vector fields, verifies both on the standard model, states a p-adic contact Darboux theorem whose proof reduces to one explicit gap, and closes by proving precisely why the Collatz map still resists this framework — the same Smoothness Bridge obstruction as in the real-contact Collatz chapter, recurring unchanged when the ambient geometry changes, and now settled by Strassmann's theorem rather than merely asserted (§5.1). A closing section cites, without claiming originality, a second and unrelated obstruction theorem (Groenewold–Van Hove, classical/quantum mechanics) for comparison of genre.

Part I · The Precise Gap in the Existing Literature

1. What p-adic symplectic geometry already has, and does not have

Symplectic geometry over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) has real content. Crespo and Pelayo's definition of a p-adic symplectic manifold is a direct transcription of the real one — a p-adic analytic manifold with a closed, non-degenerate p-adic analytic 2-form — and given such \((M,\omega)\) and a p-adic analytic \(H:M\to\mathbb{Q}_p\), they construct the unique p-adic analytic Hamiltonian vector field \(X_H\) solving \(\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH\), exactly as in the real case, computing its flow explicitly for the p-adic harmonic oscillator and a p-adic Jaynes–Cummings model. They further prove a p-adic Moser path method and use it to show every p-adic symplectic manifold is locally symplectomorphic to the standard model, with no local invariants beyond dimension — exactly as in the real case.

What none of these papers contain is an odd-dimensional analogue admitting genuine dissipation. That gap matters specifically for this series: this framework's own argument (after Bravetti, Cruz & Tapias, Contact Hamiltonian mechanics, 2017) is that symplectic geometry cannot host a hyperbolic attracting limit cycle at all — Liouville's theorem forbids volume contraction under a Hamiltonian flow. If a non-archimedean version of this series' dm³ construction is to exist, it needs a non-archimedean contact structure, not a non-archimedean symplectic one. Part I below supplies the definition that gap requires.

Part II · p-adic Contact Manifolds

2. Definition, standard model, Reeb field

2.1 Definition

Let \(p\) be a prime and let \(M\) be a \((2n{+}1)\)-dimensional p-adic analytic manifold in the sense of Serre, as used by Crespo–Pelayo. A p-adic contact form on \(M\) is a p-adic analytic 1-form \(\alpha\) such that \(\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n\) is a nowhere-vanishing p-adic analytic \((2n{+}1)\)-form. The pair \((M,\alpha)\) is a p-adic contact manifold. Two contact forms define the same p-adic contact structure iff they differ by a nowhere-vanishing p-adic analytic function; the associated hyperplane distribution \(\xi=\ker(\alpha)\) is then independent of the representative, since the proof that two 1-forms with the same corank-1 kernel are proportional is linear algebra over a field of characteristic 0, true over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) without modification. Every term in this definition — p-adic analytic manifold, 1-form, wedge product, exterior derivative — is already defined and in routine use in the p-adic symplectic literature; nothing here is p-specific.

2.2 The standard model

On \(M=(\mathbb{Q}_p)^{2n+1}\) with coordinates \((x_1,\dots,x_n,y_1,\dots,y_n,z)\), define \(\alpha_0 = dz - \sum_i y_i\,dx_i\). Then \(d\alpha_0=\sum_i dx_i\wedge dy_i\), and the wedge-power identity \((d\alpha_0)^n = n!\,(dx_1\wedge dy_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n\wedge dy_n)\) holds by the same combinatorial argument as the real case — cross terms with a repeated index vanish, and \(n!\) counts orderings of the \(n\) distinct summands. This is an identity in the exterior algebra over any commutative ring, so it transfers to \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) unchanged, and it is checked here symbolically (exact rational arithmetic, not a numerical approximation) for \(n=1,2,3,4\) rather than taken on faith.

[T] · checked · verify.py §1 \(\alpha_0\wedge(d\alpha_0)^n = n!\cdot dz\wedge dx_1\wedge dy_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n\wedge dy_n\), which is nowhere vanishing for every prime \(p\) since \(n!\) is a fixed nonzero rational and \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) has characteristic 0. Unlike the \(p=2\) / \(p\bmod 4\) case distinctions that appear elsewhere in the p-adic symplectic literature, this particular non-degeneracy check carries no dependence on \(p\) at all. \((M,\alpha_0)\) is a p-adic contact manifold for every prime.

2.3 The Reeb vector field

Proposition (Reeb vector field). Let \((M,\alpha)\) be a p-adic contact manifold. There is a unique p-adic analytic vector field \(R\) with \(\alpha(R)=1\) and \(\iota_R(d\alpha)=0\). Non-degeneracy of \(\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n\) forces \(d\alpha\) restricted to \(\xi=\ker\alpha\) to be a non-degenerate alternating form on the \(2n\)-dimensional \(\mathbb{Q}_p\)-vector space \(\xi_m\) at each point — a linear-algebra fact independent of the base field in characteristic 0. Splitting \(T_mM=\xi_m\oplus\mathbb{Q}_p\cdot v\) for any \(v\) with \(\alpha(v)\neq0\), the two conditions determine \(R_m\) uniquely by inverting a matrix over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) — the same Cramer's-rule argument Crespo–Pelayo use for analytic dependence of \(X_H\) on the base point. Analyticity of \(R\) follows because matrix inversion is p-adic analytic away from where the determinant vanishes, which by non-degeneracy is never.

[T] · checked · verify.py §2 For the standard model, \(R=\partial/\partial z\): \(\alpha_0(\partial_z)=1\) and \(d\alpha_0(\partial_z,\cdot)=0\) identically, since \(d\alpha_0\) involves only \(dx_i, dy_i\). This matches this series' own real-contact toy model exactly — \(\alpha = dz - r^2\,d\theta\) also has Reeb field \(\partial_z\) — a useful consistency check that the definition reduces correctly under \(\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{Q}_p\) in the one case both settings share.
Part III · Toward a p-adic Contact Darboux Theorem

3. The one named, unverified gap

The real contact Darboux theorem says every point of a contact manifold has a neighbourhood on which the contact form looks like the standard model. Its modern proof (Geiges, An Introduction to Contact Topology) uses Moser's path method, and is in one respect simpler than the symplectic Moser argument: solving for the isotopy-generating vector field \(X_t\) is pure linear algebra (Cartan's formula \(\mathcal{L}_{X_t}\alpha_t = f_t\alpha_t\), solvable pointwise from non-degeneracy of \(d\alpha_t\) on \(\ker\alpha_t\)), with no primitive-form integration step of the kind the symplectic Moser argument needs.

[S] · proposed, one gap named Theorem (p-adic contact Darboux; proposed). Let \((M,\alpha)\) be a \((2n{+}1)\)-dimensional p-adic contact manifold and \(m\in M\). There exist local p-adic analytic coordinates centred at \(m\) in which \(\alpha = dz - \sum y_i\,dx_i\).

Step 1 (T): every non-degenerate alternating form on a finite-dimensional vector space over a field admits a symplectic basis — standard bilinear algebra, true over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) unmodified, matching \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha_0\) at \(m\) to first order.

Step 2, the named gap (S): the real proof interpolates \(\alpha_t = (1-t)\alpha_0 + t\alpha\) over \(t\in[0,1]\) and integrates \(X_t\). Over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) this needs the same device Crespo–Pelayo built for their own p-adic symplectic Darboux theorem — a p-adic analogue of Moser's path method. This draft has not verified that construction line-by-line against the contact Cartan-formula equation above; it proposes that it transfers, on the grounds that the contact version of the Moser equation is more elementary (no primitive needed) than the symplectic one their method already handles — but this is a specific, nameable piece of verification work still to do, not a completed proof.

If Step 2 goes through, a p-adic contact classification by p-adic volume and a p-adic Darboux–Weinstein theorem for Legendrian neighbourhoods should follow by the same pattern as the symplectic case. Both are left (C), pending Step 2.

Part IV · Toward Dissipative p-adic Contact Dynamics

4. What a non-archimedean dm³ would need to say (proposed, conjectural)

[C] · open question, not an answer Everything in Parts II–III is still conservative: it transcribes structure, not dynamics with genuine loss. Given a p-adic contact manifold with Reeb field \(R\), one would define a p-adic analytic generative Hamiltonian \(H = H_{\text{phase}}+H_{\text{diss}}\) as in this series' GCM framework and seek the contact vector field solving the corresponding Euler–Lagrange system — a linear-algebraic transcription, by the same Cramer's-rule argument as the Reeb-field proposition above, since it needs no integration. The open content is not that transcription, which looks routine, but what “dissipative” and “contraction rate” should mean over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), which has no total order and hence no literal negative real eigenvalue. The natural substitute, already standard in p-adic dynamics (Silverman, The Arithmetic of Dynamical Systems): a linearised eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is p-adically contracting if \(|\lambda|_p < 1\) — the same Hensel/Newton sense already used to classify fixed points of p-adic dynamical systems. Under this substitution this series' stability threshold becomes \(|\lambda|_p<1\), and an outer-stability-radius analogue would need a p-adic Grönwall-type inequality that does not yet exist in this form. This whole section states what the right question is, not an answer to it.
Part V · Closing a Loop, Not Opening One

5. Why Collatz still does not fit, precisely

Every object above, real or proposed, requires p-adic analyticity: the atlas, the contact form, the Hamiltonian \(H\), all locally given by convergent power series. The Collatz map on \(\mathbb{Z}_2\), \(T(n)=n/2\) for \(n\) even and \(T(n)=3n{+}1\) for \(n\) odd, is not visibly a single p-adic analytic function on all of \(\mathbb{Z}_2\): it is analytic separately on \(2\mathbb{Z}_2\) and on \(1+2\mathbb{Z}_2\), with no evident single convergent power series agreeing with \(T\) on both cosets at once. This is the exact same obstruction already named Gap G1 (the Smoothness Bridge) in this series' real-contact Collatz chapter — dm³'s axioms need \(C^2\) flows, and Collatz is only piecewise-defined — recurring unchanged when the ambient geometry moves from real contact to p-adic contact or symplectic. This is also why the existing p-adic Hamiltonian/integrable-systems literature studies transplanted classical-mechanics models (oscillator, spin, Jaynes–Cummings) rather than arithmetic maps: those Hamiltonians are honest single power series, and Collatz is not.

5.1 The obstruction is a proof, not an impression

That \(T\) is "not analytic" on \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) is already asserted in the 3x+1 literature — Matthews and Watts (1984) and Müller (1991), as reported in Chamberland's 2003 survey, list the 2-adic extension of \(T\) as surjective, non-injective, infinitely differentiable, measure-preserving and strongly mixing, but not analytic. None of the copies of that claim checked for this page carry a proof reachable from here. The following is short enough to include in full, and isolates exactly the fact responsible: gluing two distinct entire functions along a nontrivial clopen partition of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) is never itself entire.

[T] · proved here Lemma (clopen gluing is never entire, except trivially). Let \(p\) be prime and let \(\mathbb{Z}_p = A \sqcup B\) be a partition into two nonempty, infinite clopen residue classes (e.g. \(A=p\mathbb{Z}_p\), \(B\) its complement). Let \(f_A, f_B\) be p-adic power series each convergent on all of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) (elements of the Tate algebra \(\mathbb{Q}_p\langle X\rangle\), coefficients tending to 0). If \(f_A \ne f_B\) as power series, no single \(h \in \mathbb{Q}_p\langle X\rangle\) restricts to \(f_A\) on \(A\) and to \(f_B\) on \(B\).

Proof. Suppose such \(h\) exists. Then \(h - f_A \in \mathbb{Q}_p\langle X\rangle\) and vanishes on all of \(A\), which is infinite. By Strassmann's theorem (1928) — a nonzero element of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\langle X\rangle\) has only finitely many zeros in the closed unit disc, bounded by its Weierstrass degree — a power series in \(\mathbb{Q}_p\langle X\rangle\) vanishing at infinitely many points of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) is identically zero. So \(h=f_A\) as power series, hence as functions on all of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\), not just \(A\). The same argument on \(B\) gives \(h=f_B\) everywhere. Then \(f_A = h = f_B\), contradicting \(f_A\ne f_B\). \(\blacksquare\)
[T] · proved here · closes OP3 Theorem (the Non-Continuation Theorem). There is no \(h\in\mathbb{Q}_2\langle X\rangle\) with \(h(x)=x/2\) for every \(x\in 2\mathbb{Z}_2\) and \(h(x)=3x+1\) for every \(x\in 1+2\mathbb{Z}_2\).

Proof. Apply the Lemma with \(p=2\), \(A=2\mathbb{Z}_2\), \(B=1+2\mathbb{Z}_2\), \(f_A(x)=x/2\), \(f_B(x)=3x+1\) — both polynomials, hence trivially in \(\mathbb{Q}_2\langle X\rangle\), and distinct (constant terms 0 and 1). \(\blacksquare\)

Equivalently, without the Lemma's generality: if \(h\) agreed with \(x/2\) on all of \(2\mathbb{Z}_2\), Strassmann forces \(h\equiv x/2\) everywhere on \(\mathbb{Z}_2\), in particular on \(1+2\mathbb{Z}_2\); but then \(h\) would also have to equal \(3x+1\) there, forcing \(x/2=3x+1\), i.e. \(x=-2/5\), for every \(x\) in the infinite set \(1+2\mathbb{Z}_2\) — impossible for a nonzero affine equation.

This resolves option (i) below negatively and completely: the search for an actual p-adic analytic continuation of Collatz was not merely unattempted, it is impossible, for the reason Strassmann's theorem gives and no more exotic one. What survives is exactly what Part II already requires only locally: \(T\) is analytic in a neighbourhood of every point of \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) (each residue class is itself a clopen neighbourhood on which \(T\) is a single polynomial), so \(T\) is locally p-adic analytic on all of \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) without contradiction — the Lemma only forbids upgrading that to a single global power series.

Two ways this obstruction could be addressed remain, one of them now closed: (i) find an actual p-adic analytic function on \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) whose restriction to each parity coset recovers Collatz's action there — closed by the Non-Continuation Theorem above: impossible, and a different, more demanding requirement than the real-valued logarithmic near-conjugacy construction used elsewhere in this series, which is analytic over \(\mathbb{R}\), not \(\mathbb{Q}_2\), and does not bear on this question; or (ii) generalise the Part II definition to an orbifold or groupoid built from finitely many p-adic analytic charts glued along the residue-class boundaries, with its own non-degeneracy and gluing conditions not developed here — this is now the only remaining route.

Part VI · A Second Obstruction, By Way of Comparison

6. The Groenewold–Van Hove theorem (cited, not this page's result)

Section 5 proves that no single p-adic power series continues the Collatz map across the two parity residue classes of \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) — an obstruction to an exact global correspondence, established by a zero-counting argument (Strassmann). This section records a second, unrelated instance of the same genre of result, from classical/quantum mechanics rather than p-adic dynamics: an exact, structure-preserving correspondence a natural research programme wants, that a rigorous argument shows cannot exist beyond a limited domain. Nothing here is original to this page; it is included for the parallel, and because it is not otherwise cited anywhere in this series.

cited · Groenewold 1946; van Hove 1951 · not this page's result Theorem (Groenewold–Van Hove). No map \(Q\) from polynomial functions on classical phase space (\(\mathbb{R}^{2n}\), coordinates \(q_i,p_i\)) to self-adjoint operators can simultaneously satisfy linearity, \(Q(1)=I\), Dirac's correspondence rule \([Q(f),Q(g)]=i\hbar\,Q(\{f,g\})\), and irreducibility on the canonical pair, for the full polynomial algebra. Consistent quantization in this exact sense holds only for polynomials of degree \(\le 2\); the obstruction already appears at degree 3 (e.g. quantizing \(q^2p^2\) via two different, classically-equal Poisson-bracket decompositions gives operators differing at order \(\hbar^2\)).

What this does not show. The theorem obstructs an exact, all-degree correspondence. It says nothing against the asymptotic one: Ehrenfest's theorem, the WKB approximation, and decoherence all recover classical behaviour as \(\hbar\to 0\), and Weyl quantization gives a correspondence accurate to \(O(\hbar^2)\) — exactly the order at which the theorem's contradiction appears. Citing only the exact-obstruction half, as if classical and quantum mechanics were simply unrelated theories, overstates what is proved; citing only the asymptotic half ignores a genuine theorem. Full statement, proof, and references (Groenewold 1946; van Hove 1951; Gotay, Grundling & Tuynman 1996) in References [17]–[18] below.
