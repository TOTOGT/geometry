The Fourier transform and phase estimation

One transform behind Deutsch–Jozsa, phase estimation and Shor. Built two ways and compared.

Nielsen & Chuang, chapter 5; Kitaev, 1995

Part I · The transform

The quantum Fourier transform is a matrix, and a circuit

On N = 2n basis states the QFT sends |j〉 to N−1/2 Σk e2πijk/N|k〉. Its matrix Fjk = e2πijk/N/√N is unitary because the columns are orthonormal: Σj e2πij(k−k′)/N = N if k = k′ and 0 otherwise (a geometric series of roots of unity). The circuit that implements it uses n(n+1)/2 gates plus swaps: for each qubit a Hadamard followed by controlled phase rotations of angle 2π/2m from each later qubit, and a final bit reversal. The product formula behind it is |j〉 → ⊗l (|0〉 + e2πij/2l|1〉)/√2.

Two structural facts. Over the group Z2n the same construction is the n-fold Hadamard, the transform inside the Deutsch–Jozsa and Bernstein–Vazirani circuits. And F diagonalises the cyclic shift S|k〉 = |k+1〉: F†SF = diag(ωk) with ω = e±2πi/N, which is the whole reason the transform detects periods.

Part II · Phase estimation

Reading an eigenvalue off the Fourier transform

Let U|u〉 = e2πiφ|u〉. Controlled-U2k applied to a uniform superposition of control qubits leaves the register in T−1/2 Σk e2πikφ|k〉 (T = 2t), by phase kickback, and the inverse QFT turns that into a peak at φT. If φ = m/T exactly, the output is |m〉 with probability 1. In general, with δ = φ − m/T, summing the geometric series gives

P(m)  =  sin^2(pi T d) / ( T^2 sin^2(pi d) )

The 4/π² bound. For the nearest estimate |δ| ≤ 1/(2T). Using sin x ≥ 2x/π on [0, π/2] gives sin(πTδ) ≥ 2T|δ|, and sin(πδ) ≤ π|δ|, so P ≥ (2Tδ)²/(T²π²δ²) = 4/π² ≈ 0.405 for the best t-bit estimate. Nielsen and Chuang give the stronger form: with t = n + ⌈log(2 + 1/(2ε))⌉ qubits, n bits of φ are returned with probability at least 1 − ε.

From estimate to fraction. If φ = s/r and |s/r − φ′| ≤ 1/(2r²), then s/r is a convergent of the continued fraction of φ′ (Nielsen & Chuang, Theorem 5.1), so a t-bit estimate with 2−t ≤ 1/(2r²) recovers the exact rational. Kitaev's 1995 paper measures an eigenvalue exactly this way: refine to precision 2−2n−1, then take continued fractions.

  40 random (t, phi)                                         worst 5.2e-15

  estimate with t = 2 ceil(log2 q) + 1

Controls: dropping the bit reversal breaks circuit = matrix; using F in place of F† returns |−3 mod 16〉 = |13〉 instead of |3〉, reading the phase backwards (the first draft of this control claimed it returned “not |3〉” with a bound that was itself wrong: it returns a different state with probability 1); a non-dyadic phase never gives a single outcome with probability 1.

What this does not show. It simulates the circuit on a classical machine at up to 7 qubits, so it verifies the algebra, not a scaling claim about hardware. Approximate QFT, gate counts for controlled-U2k, and the cost of preparing the eigenstate are not treated.
