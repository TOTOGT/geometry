MATHEMATICS · QUANTUM COMPUTING

Measurement and CHSH

Why quantum correlations reach 2√2 and no further, from an operator identity rather than a grid.

Nielsen & Chuang, chapter 2 and Problem 2.3

Part I · Measurement

The Born rule, and what a second measurement does

Measuring in an orthonormal basis {|ek〉} gives outcome k with probability |〈ek|ψ〉|². These sum to 1 because the basis is complete. The state afterwards is the normalised projection Pk|ψ〉/‖Pk|ψ〉‖, so measuring again returns the same outcome with probability 1. For an observable A with A² = I (an ±1 observable such as a Pauli) the expectation is 〈ψ|A|ψ〉 and the variance is 1 − 〈A〉².

Uncertainty (Robertson). For any two observables, Var(A) Var(B) ≥ |〈[A,B]〉|²/4. With A = Z, B = X the commutator is [Z,X] = 2iY, so Var(Z) Var(X) ≥ 〈Y〉²: Z and X cannot both be sharp.

Part II · Entanglement

Schmidt decomposition and the singlet

Every two-qubit state has a Schmidt form √λ1|a1〉|b1〉 + √λ2|a2〉|b2〉, where λ1,2 are the eigenvalues of M M† for the 2×2 coefficient matrix M. The entanglement entropy is −Σ λi log2 λi: zero for a product state and exactly one bit for the singlet (|01〉 − |10〉)/√2, whose λ1 = λ2 = 1/2.

Part III · Bell and CHSH

Two parties, four settings, and a number that separates theories

Alice measures ±1 observables A0 or A1, Bob B0 or B1. Define S = 〈A0B0〉 + 〈A0B1〉 + 〈A1B0〉 − 〈A1B1〉. Local models. If the outcomes are fixed in advance, each of A0, A1, B0, B1 is ±1 and S = A0(B0 + B1) + A1(B0 − B1); one of the brackets is 0 and the other ±2, so |S| ≤ 2, and every mixture of such assignments obeys it too.

Quantum. On the singlet, for observables cosa Z + sina X and cosb Z + sinb X the correlator is −cos(a − b). At a0 = 0, a1 = π/2, b0 = π/4, b1 = −π/4 this gives |S| = 2√2 ≈ 2.828.

Tsirelson's bound, algebraically. For any A0, A1, B0, B1 with squares equal to I, let S = A0⊗(B0 + B1) + A1⊗(B0 − B1). Expanding S² and using the squares, the cross terms collect into a commutator:

S^2  =  4 I  -  [A0, A1] (x) [B0, B1]

Each commutator has norm at most 2, so ‖S‖² ≤ 4 + 4 = 8 and ‖S‖ ≤ 2√2. The identity needs only A² = B² = I, not Hermiticity, a point the checker's control found out the hard way (see below).

The same number as a game. A referee sends bits x, y to Alice and Bob, who may not communicate; they answer a, b and win when a ⊕ b = x·y. A win at xy = 0 means equal answers and at xy = 1 different answers, so the win probability is 1/2 + S/8. Consider the family of no-signalling boxes with uniform marginals and P(win) = (1+ε)/2: every correlator is ε(−1)xy and S = 4ε. Deterministic local strategies win at most 3/4 (8 of the 16 reach it), and mixing those 8 gives exactly the ε = 1/2 box, so ε > 1/2 is outside every local model. The singlet at the settings above, with Bob's observables negated to match the −cos convention, gives P(win) = cos2(π/8) ≈ 0.854, that is ε = 1/√2. The Popescu–Rohrlich box, ε = 1, wins always and has S = 4, so no-signalling alone does not give Tsirelson's bound. Barandes, Hasan and Kagan (Phys. Rev. A 114, 022208, 2026) re-derive 2√2 in their stochastic–quantum framework, where causal locality forces the unitary to factorise as UA⊗UB; the bound then follows from the same operator inequality used above. That framework is an interpretation, not consensus, and my reading of their paper is from the text supplied, not machine-checked.

Part IV · The part you can check

Computed from matrices, not from the formula

Born rule: probabilities sum to 1 (worst 1.1e-15); repeat measurement -> same outcome (6.7e-16)
Robertson: Var(Z)Var(X) >= |<[Z,X]>|^2/4 on 500 random states, minimum slack +9.8e-07
Schmidt: singlet entropy exactly 1 bit; product state 0
Correlator -cos(a-b) vs explicit kron(A(a),B(b)) on the singlet: 25 x 25 grid, worst 4.4e-16
CHSH local:   all 16 deterministic assignments: max |S| = 2
CHSH quantum: |S| = 2.828427124746190 = 2 sqrt 2;  16^4 angle choices: max 2.828427124746
Tsirelson identity S^2 = 4I - [A0,A1](x)[B0,B1]: 60 random observable quadruples, worst 6.2e-15
spectral norm of S over those: max 2.818011 <= 2 sqrt 2
Game: box family eps in [0,1] is no-signalling; win = (1+eps)/2, S = 4 eps (101 values, worst 4.4e-16)
Local: 16 deterministic strategies, best win 3/4 (8 reach it); uniform mixture of those 8 = the eps = 1/2 box exactly
Quantum: singlet box wins cos^2(pi/8) = 0.8535533906 = (1+1/sqrt 2)/2; PR box (eps = 1) wins 1, S = 4

One game check was vacuous on first writing: my no-signalling test compared Alice's marginal across her own input, which may vary, and passed only because every marginal in the family is uniform. A box where Alice's answer equals Bob's input exposed it (the test scored 0.00 on a box that signals); it now compares across the other party's input and scores 1.00 there. A second control confirms the sign convention: with Bob's observables un-negated the singlet wins only 0.146.

Controls, each must fail and does: −cos(2(a−b)) in place of −cos(a−b) against the explicit singlet; a product state on the same grid stays at max |S| = 1.755, inside the local bound; and a non-involutory A0 breaks the identity. The checker's first control for that last point used the matrix [[1,1],[0,−1]], which is non-Hermitian but does square to I, so the identity held and the control did not fail. It was replaced with [[1,1],[0,1]], whose square is not I.

What this does not show. The 164 grid is evidence for Tsirelson's bound; the algebraic argument is the proof, and the script checks that identity on random samples, not symbolically. It does not address loopholes in real Bell tests, or Bell nonlocality beyond CHSH.
