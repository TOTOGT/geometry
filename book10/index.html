Two letters do the work of this volume's name. X is ten, and this is the tenth book. X is also the Africa term in BRIX — the variable standing for a continent that has ratified a continental trade treaty and has not yet acquired a single voice to negotiate under it. The series numbers the book; the second meaning is its subject.

1 · Why this volume exists

The earlier volumes of this series ask what a structure permits. A tiling permits six neighbours and not seven. A contact manifold permits a Reeb field. A fold permits exactly one degenerate parameter. In each case the interesting sentence is not that something happened but that something could not, and the proof is the boundary.

Economic arrangements have boundaries of the same kind and they are almost never stated. A country that has committed most of a commodity's output for twenty years cannot sell it to a new buyer, cannot switch when terms move against it, and cannot supply its own downstream industry. Those are not predictions about what it will do. They are statements about what it can no longer do, and they are as hard as any geometric constraint — harder, because a lattice can be redesigned and a signed contract cannot.

The constraint is invisible in every public statistic. Trade data records where goods went. It does not record what is already promised, and no party to the promise has an interest in publishing it. That gap is the subject of this volume.

The volume's one methodological commitment

A quantity that cannot be computed from stated sources is not a finding, it is a position. Every chapter here either gives the computation or names precisely what is missing and who holds it. Where this series has failed that standard before — and it has, repeatedly and in public — the failures are recorded in the audit log rather than quietly corrected.

2 · The three standing questions

The volume is organised around three measurables. None is published by anyone today. Each is constructible from data that institutions already hold.

3 · Where the political science enters

It enters at the point where the mathematics stops being neutral, and that point is identifiable.

A measurement is neutral while it describes a state. It stops being neutral when it describes an option, because an option is held by somebody and its removal is a transfer. The central claim of Chapter 1 is that a long-horizon supply commitment is the sale of an option that no accounting standard records as sold: the option to negotiate with somebody else. The price paid is visible. The thing sold is not on the balance sheet of either party.

That is a claim about bargaining structure rather than about anyone's conduct, which is what makes it sayable. It requires no accusation of bad faith by any buyer, and it applies identically to counterparties nobody objects to. It is also why the remedy proposed is disclosure of an aggregate and not the abrogation of contracts.

5 · The room

None of these three questions is settled by one author, and two of them cannot be: they need the parties who hold the inputs — exchanges, regulators, index providers, ministries, and the market-data desks that already compute most of what is required. The G6 Chamber is the standing forum convened for that, and it is deliberately not a terminal: no subscription, no seat fee, no data licence, and no requirement to hold a chair in order to take the floor. Access is by invitation while the room is small; write to the convenor.

What would make this volume wrong

If the encumbrance ratio turns out to be computable from existing public sources for a representative set of countries and commodities, then the gap this volume is built on does not exist and the argument collapses into a data-presentation exercise. That is the first thing to try, and the volume would rather be wrong quickly than right slowly.
