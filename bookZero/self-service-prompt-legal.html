A Self-Service Prompt for Legal

For someone who does not know how to ask the right questions to their AI. Copy the prompt below, fill in the brackets, and paste it as your first message.

This is not legal advice. You do not need a lawyer to fill out a form, and this page is for doing it yourself. It makes the AI show its work, so that you can check it and know what to ask the agency itself.

Why a prompt at all

An AI answers the question you typed, with confidence, even when the question was thin. In one real conversation (November 2026) the answer to "does she mean I have to pass the bar and then apply?" changed once the person pasted the exact wording of the job advert instead of describing it. The first answer was not wrong because the AI was careless. It was wrong because it had only a summary. Most of the prompt below exists to prevent that.

The clock comes first

A status question is usually a deadline question. A person who wants to change from a visitor status to a student status files a form (the I-539) while the stay they were given is still running. When that date passes, filing it may no longer be possible, and no amount of correct paperwork afterward changes that. So the first thing to find is the date the stay ends. It is written on the I-94 record, not on the visa. A visa can run for years while the permitted stay runs for weeks, and people confuse the two.

Everything else is built backward from that date: the school has to issue its paper, a fee has to be paid, funds have to be shown, the package has to be mailed or filed online. Each step takes time you do not control. The prompt below therefore starts with the clock and asks the AI to give you a dated list, not an explanation.

Two things to check yourself on the official page for the form, because rules and fees change: whether your type of entry allows this change at all (some visa-free entries do not), and what the form's instructions say about filing late. Do not rely on a summary, including this one.

The prompt

After the answer: five checks

Look up every citation. Open the regulation or manual it names and read the section. If you cannot find it, treat the claim as a guess.

Ask it to argue the other side. "Now tell me why this might be wrong for my facts." A good answer survives this.

Ask who decides. Some questions are settled by text. Others are settled by an officer, a court or a school's own policy, and no AI can settle those.

Ask the person who wrote it. If the doubt is about an advert or an email, the writer knows what they meant. A short, polite question is often faster than a long guess.

Check the date. Rules change, and an AI may describe an older version.

A note on code that looks like law

The same conversation went on to a Lean 4 file that claimed to check eligibility rules for a change of status. Lean checks that the logic is consistent. It does not check that the rules written into it match the real law. Several conditions in that file were the AI's own wording, and they were not checked against the regulations. A proof that compiles is only as good as the statements typed into it. Nothing from that file is reproduced here.
