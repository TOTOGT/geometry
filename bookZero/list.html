  7. 1994

    Punk

    StatedBy 1994 he had decided to play with the band instead of continuing the music school program. Punk rock felt like his thing.

    StatedThe band was called MOFO.

    StatedThe kids from CASEB made him a bass player.

    StatedHe revived the Grêmio Estudantil (student union) at CASEB, served as its cultural director, and planned a concert for the Festa Junina.

    StatedThe concert happened, his parents saw him play, and he remembers it as funny.

    StatedStudents had been forbidden by the military government to organize.

    StatedThe UNE (União Nacional dos Estudantes, the national student union) had lapsed and had returned. When the student movement was reformed, it led to Caras Pintadas.

    StatedThere were no links between the scene and the student movement. Punks and skaters were not friends, and did not necessarily talk to each other, just because they skateboarded or played in a band. Bands shared material so they could pass their message. In the 90s, bands mostly wrote to each other with cassette tapes, which is why the sleeves carry the addresses and phone numbers of the people behind them. Bands also wrote to venues, trying to tour, to papers and zines, and to TV stations.

    StatedMemory: another band played too. He also remembers a kid they called Turco, who he doesn’t think was Turkish. He can’t remember the other band’s name.

    StatedMemory: the Festa Junina was pretty fun. At that party he met a red-haired girl who went to the same school in the afternoon program. He wishes he had kept a picture or an album of those days.

    OpenThe band’s members (names only with their agreement), when the Grêmio at CASEB was last active before he revived it (the military government ended in 1985, as far as I know, so it may have lapsed afterward), who else served with him, the other band’s members (the name is not remembered), what Turco’s role was (band, friend or classmate) and why he got the nickname, what was funny about it, what happened with the red-haired girl, whether he left the music school completely, the first bass he played, which tapes he sent or received, and who he wrote to, and whether any photos exist (parents, classmates, CASEB).

    StatedReflection: being a teenager was the time to make mistakes, so he was a punk trying to err a little, and he had to learn a lot from his own actions.

    If you were there, help me remember. CASEB, Brasília, 1994: the band MOFO, the Grêmio Estudantil, the Festa Junina concert. Do you remember the other bands that played, the kid we called Turco, the photos, or anything else from that day? Your memories will be added to this timeline, with your permission.

  10. 1997 (age 17)

    Atitude

    StatedIn 1997 Atitude, a compilation (coletânea) with S.L.U. and other bands, was released. S.L.U. and the other bands on it went on to perform on TV, and S.L.U. was featured on the first of many appearances.

    StatedThe TV station had them do playback, so they “performed” instead of playing the music.

    StatedThey were there to share a message. There was a quick interview in which they got to speak and answer questions, and then they pretended to play. He turned the bass the wrong way, as a joke, and the whole band started doing macaquices (monkey business, clowning around).

    StatedHis essay says S.L.U. tracks appear on the Atitude compilation.

  14. September 1999

    Two schools

    StatedClasses started in September 1999. He attended two schools: Geologia (geology) during the day, at the UnB (Universidade de Brasília), and Tourism at night. The night school was UPIS, where he earned a bacharelado (bachelor’s degree) in Brasília. He used credits from the UnB there.

    Stated“I could have had the time of my life there, but too many friends, and a large campus, made it more difficult to study. I had a private place to study at night where all my classes took place on the same building and the convenience of getting that degree while I went to work made sense since I was going to be a parent.”

    StatedMemory: at the UnB, if he didn’t stop to say hello to anyone he could just make it across campus in time to sit for the next class, with no food and nothing else. But there were friends everywhere, for blocks, all trying to get him to chat. He was tempted. There was a swimming pool to take a dip in. He was going to find it awesome, and he did, for one semester.

    StatedHe gave the UnB a document, a procedure permitted in Brazil, asking to return to the program at a later date to finish it. He thinks the UnB may have held his place for a few years, but is sure it doesn’t excuse you forever.

    OpenThe field of the UPIS degree (read here as Tourism) and the year he finished it (after this timeline ends), how many credits transferred and from which UnB courses, what the document was called (trancamento de matrícula, enrollment suspension, as far as I know) and when he filed it, whether the UnB ever held his place and for how long, whether he ever returned, which of the two the quote refers to (read here as the UnB for the large campus and UPIS for the single building), how he balanced both with Ganesh Emporium and teaching, and how each one went.
