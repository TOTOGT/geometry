01Why this matters right now: New Jersey’s air

Through the summer of 2026, smoke from more than 800 Canadian wildfires has repeatedly pushed New Jersey into unhealthy air. The state issued Code Red air-quality alerts, and the New Jersey Department of Health cautioned residents to stay indoors. This is not a distant problem — it is Newark’s sky.

I raise this first because it is exactly the kind of issue where independent, technically grounded, locally rooted voices are useful to the state: connecting satellite-scale science to the health of a specific city, and to the infrastructure and monitoring decisions that follow.

02What I would bring to the board

Climate resilience & distributed energy

Helical Vortex Energy Harvesting (HVEH)

A proposed approach to distributed, low-head hydrokinetic generation and flood-resilience for New Jersey watercourses (e.g., the Second River / Belleville corridor) — year-round baseload from existing flow, sited where municipal power and stormwater resilience overlap. Presented as an engineering concept and development case, not a deployed system. Background →

Environmental science & air quality

Transcontinental smoke transport

Published modeling of how wildfire smoke crosses the continent and degrades downwind air — the mechanism behind New Jersey’s recurring Code Red days. A reproducible data pipeline (PM2.5 vs. mixed-layer depth) accompanies the paper. Read →

Advanced manufacturing

Sustainable aviation fuel (ethanol → hydrocarbons)

An early-stage SAF process concept, with analytical validation (DRIFTS) planned at NJIT’s Otto York Center. Funding avenue; not yet secured.

Trust & verification technology

Machine-checked proof (Lean 4 / AXLE)

I maintain AXLE, a formal-verification environment where mathematical claims are checked by a proof kernel rather than asserted. The discipline — nothing is “verified” without a clean kernel check — is the same rigor emerging AI and critical-software sectors increasingly need. Repository →

STEM education & workforce

Bringing the science to classrooms

Open, browser-based teaching tools (e.g., interactive Chladni nodal-set plates for math teachers) and a full dm³ course series — built to put working scientific instruments in front of students and educators for free. Example tool →
