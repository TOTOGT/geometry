Primitive

The part of a variety's algebraic classes that its polarization does not already generate — and the one question about it still open.

Pablo Nogueira Grossi · G6 LLC · 11 November 2026

What this page is
Three worked cases of the Hodge-type inequality, each computed exactly, and the lemma that stops them short of a general statement. It is not a proof of the standard conjectures and does not approach one. The questions it leaves open are collected, with what is known about each, on the open questions page. Each claim carries a tag: [COMPUTED] by a script that runs, [CITED] from a source, [ASSUME] taken as an input, [OPEN] not known.

1What primitive means

Take a smooth projective variety $X$ of dimension $d$ and an ample class $h$. Let $A^r$ be its algebraic classes of codimension $r$, taken rationally and modulo homological equivalence, and let $L$ be multiplication by $h$. For $0\le r\le d/2$ the primitive classes are

Definition
$$P^r=\ker\bigl(L^{\,d-2r+1}:A^r\to A^{d-r+1}\bigr).$$ These are the classes in codimension $r$ that are not accounted for by multiplying lower-degree classes by $h$. When hard Lefschetz holds, $A^r=P^r\oplus L\,A^{r-1}$.

The Hodge-type standard conjecture says the signed pairing on this part is positive:

Hodge-type statement [CITED]
$$(-1)^r\deg\!\bigl(z^2\,h^{\,d-2r}\bigr)>0\qquad\text{for every nonzero }z\in P^r.$$ Formulation as in Milne (2002) and Grothendieck (1969). Known in characteristic $0$ by Hodge theory; over finite fields it is the open question.

Two things about the word. First, primitive is relative to $h$: change the polarization and the primitive space moves. On $\mathbf P^2\times\mathbf P^2$ with $h=sa+tb$ the primitive divisor class is $sa-tb$, which depends on $(s,t)$ [COMPUTED]. Second, the sign $(-1)^r$ is not decoration: that class has $\deg(h^2p_1^2)=-2s^2t^2<0$, so without the sign the inequality would fail [COMPUTED].

For the first two, "all primitive algebraic classes" is a true statement, not a statement about chosen generators, because both varieties are cellular: every cohomology class is algebraic, the Chow ring is $\mathbb Q[a,b]/(a^{m+1},b^{m+1})$, and the primitive spaces are computed as kernels. These two facts are inputs [ASSUME] and are not proved by the scripts. For $\mathbf P^3\times\mathbf P^3$ the dependence on $(s,t)$ is removed by the ring automorphism $a\mapsto sa$, $b\mapsto tb$, which reduces every ample rational $h$ to $s=t=1$ and multiplies the degree by $s^3t^3$ [COMPUTED].

3The full form is indefinite

On $\mathbf P^2\times\mathbf P^2$ the intersection form on the middle degree, in the basis $(a^2,ab,b^2)$, has matrix with rows $(0,0,1),(0,1,0),(1,0,0)$, signature $(2,1)$ [COMPUTED]. So positivity is a property of the primitive part only; asking it of all of $A^{d/2}$ is asking for something false.

4A shortcut that fails

One might hope to deduce positivity from two facts about Frobenius: its eigenvalues on the relevant cohomology have the right absolute value, and it is compatible with the intersection pairing. That does not follow. Take $F=q^2I_2$ and $J=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$. Every eigenvalue of $F$ has absolute value $q^2$, $F^{T}JF=q^4J$, and yet $J$ has a negative direction [COMPUTED]. This is a statement about $2\times 2$ matrices. It does not arise from a variety and is not a counterexample to any standard conjecture; it rules out one route only. The matrix identity is proved in FrobeniusCountermodel.F_similitude, which compiled with the standard axioms [COMPUTED].

5The open lemma

For $T\times S$ the computation above covers only the product-generated classes: the points of each factor and $NS(T)\otimes NS(S)$. What lies outside is the difficulty. There are algebraic classes in $H^2(T)\otimes H^2(S)$ not in $NS(T)\otimes NS(S)$ (correspondences between the surfaces' transcendental parts), and classes in $H^1\otimes H^3$ and $H^3\otimes H^1$ (homomorphisms between Albanese-type abelian varieties).

Open [OPEN]
Over a finite field, show that $(-1)^2\deg(z^2)$ is positive on the primitive algebraic classes of $T\times S$ outside the product-generated subspace. In characteristic $0$ this follows from Hodge theory. For the $H^1\otimes H^3$ parts, a recent report of Milne on abelian varieties may bear on it; how far it reaches was not checked, and its proofs were not audited. For the exotic $H^2\otimes H^2$ classes no reduction is known to me.

Separately, the Lefschetz-type conjecture asks that the inverse of $L^k$ be induced by an algebraic correspondence. That is a different obligation from positivity. For the cellular varieties above it holds because every class on $X\times X$ is algebraic [ASSUME]; nothing here bears on it for general $X$.

6Intersections as a graph

A symmetric matrix of intersection numbers is a weighted graph: a node for each class, an edge for each nonzero off-diagonal entry. On a surface this is a classical picture. Curves $E_1,\dots,E_n$ with $E_i^2=-2$ and $E_i\cdot E_j\in\{0,1\}$ that form a Dynkin diagram have intersection matrix $-C$, where $C$ is that diagram's Cartan matrix [CITED] (Artin; Du Val; standard). A caution that matters here: for an ample $H$ every effective curve has $H\cdot E_i>0$, so an actual exceptional chain is never itself primitive (primitive on a surface means $H\cdot D=0$). The chain has to be projected first, as below.

What was computed [COMPUTED]
With exact rational arithmetic, the Cartan matrices of types $A_1,\dots,A_8$, $D_4,\dots,D_8$ and $E_6,E_7,E_8$ are positive definite, each with an explicit certificate $C=LDL^{T}$ ($L$ unit lower triangular over $\mathbb Q$, every entry of $D$ positive), with determinants $n+1,\ 4,\ 3,\ 2,\ 1$ respectively. The affine triangle $\tilde A_2$ is singular, so it is only semidefinite: this is the definite-versus-affine dichotomy. The eigenvalues of the $A_n$ Cartan matrix are $4\sin^2\!\bigl(k\pi/(2(n+1))\bigr)$, $k=1,\dots,n$, and a Kronecker product of two positive definite matrices is positive definite with eigenvalues the pairwise products. Script: verify_cartan.py, 40 exact checks including two negative controls.

Projecting the chain. On a surface $T$ with ample $H$, put $u_i=H\cdot E_i>0$, $d_T=H^2>0$ and $e_i=E_i-\tfrac{u_i}{d_T}H$. These are rational algebraic classes with $H\cdot e_i=0$, and since $E_i\cdot E_j=-C_{ij}$, expansion gives $-(e_i\cdot e_j)=K_T:=C_m+uu^{T}/d_T$. The primitive Gram matrix is a positive rank-one correction of the Cartan matrix, not the Cartan matrix itself. Do the same on $S$ with $f_j$, $v$, $d_S$ and $C_n$, giving $K_S$.

Proposition (projected chains on $T\times S$) [COMPUTED]
With the sum polarization, every $z=\sum z_{ij}\,e_i\boxtimes f_j$ is primitive in codimension two, with intersection matrix $K_T\otimes K_S$. Let $D_m x=(x_1,x_2-x_1,\dots,x_m-x_{m-1},-x_m)$, so $C_m=D_m^{T}D_m$ and $D_m$ is injective. With $Z=(z_{ij})$, $$\deg(z^2)=\|D_mZD_n^{T}\|_F^2+\frac{\|u^{T}ZD_n^{T}\|^2}{d_T}+\frac{\|D_mZv\|^2}{d_S}+\frac{(u^{T}Zv)^2}{d_Td_S}.$$ Every term is nonnegative and the first is positive for $Z\ne0$, so the form is positive definite on this subspace, over any algebraically closed field, with no trigonometric eigenvalues needed. Script: verify_projected_chain.py, 26 exact checks (identity on random rational data for five pairs $(A_m,A_n)$, Kronecker form, LDL certificates, and a control where $H^2\le0$ destroys definiteness).

Scope. This is a certificate for the subspace spanned by the projected chains, nothing wider. Those classes are products of divisors, so they already lie in the product-generated part treated earlier; what is new is only that the certificate is explicit. The earlier sentence about eigenvalues of $C_m\otimes C_n$ describes the unprojected matrices and is a remark about Cartan matrices, not a statement about primitive classes. Two further limits: “definite if and only if Dynkin” holds only for matrices that are Cartan-type (generalized Cartan matrices that are not definite or affine are indefinite), and a proof for all primitive classes need not list them, since a structural decomposition or an injective map to a positive form would do. What is missing is such a theorem for the classes outside the product-generated part.

The quiver note. The companion note on affine orthogonal quiver varieties and their contact links (collab/pinot-orthogonal-quivers, version 0.2, for evaluation by V. Pinot) compares moduli surfaces with the Kleinian surfaces of types $A$ and $D$. The same Cartan matrices are the intersection matrices of the exceptional curves in the resolutions of those surfaces [CITED] (McKay, Du Val, Kronheimer; standard), and the Milnor number of each normal form equals the number of nodes: $xy+z^{2n}$ has $\mu=2n-1$, the rank of $A_{2n-1}$, and $z^2+xy^2+x^{m-1}$ has $\mu=m$, the rank of $D_m$ [COMPUTED]. That is the whole connection established here: the same matrices occur in both places.

Not established [OPEN]
Nothing on this page links the quiver note to the standard conjectures. Whether the Cartan-matrix reading gives a definiteness criterion for the hard classes, beyond restating the Hodge index theorem on surfaces, is a question, listed with the others on the open questions page.

8A note on the word

This section makes no mathematical claim. "Primitive" in the sense used here is a method as well as a definition: fix what you already have, here a polarization, and ask what is left that it does not generate. In every case above the answer was small, a line in each codimension, and the work was in showing that it was the whole answer and not a sample. Where the answer is not small, and cannot yet be shown to be the whole, the page says so and stops.
