Primitive

The part of a variety's algebraic classes that its polarization does not already generate — and the one question about it still open.

Pablo Nogueira Grossi · G6 LLC · 11 November 2026

What this page is
Three worked cases of the Hodge-type inequality, each computed exactly, and the lemma that stops them short of a general statement. It is not a proof of the standard conjectures and does not approach one. Each claim carries a tag: [COMPUTED] by a script that runs, [CITED] from a source, [ASSUME] taken as an input, [OPEN] not known.

1What primitive means

Take a smooth projective variety $X$ of dimension $d$ and an ample class $h$. Let $A^r$ be its algebraic classes of codimension $r$, taken rationally and modulo homological equivalence, and let $L$ be multiplication by $h$. For $0\le r\le d/2$ the primitive classes are

Definition
$$P^r=\ker\bigl(L^{\,d-2r+1}:A^r\to A^{d-r+1}\bigr).$$ These are the classes in codimension $r$ that are not accounted for by multiplying lower-degree classes by $h$. When hard Lefschetz holds, $A^r=P^r\oplus L\,A^{r-1}$.

The Hodge-type standard conjecture says the signed pairing on this part is positive:

Hodge-type statement [CITED]
$$(-1)^r\deg\!\bigl(z^2\,h^{\,d-2r}\bigr)>0\qquad\text{for every nonzero }z\in P^r.$$ Formulation as in Milne (2002) and Grothendieck (1969). Known in characteristic $0$ by Hodge theory; over finite fields it is the open question.

Two things about the word. First, primitive is relative to $h$: change the polarization and the primitive space moves. On $\mathbf P^2\times\mathbf P^2$ with $h=sa+tb$ the primitive divisor class is $sa-tb$, which depends on $(s,t)$ [COMPUTED]. Second, the sign $(-1)^r$ is not decoration: that class has $\deg(h^2p_1^2)=-2s^2t^2<0$, so without the sign the inequality would fail [COMPUTED].

For the first two, "all primitive algebraic classes" is a true statement, not a statement about chosen generators, because both varieties are cellular: every cohomology class is algebraic, the Chow ring is $\mathbb Q[a,b]/(a^{m+1},b^{m+1})$, and the primitive spaces are computed as kernels. These two facts are inputs [ASSUME] and are not proved by the scripts. For $\mathbf P^3\times\mathbf P^3$ the dependence on $(s,t)$ is removed by the ring automorphism $a\mapsto sa$, $b\mapsto tb$, which reduces every ample rational $h$ to $s=t=1$ and multiplies the degree by $s^3t^3$ [COMPUTED].

3The full form is indefinite

On $\mathbf P^2\times\mathbf P^2$ the intersection form on the middle degree, in the basis $(a^2,ab,b^2)$, has matrix with rows $(0,0,1),(0,1,0),(1,0,0)$, signature $(2,1)$ [COMPUTED]. So positivity is a property of the primitive part only; asking it of all of $A^{d/2}$ is asking for something false.

4A shortcut that fails

One might hope to deduce positivity from two facts about Frobenius: its eigenvalues on the relevant cohomology have the right absolute value, and it is compatible with the intersection pairing. That does not follow. Take $F=q^2I_2$ and $J=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$. Every eigenvalue of $F$ has absolute value $q^2$, $F^{T}JF=q^4J$, and yet $J$ has a negative direction [COMPUTED]. This is a statement about $2\times 2$ matrices. It does not arise from a variety and is not a counterexample to any standard conjecture; it rules out one route only. The matrix identity is proved in FrobeniusCountermodel.F_similitude, which compiled with the standard axioms [COMPUTED].

5The open lemma

For $T\times S$ the computation above covers only the product-generated classes: the points of each factor and $NS(T)\otimes NS(S)$. What lies outside is the difficulty. There are algebraic classes in $H^2(T)\otimes H^2(S)$ not in $NS(T)\otimes NS(S)$ (correspondences between the surfaces' transcendental parts), and classes in $H^1\otimes H^3$ and $H^3\otimes H^1$ (homomorphisms between Albanese-type abelian varieties).

Open [OPEN]
Over a finite field, show that $(-1)^2\deg(z^2)$ is positive on the primitive algebraic classes of $T\times S$ outside the product-generated subspace. In characteristic $0$ this follows from Hodge theory. For the $H^1\otimes H^3$ parts, a recent report of Milne on abelian varieties may bear on it; how far it reaches was not checked, and its proofs were not audited. For the exotic $H^2\otimes H^2$ classes no reduction is known to me.

Separately, the Lefschetz-type conjecture asks that the inverse of $L^k$ be induced by an algebraic correspondence. That is a different obligation from positivity. For the cellular varieties above it holds because every class on $X\times X$ is algebraic [ASSUME]; nothing here bears on it for general $X$.

7A note on the word

This section makes no mathematical claim. "Primitive" in the sense used here is a method as well as a definition: fix what you already have, here a polarization, and ask what is left that it does not generate. In every case above the answer was small, a line in each codimension, and the work was in showing that it was the whole answer and not a sample. Where the answer is not small, and cannot yet be shown to be the whole, the page says so and stops.
