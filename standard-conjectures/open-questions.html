Open questions

Six questions, stated as precisely as we can state them, with what is known and what has been computed. If we understand the questions better, we may be closer to the answers.

Pablo Nogueira Grossi · G6 LLC · 11 November 2026

What this page is
An invitation, not a result. Nothing here proves, or approaches a proof of, the Hodge-type standard conjecture over finite fields. Each question carries a tag: [COMPUTED] checked by a script in this folder; [CITED] from the literature, not re-proved here; [OPEN] unresolved. Corrections are welcome, especially where a question is already answered in the literature and we have missed it. Background: the chapter on primitive classes.

0Setting

Let $X$ be smooth projective of dimension $d$ over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, $h$ an ample class, and $P^r=\ker(L^{d-2r+1}\colon A^r\to A^{d-r+1})$ the primitive algebraic classes of codimension $r$ modulo numerical equivalence. The Hodge-type standard conjecture asserts $(-1)^r\deg(z^2h^{d-2r})>0$ for nonzero $z\in P^r$ [CITED] (Grothendieck). In characteristic $0$ it follows from Hodge–Riemann; in characteristic $p$ it is open in general. The Lefschetz-type conjecture B is a separate obligation and is not addressed here.

1Questions

Q1 · The exotic classes [OPEN]
For a product $T\times S$ of smooth projective surfaces over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, positivity on the part of $P^2$ generated by products of divisors is a certificate of definiteness over $\mathbb Q$ [COMPUTED] (product_of_surfaces.md). Is the form positive on algebraic classes in $H^2(T)\otimes H^2(S)$ (and the $H^1\otimes H^3$ parts) that are not sums of products of divisors? Over a finite field these can exist, for instance from homomorphisms between Albanese-type pieces. Which known results (Milne's treatment of abelian varieties, for example [CITED]) reach them?
Q2 · Frobenius alone [OPEN]
The matrix statement is false: $F=q^2I$, $J=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$ gives $F^{T}JF=q^4J$ with $J$ indefinite, so similitude under Frobenius does not force definiteness [COMPUTED] (checked, and formalized in P2xP2.lean, namespace FrobeniusCountermodel). This is a statement about matrices, not a variety. What extra input about the Frobenius action on algebraic classes (a rationality or positivity-of-trace property, for instance) would rescue the deduction, and is any such input available without the conjecture itself?
Q3 · What data separates the hypotheses [OPEN]
All three cellular and product cases computed here are consistent with the conjecture, and none can distinguish it from a weaker true statement. Is there a small family (a surface product, an abelian variety, a threefold) where the conjecture is not already known, and where the sign pattern is checkable by exact arithmetic?
Q4 · Intersection graphs [OPEN]
Negated intersection matrices of Dynkin configurations of $(-2)$-curves are Cartan matrices, and Kronecker products of definite matrices are definite with product eigenvalues [COMPUTED] (verify_cartan.py, 40 exact checks). Is there a graph-theoretic criterion, in the spirit of the McKay correspondence [CITED], that forces definiteness on a class of primitive subspaces beyond what the Hodge index theorem on surfaces already gives? A negative answer ("it is only the Hodge index theorem restated") would also be useful.
Q5 · The quiver note [OPEN]
The companion note on affine orthogonal quiver varieties (collab/pinot-orthogonal-quivers) concerns Kleinian surfaces of types $A$ and $D$, whose exceptional-curve configurations have the Cartan matrices above. The shared objects are the matrices, nothing more has been shown. Is there any statement relating positivity of such intersection forms in a quiver-variety family to positivity on primitive classes of a variety that contains them? This question is posed here for discussion; it has not been put to the note's evaluator, and that evaluator is not speaking for it.
Q6 · Where does Hodge–Riemann actually enter? [OPEN]
In characteristic $0$ the sign $(-1)^r$ comes from the Hodge decomposition and the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations. To our knowledge, the positive-characteristic substitutes for that decomposition (crystalline, $\ell$-adic with Frobenius weights) yield weights, not signs; please correct this if it is wrong. Is the gap between "weights" and "signs" localised to a specific lemma that can be stated without the conjecture? We would value a precise statement of that lemma from anyone who has one.

2How to respond

Answers, counterexamples, references to existing results, and corrections of anything above are all welcome. The simplest route is an issue on this repository's Issues tab, one issue per question, titled with the question number. A references-only reply ("Q1 is answered by Theorem X in Y") is as useful as a proof. Replies will be cited with attribution to their authors, and a corrected or retracted question will be marked as such here rather than removed.
