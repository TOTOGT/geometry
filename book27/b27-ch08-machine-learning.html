Unit 1 · Introduction to Machine Learning

Machine learning builds a model from data instead of hand-written rules. The main settings are supervised (labelled examples; regression predicts a number, classification a category), unsupervised (no labels; find structure such as clusters) and reinforcement (learn actions from rewards). A typical workflow is: define the problem and metric, collect and clean data, split into training, validation and test sets, fit, evaluate, and monitor after deployment. The test set must stay untouched until the end, or the estimate of future performance is optimistic.

The central tension is overfitting (a model memorizes noise and fails on new data) versus underfitting (too simple to capture the pattern). Model capacity, data size and regularization control it.

Self-check Why is accuracy measured on the training set a misleading estimate of how the model will perform on new data?

Unit 2 · Linear Regression

Simple linear regression fits y ≈ w x + b by minimizing the sum of squared errors. The closed form is w = Sxy/Sxx and b = mean(y) - w·mean(x), where S are centered sums. The coefficient of determination R² = 1 - SSE/SST measures the share of variance explained. Multiple regression extends it with a design matrix and the normal equations; gradient descent reaches the same minimum iteratively by stepping against the gradient with a learning rate.

Worked example computed. Data x = 1..5, y = 2, 4, 5, 4, 5. Then w = 3/5 = 0.6, b = 11/5 = 2.2, and R² = 0.6. One gradient-descent step from w = 0, b = 0 with learning rate 0.01 on mean squared error moves to w = 0.264, b = 0.08. Step size matters: too large overshoots, too small crawls.

Self-check Predict y at x = 6 with the fitted line, and say why extrapolating that far is riskier than predicting at x = 3.

Unit 3 · Supervised Learning

Classification assigns labels. Common models are logistic regression, k-nearest neighbours, decision trees and support-vector machines. Fitting minimizes a loss; evaluation uses held-out data, and cross-validation repeats the split to reduce luck. Accuracy alone misleads on imbalanced classes, so use the confusion matrix.

Worked example computed. A classifier on 100 cases gives TP = 40, FP = 10, FN = 20, TN = 30. Accuracy = 70/100 = 0.7; precision = TP/(TP+FP) = 40/50 = 0.8; recall = TP/(TP+FN) = 40/60 = 0.6667; F1 = 2PR/(P+R) = 8/11 = 0.7273. Precision asks how many flagged cases were right; recall asks how many real cases were found. Which matters more depends on the cost of each error.

Self-check For a cancer screen and for a spam filter, say whether you would favor recall or precision and why.

Unit 4 · Unsupervised Learning

Without labels, algorithms look for structure. k-means alternates two steps: assign each point to its nearest centre, then move each centre to the mean of its points; it converges to a local optimum that depends on the start. Hierarchical clustering builds a tree of merges, and principal component analysis finds directions of greatest variance for dimensionality reduction. Evaluating clusters is harder without labels; silhouette scores and domain judgement help.

Worked example computed. Points 1, 2, 9, 10, 20 on a line with k = 2 starting at centres 1 and 2 end with centres 1.5 and 13: clusters [1, 2] and [9, 10, 20]. The outlier 20 drags the second centre away from 9 and 10, which shows k-means' sensitivity to outliers.

Self-check Run one assignment and update step by hand from centres 1 and 2 and confirm the first new centres.

Unit 5 · Artificial Neural Networks

A neuron computes a weighted sum of inputs plus a bias and passes it through a nonlinearity (sigmoid, tanh, ReLU). Layers of neurons form a network; without nonlinearity, any depth collapses to one linear map. Training uses backpropagation, the chain rule applied layer by layer to get the gradient of the loss, then a gradient-descent update. Practical issues are initialization, learning rate, vanishing gradients and overfitting, addressed by regularization, dropout and early stopping.

Self-check Why would a network of ten layers with only linear activations be no more expressive than a single layer?

Unit 6 · Ensemble Learning

An ensemble combines models to beat any single one. Bagging trains models on bootstrap resamples and averages them, which lowers variance; a random forest adds random feature choice. Boosting trains models in sequence, each focusing on earlier mistakes, which lowers bias; gradient boosting is the modern form. Stacking trains a model on other models' outputs. Ensembles work because errors that are not perfectly correlated partly cancel; they cost more compute and are harder to interpret.

Self-check Name which of bagging or boosting you would try first on a high-variance deep tree, and why.

Unit 7 · Overview of Deep Learning and Gen AI

Deep learning uses many-layer networks to learn features from raw data. Convolutional networks exploit spatial structure in images, recurrent networks and transformers handle sequences, and attention lets each position weigh all others. Generative models learn to produce new data: large language models predict the next token, diffusion models denoise images, and variational autoencoders and GANs learn latent spaces. Transfer learning reuses a model trained on a large task for a smaller one.

These models are powerful and costly: they need data and compute, can produce fluent but wrong output, and can reproduce biases and memorized text from training data.

Self-check In one paragraph, say what transfer learning saves and what risk it carries.

Unit 8 · Responsible and Explainable AI

Responsible AI treats fairness, transparency, privacy, safety and accountability as engineering requirements. Bias enters through unrepresentative data, labels that encode past prejudice, and proxy features. Fairness has several non-equivalent definitions (equal error rates across groups, equal selection rates, calibration), and not all can hold at once when base rates differ, so a team must choose and justify one. Explainability methods include feature importance, partial-dependence plots and local explanations such as LIME and SHAP; simpler models are explainable by design. Governance adds documentation, audits and human oversight.

Self-check A loan model has equal accuracy for two groups but different approval rates. Name two fairness definitions and say whether they agree here.
