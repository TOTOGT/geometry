Benoit Mandelbrot

Zipf's $1/r$ fits poorly at the top of the ranking, and a shifted form $(r+\beta)^{-\alpha}$ fits better. This page proves the one clean fact behind that form: if the cost of a word grows like the logarithm of its rank, the maximum-entropy distribution at a fixed mean cost is exactly the shifted power law. It checks the proof numerically and says what is not shown.

The man, in cited facts

CITED RESULTCITED Benoit Mandelbrot (1924 to 2010) is best known for fractal geometry. Early in his career, in the 1950s, he wrote on the statistics of word frequencies and proposed a generalization of Zipf's law with an extra parameter, argued from an information-theoretic cost model. The generalized form is now called the Zipf–Mandelbrot law. These facts are reported from the literature and were not checked for this page. See ch-zipf for Zipf's law and the random-typing null model.

The cost argument, in a form that can be proved

Give each rank $r = 1, \dots, N$ a cost $c_r = \ln(r + \beta)$ and ask for the distribution $p$ on ranks of greatest entropy $H(p) = -\sum p_r \ln p_r$ among those with a given mean cost $\sum p_r c_r$. The Lagrange condition gives $p_r \propto e^{-\alpha c_r} = (r+\beta)^{-\alpha}$, where $\alpha$ is the multiplier on the cost constraint.

THEOREMPROVED Let $p_r = e^{-\alpha c_r}/Z$. For every distribution $p'$ on the same ranks with the same mean cost, $H(p) - H(p') = \mathrm{KL}(p' \,\|\, p) \ge 0$, with equality only when $p' = p$. Proof: $\mathrm{KL}(p'\|p) = \sum p'_r \ln p'_r - \sum p'_r \ln p_r = -H(p') + \alpha \sum p'_r c_r + \ln Z$. The middle term equals $\alpha E$ where $E$ is the common mean cost, so $\mathrm{KL}(p'\|p) = -H(p') + \alpha E + \ln Z$. The same computation with $p' = p$ gives $H(p) = \alpha E + \ln Z$. Subtract.

CHECKED INSTANCECOMPUTED With $N = 1000$, $\alpha = 1.1$ and $\beta = 2.7$ (illustrative values, chosen arbitrarily), the distribution has entropy 5.5013 nats and mean cost 3.7668 nats. On 200 seeded perturbations that keep total probability and mean cost fixed, the entropy gap equals the divergence to better than $10^{-12}$ and every gap is positive. This is a numerical check of an identity that the proof above already establishes.

Why a logarithmic cost

In the random-typing model of ch-zipf, with $k$ letters, words are ranked by length first. The word of rank $r$ has length $L$ with $\log_k\!\bigl(1 + r(k-1)/k\bigr) \le L < \log_k\!\bigl(1 + r(k-1)/k\bigr) + 1$, so its cost in characters is $\log_k r$ up to one character. A cost that is logarithmic in rank is what a code tree over a finite alphabet produces.

CHECKED INSTANCECOMPUTED For $k = 26$ the length inequality holds for every rank up to 300,000. The shift $\beta$ is the part of Mandelbrot's form that the proof above does not derive: it is simply part of the assumed cost $\ln(r+\beta)$.

What is not shown

OPEN QUESTIONOPEN Whether real speakers minimize cost per unit of information, whether real word costs are logarithmic in rank, and what fixes $\beta$ and $\alpha$ for a given language are not established on this page. The theorem says only that if the model's assumptions hold, the shifted power law is what they produce. It does not say that language satisfies them, and the random-typing result on the Zipf page shows that a power law can appear with no cost-minimizing speaker at all.

Where it connects

The maximum-entropy step is the same one that turns up in statistical mechanics (see ch-boltzmann), where a fixed mean energy gives the Boltzmann weights $e^{-\alpha E}$. That is a shared mathematical form, not a shared mechanism. The coding side is in ch-shannon.
