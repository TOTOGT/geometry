Amal Kumar Raychaudhuri

1923–2005 · Calcutta. One equation about how a bundle of paths spreads or squeezes, which the singularity theorems use at their first step.

θ(0) = −c ⇒ focus within 2/c · two exact solutions and 2000 random cases

1 · One line

Take a family of light rays leaving a surface, or a family of free-falling particles, and ask one thing: is the bundle spreading or squeezing? The answer is a single number at each point, the expansion $\theta$, the rate at which a small cross-section of the bundle grows. For light rays, with an affine parameter $\lambda$ along them, it obeys

$$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} \;=\; -\tfrac12\theta^2 \;-\; \sigma^2 \;+\; \omega^2 \;-\; R_{ab}k^ak^b .$$

Shear $\sigma^2$ squeezes, twist $\omega^2$ spreads, and the last term is the matter: for ordinary matter $R_{ab}k^ak^b \ge 0$, so it squeezes too. Hawking and Ellis attribute the timelike version of this equation to Landau and independently to Raychaudhuri, and say it plays a central role in the proofs of the singularity theorems. That is the whole reason this man has a chapter in a gallery of people who put a brick in the wall.

2 · What the inequality buys

Drop the twist, as for a bundle that comes from a surface. Then every term on the right is non-positive (shear squeezes, and the matter term squeezes when $R_{ab}k^ak^b \ge 0$), so

$$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} \;\le\; -\tfrac12\theta^2 .$$

Suppose the bundle is already converging, $\theta(0) = -c < 0$. Comparing with the equation $\theta' = -\theta^2/2$, whose solution is $\theta = -c/(1 - c\lambda/2)$, the expansion reaches $-\infty$ no later than $\lambda = 2/c$. A point where it does is a conjugate point: neighbouring rays cross.

Computed — the bound, and where it is exact
Blow-up by $\lambda = 2/c$, attained when shear and matter vanish

The script integrates 2000 random cases with random non-negative shear and matter terms and checks that the blow-up is never later than $2/c$. It also checks the exact solution: ingoing light rays from a sphere of radius $r_0$ in flat space have $\theta = -2/(r_0-\lambda)$, which solves the equation with no shear and no matter and focuses at the centre, at exactly $\lambda = r_0 = 2/c$.

∎

Nothing here needs a metric beyond the equation itself. That is why the same three lines serve for a lens, a star and a universe.

3 · Schwarzschild: where the sign flips

In ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates the radial light rays that move outward have an affine parameter with $\dot r = E$ constant, so a small sphere of them has $\theta = 2E/r$. The script checks, symbolically, that this solves the equation with $R_{ab}k^ak^b = 0$ (vacuum) and no shear or twist. With a different normalisation of the same outgoing family the expansion is $(1-2M/r)/r$. It is positive outside $r = 2M$ and negative inside. A sphere where both the ingoing and the outgoing expansions are negative is what the books call a closed trapped surface. The sign change is computed here; the existence of such surfaces in a real collapse is not.

4 · From a focus to a singularity theorem

Focusing alone is not a singularity theorem. Penrose's theorem (Hawking and Ellis, §8.2, Theorem 1) needs three things together: non-negative $R_{ab}k^ak^b$ for null $k$, a non-compact Cauchy surface, and a closed trapped surface. The focusing bound then says that if the space-time were null geodesically complete, every ray leaving the trapped surface would meet a conjugate point within a bounded distance, which the compactness argument turns into a contradiction. The conclusion is that some null geodesic cannot be continued. It says nothing about where, or how many, or whether a unique point is involved.

The Hawking–Penrose theorem (§8.2, Theorem 2) replaces the Cauchy surface by two other conditions, the generic condition and the chronology condition, and allows three alternative starting hypotheses. The Hawking chapter reads these theorems as a Banach fixed point. This chapter does not: the proof runs through focusing and compactness, and no contraction map appears in it. The repository's audit record for that chapter (docs/audit-book7-ch-hawking-2026-11-20.md) sets out the difference.

5 · Caustics, and the fold the series keeps naming

A conjugate point is where a bundle of rays has a caustic. In the plane the picture is familiar: the paths normal to an ellipse form a family whose map from (position on the ellipse, distance travelled) is singular exactly on the ellipse's evolute, the curve of its centres of curvature. The script confirms this on an ellipse with semi-axes 3 and 2: the singular image coincides with the evolute, the evolute has four cusps, and elsewhere it is a regular curve of fold points. That is the standard local picture of a generic wavefront caustic: folds, with cusps at isolated points.

Not claimed The dm³ model has a fold at a radius r* in an ordinary differential equation on a contact 3-manifold. That is a fold of a different map in a different system. This chapter does not say the two are the same object, and no map between them is written down anywhere in the corpus. The repository's audit log for 15 November 2026 already records a correction to wording about an "A1 fold" in this context. Until a map exists, the honest statement is that both pictures contain the word fold.

6 · The chain, as a reading

One may read the bundle as C (a family of rays compressed into a surface), the sign of $\theta$ as K (a threshold), the conjugate point as F (the fold where rays cross), and the incomplete geodesic as U (the continuation that does not exist). This is the author's reading, offered because the gallery asks which operator each person was. It is not derived from the equation.

7 · What is open

Six things are not done. The timelike equation, with $-\theta^2/3$ and a focusing distance of $3/|\theta|$, is not checked by the script. Shear and twist are never computed for a physical congruence; they are only bounded by sign. Penrose's theorem is described, not verified. No map from the dm³ fold to a conjugate point is attempted. The biographical dates and the 1955 Physical Review reference for Raychaudhuri's paper are from memory and have not been fetched from a source. The claim that the source text bounds the conjugate distance by $2/c$ is checked by the script only when the Hawking–Ellis PDF is in the Downloads folder, and otherwise reports SKIP.
