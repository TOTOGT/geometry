Nikolai Kardashev

He made civilizations rankable by the one thing they all must obey. What the corpus holds of him is three descriptions of the scale, two recomputed sentences about waste heat, and a Dyson chapter that never mentions any of it.

Part I · What the Books Say He Did

A scale by one flux, and who added what

The Kardashev paper is not held. What is held is three books by Michio Kaku that describe it, and the script confirms each sentence below is on the page it names. Parallel Worlds says the ranking was introduced in the 1960s to classify radio signals from possible civilizations, that each type emits a characteristic radiation that can be measured, and that by the second law none can hide its waste heat. Physics of the Future gives 1964, and gives the reasoning: civilizations may differ in culture and government, but the one thing all must obey is physics, so energy consumption is the observable that sorts them, and the scale was built to guide astronomers. Physics of the Impossible calls it a conjecture that the stages could be ranked by energy consumption.

What the books do not credit to him is worth keeping apart. The information scale is Sagan's, by Physics of the Future's account. The entropy scale is Kaku's own addition, by the same account, and Parallel Worlds says the original classification came before miniaturization, nanotechnology and awareness of environmental degradation. And Physics of the Future says Freeman Dyson tried to find Type II civilizations as infrared sources, and that none were found.

Held vs. claimed This is a description of a scale from its popularizer. What Kardashev himself wrote, who he was, and what Dyson's search actually did are all WANTED; the chapter gives no dates of his life and no biography.
Part II · A Rung Is a Rate

The scale fixes the distance; the rate fixes the time

Both printed ladders step by exactly 10 in $\log_{10}$ of watts, one rung being a factor of ten billion (Kaku's chapter holds the two ladders side by side and finds that they differ by a decade). A rung by itself says nothing about when. Ten billion at 1% a year is 2,314 years; at 2%, 1,163; at 3%, 779. The scale is a fixed distance on the log axis and everything about time enters through a growth rate that is an assumption about the civilization, not a property of the scale. That is the reason the ladder is worth putting beside a corpus that claims one chain of operators acts at every scale: the ladder is honest about needing a rate, and the corpus's own ladder does not yet state one.

Part III · The Two Waste-Heat Sentences

Why he is connected to Dyson

Two sentences in the held books are recomputable. First, that a Type II civilization hiding behind a shell would still glow in the infrared. A shell that absorbs the Sun's whole output at 1 AU and radiates it from its outer surface sits at $T=(L/4\pi r^2\sigma)^{1/4}=394$ K, with a blackbody peak at 7.4 micrometres: infrared, as the book says. Second, that with waste heat left unchecked the planet's temperature “will rise until it melts.” For an Earth-sized planet running a whole star's output, with half of it shed as heat from the surface and no atmosphere, the same formula gives about 50,700 K. That is far above any melting point, so the sentence is right; the assumptions are stated on the page of the script, including that “half” is the lower of two readings. The reference values ($\sigma$, the solar luminosity, the astronomical unit, the Earth's radius) are CITED, not held.

Book 7 already has a chapter on Freeman Dyson. The script counts what it says of any of this: nothing. It mentions no spheres, no infrared search, no civilizations, no Kardashev and no SETI. So the connection between the two chapters that the books draw in one paragraph is unmade in the corpus, and is offered here as a gap rather than filled: the Dyson chapter is not edited.
