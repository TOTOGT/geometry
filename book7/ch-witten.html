Edward Witten

In 1982 he reread Morse theory as the ground states of a supersymmetric quantum mechanics, so that counting critical points of a function and counting holes in a space became one computation. This page does that computation on a sphere and a torus, and connects it to the Euler characteristic of ch-euler.

The man, in four cited facts

CITED RESULTCITED Edward Witten (born 1951) is a theoretical physicist at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. He received the Fields Medal in 1990, as a physicist, for work that changed mathematics. Two of the papers behind that are E. Witten, “Supersymmetry and Morse theory”, Journal of Differential Geometry 17 (1982), and E. Witten, “Quantum field theory and the Jones polynomial”, Communications in Mathematical Physics 121 (1989). The journals and titles were confirmed from library listings; the page numbers, the exact birthday and the Fields citation text were not checked for this page.

The 1982 idea

Take a closed manifold $M$ and a smooth function $f$ on it whose critical points are all non-degenerate (a Morse function). Each critical point has an index, the number of downhill directions. Let $c_k$ be the number of critical points of index $k$, and let $b_k$ be the $k$-th Betti number of $M$, the number of independent $k$-dimensional holes.

THEOREMPROVED (Morse inequalities.) $c_k \ge b_k$ for every $k$, and $\sum_k(-1)^k c_k = \sum_k(-1)^k b_k = \chi(M)$. This is classical Morse theory; it is stated here, not proved here.

CITED RESULTCITED Witten's proof of the inequalities: replace the exterior derivative $d$ by $d_t = e^{-tf}\,d\,e^{tf}$ and study the Laplacian of $d_t$. As $t\to\infty$ its low-lying eigenstates concentrate at critical points of $f$, one family for each index. The number of low eigenstates in degree $k$ is then $c_k$ up to corrections, and the true zero-energy states are counted by $b_k$. The alternating count $\sum(-1)^k\,\#\{\text{states of degree }k\}$ does not change when states pair up and leave zero energy, which is why $\chi(M)$ comes out in the end. Described here from the cited paper; the analysis is not reproduced.

The reading in physics language: $\sum(-1)^k b_k$ is a Witten index, the count of bosonic minus fermionic zero-energy states. It cannot change under a smooth deformation of the problem, which is the same fact as saying $\chi(M)$ does not depend on which Morse function you chose.

CHECKED INSTANCECOMPUTED On the torus, $f=\cos x+\cos y$ has critical points $(0,0)$, $(0,\pi)$, $(\pi,0)$, $(\pi,\pi)$ with Hessians $\mathrm{diag}(-\cos x,-\cos y)$, giving indices 2, 1, 1, 0. On the sphere, height has a maximum (index 2) and a minimum (index 0). In both cases $c_k = b_k$ exactly, so these are perfect Morse functions, and the alternating sums are 2 and 0. The Betti numbers come from a $3\times3$ grid torus (9 vertices, 18 edges, 9 faces) and a cube surface (8, 12, 6). Printed by book7/ch-witten-verify.py.

The torus row is the zero that ch-euler kept meeting: $F-E+V=0$ there came from counting cells; here the same $0$ comes from $1-2+1$ counting critical points. Two counts of different objects, one number. That agreement is what the theorem predicts, and the check on this page is that these two instances do agree, nothing more.

Where this touches the rest of the corpus

The critical points counted above are the rest points of the gradient flow of $f$, and the index $k$ is the dimension of the unstable manifold. ch-conley assigns Conley indices to isolated invariant sets of flows, and the Morse index of a critical point is the simplest case. That is the whole link claimed here: a shared vocabulary of critical points and their indices, not a shared mechanism between this page's supersymmetric argument and anything else in the corpus.

OPEN QUESTIONOPEN Whether the supersymmetric argument carries over to the flows in ch-conley (for example, to give a Witten-style count for their Conley indices) is not examined on this page. It is not claimed.
