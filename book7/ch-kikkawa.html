Keiji Kikkawa

The corpus's nano-scale row leans on string field theory and on a self-dual point at R = 1. Four passages in Kaku's book and three papers from a workshop in his honour are what can be held about the man behind both — one credit corroborated, one still resting on a collaborator's word.

Part I · What Is Held

One book, and three papers that cite him

Two layers, kept apart. The first is Michio Kaku's account in Parallel Worlds, which is a collaborator's. The book's own index lists Keiji Kikkawa on four stretches of pages, and the script confirms the sentences below are on them. Page numbers are the book's; the PDF page is the book page plus 19.

Book pageWhat Kaku says he did

The second layer is three papers the author supplied, all presented at a workshop in Osaka, 14–16 December 1995, in honour of Kikkawa's 60th birthday. They are independent of Kaku and of one another in what they cite:

Held vs. claimed The 1984 credit and the 1969 paper are now corroborated; the 1974 field theory is not. Schwarz, Ooguri and Kubota cite the 1984 duality paper, the 1969 paper and the 1970 paper, and place him at Osaka University. None of them cites the 1974 string-field-theory work Kaku describes, so that claim and the count of five interactions still rest on Kaku alone (WANTED). None of the cited papers themselves is held; what is held is that other physicists cite them, with journals and years. A fourth text, a 2025 journal paper, repeats the 1984 credit; it is tertiary and not relied on. This chapter gives no dates of birth and no career beyond what these pages say.

A namesake, so nobody merges the two: book6/ch07-microtubule-fibonacci cites an M. Kikkawa, a J. Cell Biol. paper on the microtubule seam. Same surname, different field; the script checks the citation is there and this page does not connect them.

Part II · The Row That Counts Vertices

Three, five and one

The F row of the nano-scale column in chGravity-scales.html says: string interaction vertex: A₁ = emission, A₂ = splitting, A₃ = four-string junction; ADE singularity hierarchy in scattering amplitudes. That names three vertices. The book above says type I strings have five interactions and closed strings one. Three, five and one are three different lists.

They do not have to conflict. The corpus's A₁–A₃ are singularity types in a fold hierarchy; the book's five are terms in a field-theory action, and one is not obliged to be the other. But nothing on the corpus page says which it means, and the mapping is exactly the kind a reader will assume. Under R9 a count that collides is recorded and left to the author, so this chapter does not choose:

OPEN · author's call Is the A₁→A₃ hierarchy meant as the string interaction terms? If yes, the row lists three of five (or three where closed strings need one) and should say why. If no, the row's label “string interaction vertex” is the word a reader will take for the book's. The script confirms both counts and the row's exact wording, and computes nothing else; it does not guess a mapping.
Part III · A Reflection, Not a Rung

What the duality does to a ladder of scales

The same row's column header says the theory has a self-dual point at R = 1. Kaku's description is one line: wrap a dimension into a circle of radius R, or one of radius 1/R, and the two theories are the same. Schwarz is more careful about what it relates: type IIB on a circle with type IIA on a circle of reciprocal radius, and the two heterotic strings likewise. For those pairs the dual of a theory is a different theory, so the nano row's “self-dual point” needs to say which string it means; the row does not. The script confirms, for the standard closed-string zero-mode formula (CITED; Schwarz states only the reciprocal-radius statement), that the spectrum at R equals the spectrum at 1/R with the two integers swapped, for 49 ratios, and that R = 1/R has one positive solution.

On the log axis that is a reflection: $\log R \mapsto -\log R$, an involution with exactly one fixed point. A scale ladder such as Kardashev's is the opposite kind of map: one rung is $+10$ in $\log_{10}$ of power (the paper's own are 14 and 11), it has no fixed point and it only ever moves one way. The scales column carries a ladder and a self-dual point on the same nano column; whether they are one structure is a model question and is not claimed here. The unit is also open: the script's R = 1 is the string length, and Parallel Worlds states the circle sizes against the Planck length (OPEN).

Part IV · Where This Connects

Kaku is the chapter that found the gap and holds the level-N counts the same row needs; Kardashev is the ladder side of Part III; the row itself is chGravity-scales. The three chapters share one measurement — before them, no page of the corpus named Keiji Kikkawa or the Kardashev scale — and one open question about what the nano-scale column's rungs are.
