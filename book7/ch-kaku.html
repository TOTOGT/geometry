PHYSICS · THEORETICALPROFILE

Michio Kaku

He wrote the theory of strings as a field theory. The corpus's nano-scale row stands on that field theory and never names him — and the civilization ladder he made popular is a scale whose rungs, once given numbers, do not agree across his own books.

Part I · The Row That Never Named Him

What the nano-scale column stands on

chGravity-scales.html says the operator chain is “not metaphorical across scales”, and its table gives the compression operator C a technical identity at each one. At the nano scale the identity is level truncation in Witten's cubic string field theory — a projection to a finite-dimensional subspace of the string Hilbert space — and the scale's description says the theory has a T-duality self-dual point at R = 1. Those are two claims about string theory, and both rest on work that other people did first.

The script beside this page read all 873 chapters of the corpus as they stood at commit aaba379, before this chapter existed. No page names Michio Kaku. No page names Keiji Kikkawa. No page mentions the Kardashev scale. The three phrases — string field theory, T-duality, level truncation — appear on exactly one page, and it is this one. (One other chapter, book6/ch07-microtubule-fibonacci, cites a Kikkawa — M. Kikkawa, on the microtubule seam. A namesake, a different field. The script scopes its claim to Keiji Kikkawa so nobody merges the two.)

His account, and what it is worth

In Parallel Worlds (PDF pp. 209–210, book pp. 190–191) he says that in 1974, with Keiji Kikkawa of Osaka University, he extracted the field theory of strings, having set out to write the string as a field in the way Faraday's lines of force write electromagnetism. In Physics of the Impossible (p. 6) he says he wrote the theory of strings in terms of Faraday's force fields and so founded string field theory, and the jacket of the same scan calls him its cofounder. Later in the same book (PDF p. 256, book p. 237) he credits the R ↔ 1/R duality to Kikkawa and his student Masami Yamasaki, in 1984.

Held vs. claimed This is his own account, checked only for what the pages say. The script confirms each sentence above is on the page cited. It does not confirm the priority claim, the year, or the credit for the duality: those need the original papers, which are not held (WANTED). The corpus row names Witten's theory; this chapter does not say the row is wrong to, only that the theory it names has a predecessor the corpus never mentions.

What the row needs, computed

C is a projection to a finite-dimensional subspace, so the first thing to know is how big the subspace is. In light-cone gauge, for the open bosonic string in 26 dimensions, there are 24 transverse oscillators and the number of states at level $N$ is the coefficient of $q^N$ in $\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{-24}$. The script computes it exactly:

Level N012345678
States1243243,20025,650176,2561,073,7205,930,49630,178,575

Three facts follow and the script checks each. Level 1 has 24 states, the 24 polarizations of the massless vector. Level 2 has $324 = 24 + 300$, which is also the dimension of a symmetric traceless tensor of $SO(25)$ — arithmetic, not a claim about representations. And the series is the coefficient list of $1/\Delta$: the product $\prod(1-q^n)^{24}$ expands to $1 - 24q + 252q^2 - 1472q^3 + 4830q^4 - 6048q^5 \cdots$, which is Ramanujan's function $\tau(1),\dots,\tau(6)$, so the counts are the reciprocal of the series with those coefficients. (This $\tau(n)$ is Ramanujan's, not the corpus's $\tau = 2$. The neighbouring chapter, Ramanujan, is about partitions and modular objects and does not develop $\tau(n)$; it is the neighbour, not the source.) The growth is exact to better than one part in $10^{9}$ from $N=10$ to $N=1000$:

p24(N) = 2π (N−1)^(−13/2) · I_13( 4π √(N−1) ) ∼ e^(4π√N) N^(−27/4)

So the cost of C at level $N$ is sub-exponential in $N$ and super-polynomial: a real number the row can be held to, and one the chapter it sits in never states.

The duality half of the row is a smaller check. For a closed string on a circle of radius $R$ (with $\alpha'=1$) the zero-mode part of $M^2$ is $(n/R)^2+(wR)^2$; the script confirms the spectrum at $R$ equals the spectrum at $1/R$ with $n$ and $w$ swapped, for 49 ratios, and that $R = 1/R$ has the single positive solution $R=1$. So “self-dual point at $R=1$” is right in units of the string length.

OPEN · WANTED Three things this section does not establish. (1) The count is for light-cone gauge. Witten's cubic theory uses a covariant truncation with ghosts, whose subspace dimensions are not these numbers; that count is not computed here (OPEN). (2) That light-cone gauge is the gauge of the 1974 Kaku–Kikkawa papers is not in the held text, which says “field theory of strings” and no more (WANTED). (3) Parallel Worlds states the R and 1/R circles against the Planck length; the formula above is in string length. What the difference does is unchecked (OPEN), and the mass formula itself is standard but not in a held source (WANTED).
Part II · A Ladder Ten Billion Steps Wide

Three books, two ladders

Read plainly: Physics of the Future is internally consistent — its rungs sit on the physical quantities they name, and its years follow from its rates. Parallel Worlds is not: its Type I is a decade low, and its time to Type III does not follow from the growth rate it states. The two books are consistent with each other on the step and on nothing else. Which is the later, and so which corrects which, is not asserted here: Parallel Worlds' page footers carry the date 27 October 2004, and Physics of the Future refers to the recession of 2008, so it is the later of the two; the third book's date is not checked.

Why a corpus about scales should care

chGravity-scales says the same operator chain acts at every scale, and gives it three: 10−35 m, 10−15 m, and 10−3 to 101 m. A ladder is a claim only when each rung has a number and there is a rate that takes you between them. Kaku's ladder is the case where that was done in public, more than once, and the numbers moved. The corpus's ladder has the rungs and no rate. What the rate would be — how the compression cost computed above changes from rung to rung — is OPEN, and it is the question this chapter leaves the nano-scale row with.

Part III · What This Chapter Does Not Say
Not established Not checked, and left open on purpose. The 1974 papers and the 1984 duality paper, from which the priority and the credit above would come (WANTED). Kardashev's own 1964 paper and Sagan's grading, which the books describe and this chapter does not read (WANTED). Whether the ten-billion step is the right one physically — the script shows it is a definition, 2.2 × 109 between a star and the sunlight on its planet, not a measurement. The dates and editions of the three books; the scans are OCR, with superscripts flattened, and every needle is matched with that in mind. Nothing here is a judgment on his physics, which this chapter did not evaluate: it holds sentences that carry numbers against the numbers.

Physics of the Future (PDF pp. 187–188) adds an information scale and an entropy scale to the energy one. This chapter reads those pages and does not use them. Whether the three axes are independent, and whether the corpus's evidence tags map onto any of them, is not tested (OPEN).
