The Language of Mathematics

This corpus has written about words all along: five words for one hole, a double root that was also called degenerate, a numeral that names a count, a holology that names a whole. It has never had one place to ask how mathematics becomes a language, and how a machine reads that language back. This volume is that place.

Mathematics is written in two languages at once: a natural language that has been bent into precision, and a formal language that a machine can check. Most errors in this corpus happened where the two meet.

Lean lives elsewhere Formal proof language already has three books: XIX · AXLE and the Manual (the language and its manual), XIII · Coherence (what a verification establishes) and XVII · The Machine Floor (each level bound to its text). This volume does not repeat them. It covers the natural-language side of mathematics, links to those books wherever the two sides meet, and makes its own checks in Python.

3 · What this volume will not claim

  • That mathematics is a language in any sense stronger than the one each chapter defines.
  • That a coined term is a discovery. Holology is named here as a vocabulary, and its mathematical content stays where Book VIII puts it.
  • Any linguistic count without the script that printed it.
