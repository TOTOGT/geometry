This series writes one chain of letters, C → K → F → U, across physics, biology, chemistry, economics and theology. Pablo Nogueira Grossi's reading is that the letters are translation keys: each domain and each scale gives them a new meaning. This chapter runs Chapter 3's dictionary test on the letters themselves and measures where that new meaning actually enters.

2 · Translation keys

The domain chooses the instantiation, not the word

If the letters were simply ambiguous, each domain would pick its own gloss word. It does not. "Threshold" appears on 9 mathematics pages, 9 physics pages and 9 biology pages; "curvature" leans only mildly toward physics (9 of its 21 tagged pages). What a translation key is A key that keeps one head sense and takes a domain-specific filler. That is the structure of Chapter 3's entry, where the head sense (C) unfolds under context (U). It is also the structure of Chapter 7's kārakas: Agent is constant, and Maitra fills it in one sentence and the wind in another. On this reading K means "the threshold at which the system commits", and the pH of a membrane or the bound of a loop is what that threshold is in each domain. The reading is Pablo Nogueira Grossi's. The measurements above support it for K; they do not show that every domain adds meaning. MODEL
3 · Where the keys collide
  • U = Union (10 pages) belongs to a second key set. The series hub lists it as "G = Genesis · L = Logos · R = Resonance · U = Union", the chain of the Omega gallery. It is the same letter used as a different key, a homonym, not a domain filling of Unfolding. COMPUTED
  • U = Unification (3 pages) and U = Universal (3 pages) are strays. One page joins Universal to Unfolding; nothing joins Unification to anything.
  • The GCM Institutional Edition (2025) calls its Appendix A a "Unified Lexicon", but the operators it defines are g, L, R, U and B. It does not define C, K or F. Its U is the Unfolding operator, which agrees with the majority here. COMPUTED
Proposal: a key registerOne entry per letter, in Chapter 3's four-part form. The head is the single gloss. The key set says which chain the letter belongs to (the dm³ chain or the Omega chain). The fillers list the domain instantiations, one line per domain, each with the page that uses it. The register would be generated from the pages, like the subject tags, not written by hand. 4 · Holology

A word that passes the test

Holology appears on 20 pages and is defined on 6. All six definitions are about the whole: "the logic of the whole as a coherent whole" (Book VIII ch 13), "the logic by which a totality is coherent as a totality" (Book IV ch 26). By Chapter 3's test it has one sense. The operator letter U does not. COMPUTED

The coinage follows a regular pattern. Book VIII ch 12 sets topos/holos against -logy/-graphy. Three cells of that grid are established English words: topology, topography and holography. The fourth, holology, fills the empty cell, which is the way a lexicographer expects a regular new word to form. The word and its place in the grid are Pablo Nogueira Grossi's. COMPUTED
