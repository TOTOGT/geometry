Tribonacci as Critical Constant

A numerical DNLS result — Tribonacci mid-gap states markedly more robust than Fibonacci under nonlinearity — whose original explanation (“η sits exactly at the fold threshold c*=3”) was checked in Lean and withdrawn, 2026-09-29. The effect stands; the reason given for it did not.

"The Tribonacci constant is not interesting because it is large. It is interesting because it is exactly large enough — and not one bit more — to sit at the fold threshold. Below it, systems are subcritical. At it, they crystallize. Above it, they shatter."

η ≈ 1.839287 is the dominant root of x³ − x² − x − 1 = 0. It is the spectral radius of the Tribonacci companion matrix and the dm³ η weight. This chapter measures — numerically, on the two chains actually simulated — a Tribonacci DNLS chain markedly more robust under nonlinear perturbation than a Fibonacci one; the claim is scoped to those two chains, not to a proven property of η among all n-bonacci constants, and the formal side of the chapter (§η.4) checks a narrower set of facts than earlier drafts claimed.

§ η.1 · The NumberWhere η Lives

The n-bonacci ladder runs from φ = 1.618 (subcritical, Fibonacci) through η = 1.839 to Δ = 1.927, Σ = 1.966, and finally τ = 2 (the limit of all n-bonacci roots, the embodiment threshold of the dm³ framework). Each step is a different answer to the same question: how many prior terms does the recurrence sum?

Fibonacci sums two. Tribonacci sums three. The characteristic polynomial of the tribonacci companion matrix is x³ − x² − x − 1 = 0. Its dominant root is η. The matrix is:

Tribonacci companion matrix T₃ 
T₃ = ⎡1 1 1⎤    det(T₃) = 1    T₃ ∈ SL(3,ℤ)
     ⎢1 0 0⎥    spectral radius ρ(T₃) = η ≈ 1.839287
     ⎣0 1 0⎦

The key structural fact: det(T₃) = 1 (proved directly in EtaChecks.lean’s T3_det). T₃ lives in SL(3,ℤ) — the group of integer volume-preserving transformations. Corrected 2026-09-29: calling this “the same statement as modular invariance in string theory (Sp(2g,ℤ))” is a category error, not an open conjecture — Sp(2g,ℤ) acts on ℤ2g for g ≥ 1 (dimension 2, 4, 6, …), and a 3×3 matrix cannot be a member of any Sp(2g,ℤ) regardless of what it satisfies. The genuinely open question, stated without that false equivalence, is whether SL(3,ℤ)-invariance of T₃ has any analogue of modular invariance’s role, not whether T₃ sits inside Sp(2g,ℤ) — it structurally cannot.

§ η.2 · The DNLS ResultWhat the Simulation Proves

The Discrete Nonlinear Schrödinger equation (DNLS) is the canonical model for how nonlinearity and quasiperiodicity compete. The amplitude evolution on site n is:

DNLS equation 
i·dψₙ/dt = −(ψₙ₊₁ + ψₙ₋₁) + εₙ·ψₙ + λ|ψₙ|²·ψₙ

The on-site potentials {εₙ} are drawn from a substitution chain — either the Fibonacci or the Tribonacci sequence. The coupling is λ (nonlinearity strength). We measure the Inverse Participation Ratio (IPR): a high IPR means the wavefunction is strongly localized; a low IPR means it has spread. At λ = 0 (linear limit), the Fibonacci and Tribonacci chains both show Anderson-like localization at the gap edges — high IPR, tight localization.

The question is: what happens to that localization under nonlinear perturbation?

Prior art — added 2026-09-29. This chapter did not cite the closest existing work, and it should: Z. Zhang, P. Tong, J. Gong and B. Li, “Wave packet dynamics in one-dimensional linear and nonlinear generalized Fibonacci lattices”, Phys. Rev. E 83, 056205 (2011), doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.83.056205. It studies wave-packet spreading on generalized Fibonacci lattices, linear and nonlinear, and reports nonlinear self-trapping in exactly the regime this chapter measures — using the participation number, of which IPR is the reciprocal.

What that paper classifies by, and why it does not settle this chapter. Zhang et al. split generalized Fibonacci lattices into two classes according to whether the lattice has the Pisot–Vijayaraghavan property. Both constants here have it, so both chains fall in the same class and the dichotomy does not separate them:
constantminimal polynomialvalueconjugate moduliPV?
φx² − x − 11.6180340.618034yes
ηx³ − x² − x − 11.8392870.737353, 0.737353yes
So the φ-vs-η difference this chapter reports is a distinction within Zhang's PV class, finer than the classification that paper uses. That is a defence of the result's novelty and it is also a constraint on how it may be explained.

And the obvious explanation is ruled out. If robustness tracked “how strongly Pisot” a constant is — how far its conjugates sit inside the unit circle — then φ (0.618) would beat η (0.737), since η’s conjugates lie closer to the unit circle. The chapter reports the opposite ordering. So the conjugate modulus does not explain the 4× robustness claim; it runs against it. Whatever distinguishes η is not its strength as a Pisot number, and the chapter does not currently say what it is. Status: the measurement stands; the mechanism is open, and one candidate mechanism is now excluded.
Prior art, second item — the experiment already exists for φ. Added 2026-09-29. The chapter says its falsifiable experiment “is set out in WP-62” as future work. Half of it is done and published: Y. Lahini, R. Pugatch, F. Pozzi, M. Sorel, R. Morandotti, N. Davidson and Y. Silberberg, “Observation of a Localization Transition in Quasiperiodic Photonic Lattices”, Phys. Rev. Lett. 103, 013901 (2009), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.013901. In AlGaAs waveguide lattices they realise this chapter’s equation — their Eq. (2) is the DNLS with a Kerr term γ|ψn|²ψn — at the golden mean χ = (√5+1)/2, and measure the participation ratio PR = (Σ|ψn|²)²/Σ|ψn|⁴, which is the reciprocal of this page’s IPR. The φ column of the proposed comparison is therefore not open; it is a 2009 measurement.

One caveat, stated so the citation is not overread: their modulation is the Aubry–André cosine λcos(2πnχ), not a substitution chain. It is the same equation and the same observable at the same constant, but not the same potential, so it constrains this chapter rather than settling it. The substitution-chain case is Zhang et al. above.

What they found about nonlinearity, which cuts across the chapter’s framing. Above the transition (λ/C > 2, the localized regime) nonlinearity produced a slight increase in the width of the localized packet — a rise in PR, i.e. a fall in IPR — while below it (λ/C = 0) nonlinearity reduced expansion. So the sign of the nonlinear effect on IPR depends on which side of the localization transition the system sits. A single “IPR drop under λ” number is only interpretable once the chain’s position relative to its own transition is stated, and this page does not state it.

The finite-size test, computed here for both constants. Lahini et al. (after Bar-Gill) give a concrete criterion: at length L, a ratio behaves as incommensurate only if no commensurate ratio with more than one period in L lies within 1/L of it. They note that Roati et al.’s ratio 1.1972 failed this — its approximant 6/5 has period 5 — and the sharp transition was lost, localization appearing smoothly near λ/C = 7 instead of at 2. Applying the same test to this chapter’s constants, at this chapter’s own chain lengths:

At N = 50 — the low end of this chapter’s range — φ’s nearest approximant repeats ten times across the chain and η’s eight. Neither is comfortably in the regime Lahini et al. call incommensurate, and the N = 50 results should carry that caveat.

And the second candidate mechanism is excluded too. φ has continued fraction [1;1,1,1,…], which makes it the worst-approximable real number there is — the most irrational. η has [1;1,5,4,2,305,…]; the 5, and then the 305, mean it admits far better rational approximations. By the Diophantine measure that governs sharpness of the localization transition, φ is the more robustly quasiperiodic of the two. Together with the Pisot note above, both standard explanations from this literature predict the opposite ordering to the one this chapter reports. That does not refute the numerics — it means the effect, if real, is not explained by either the Pisot property or Diophantine quality, and the chapter should say so rather than leave a mechanism implied. Status: two candidate mechanisms excluded; the measurement stands and is now the thing needing an explanation.
CORRECTION — 2026-09-29. The headline claim above is false, and the kernel says so. EtaChecks.lean checks this chapter against Orthogenesis.Tribonacci and refutes three things on this page.

1. “The only n-bonacci root that sits exactly at c* = 3” — no. η = 1.839287… and c* = 3. These are different numbers, and eta_ne_three proves η ≠ 3 directly from the bracket η < 1.83929. The page’s own §η.3 arithmetic is correct and is what defeats the headline: for V(q) = q³ − c·q the point q = 1 is critical iff c = 3 (V_critical_at_one_iff), so at c = η the point q = 1 is not critical (not_critical_at_eta). The only thing here equal to 3 is n = 3, the number of terms the recurrence sums. The headline rests on reading the index n = 3 and the fold parameter c* = 3 as the same 3. They are not.

2. “Fibonacci is subcritical (φ < c*); Tribonacci is exactly critical (η at c*)” — the distinction does not exist. nbonacci_root_lt_two proves that every root above 1 of xⁿ = Σk<n xᵏ is below 2. The whole ladder converges to 2 from below, so no rung reaches 3 and both φ and η are subcritical. φ < c* is true; η < c* is equally true. There is no critical rung to be the unique one.

3. The Lean block displayed on this page would not compile as shown. It offers ⟨1.839287, by norm_num, by native_decide, …⟩ as a witness that the tribonacci polynomial vanishes. 1.839287 is a six-place decimal, not a root — its residual in x³−x²−x−1 is +1.34×10⁻⁶ (page_witness_not_a_root) — so that conjunct is false at that witness; and native_decide would add Lean.ofReduceBool to the axiom list, contradicting this page’s “no axioms beyond Mathlib”. The real file, AXLE/TribonacciMeasure.lean, does not do this: it defines η by Classical.choose. “Formally verified in Lean 4” in the hero refers to a block that is not the verified file.

What survives. The DNLS measurement itself is untouched by all three: the IPR comparison is numerical work and stands or falls on its own, and the two prior-art notes below bear on it. What does not survive is the explanation offered for it — “consistent with, and explained by, the formal verification that η is the unique critical constant c* = 3” — because there is no such verification and no such criticality. The <meta name="description"> tag repeats the false claim and needs the author’s decision on rewording. Status: headline refuted; measurement open and unexplained.
Prior art, third item — the canonical reference, and the Peierls–Nabarro tool. Added 2026-09-29. The page calls DNLS “the canonical model” and cited no source for it. The standard text is P. G. Kevrekidis, The Discrete Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation, Springer Tracts in Modern Physics 232 (2009), ch. 1, doi:10.1007/978-3-540-89199-4_1, which derives DNLS from the continuum NLS in a periodic potential.

It also bears directly on this chapter’s proposed experiment. The page offers a “Fibonacci/Tribonacci Peierls–Nabarro threshold comparison” as the falsifiable test set out in WP-62 §3. The Peierls–Nabarro barrier — the energetic cost for a localized mode to move one lattice site — is not a new instrument: Kevrekidis ch. 1 lists Peierls barriers among the phenomena already experimentally observed in fabricated AlGaAs waveguide arrays, alongside discrete diffraction and gap solitons. The comparison is a good test; the tool is standard and should be cited as such.

One thing the comparison needs before it is well posed. In a uniform lattice the PN barrier is a single number. In a quasiperiodic chain the on-site energies differ from site to site, so the barrier does too, and “the PN threshold of a Fibonacci chain” is not yet defined — minimum over sites, mean, or the value at the soliton’s own location are three different quantities that will not rank the two chains the same way. WP-62 §3 should say which one it means. Status: test well motivated, definition incomplete.
The Pisot mechanism, quantified — added 2026-09-29. This strengthens the first note above from a qualitative remark to a measured gap. C. McMullen, The Pisot–Vijayaraghavan Numbers (minor thesis, 1983), §1, states the defining property in one sentence: these are the algebraic numbers greater than one whose powers come closer and closer to the integers. §2.1 places that against Weyl’s criterion — for a typical θ the sequence {θⁿ} is uniformly distributed mod 1, and the PV numbers are precisely the atypical cases, “arithmetic in nature”.

That converts the conjugate modulus from a bare number into a rate: ‖xⁿ‖, the distance from xⁿ to the nearest integer, decays like (max|conjugate|)ⁿ. For φ the identity is exact — φⁿ + (−1/φ)ⁿ is the Lucas number Ln (L10 = 123, L20 = 15127, both confirmed), so ‖φⁿ‖ = 0.618034ⁿ on the nose. For η the two complex conjugates give ‖ηⁿ‖ ≤ 2·0.737353ⁿ, approached in an oscillating way. Measured:

(At small n the ordering can invert — at n = 5, η is the closer of the two — because η’s approach oscillates while φ’s does not. The asymptotic ordering is settled from about n = 10 on.)

So the third mechanism fails in the same direction as the other two. On the property that defines the class, φ is not marginally but by two orders of magnitude the stronger Pisot number of the pair. If arithmetic rigidity — how nearly the chain’s generating powers land on integers — were what protects a critical state against nonlinearity, Fibonacci would be the robust one and Tribonacci the fragile one. This chapter measures the reverse. Three independent candidate mechanisms (Pisot rate, conjugate modulus, Diophantine approximation) now all predict the opposite of the reported 4×, which makes the numerical result more interesting rather than less — but it means the page cannot keep gesturing at “η is special” as though the specialness were established. Status: three mechanisms excluded; the effect, if real, is not arithmetic rigidity.
Main Result · Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20230642 · concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20026942
Tribonacci mid-gap states are four times more robust than Fibonacci.
At nonlinearity strength λ = 1.5, the mid-gap IPR of the Fibonacci chain drops by approximately 57% from its linear-limit value. The mid-gap IPR of the Tribonacci chain drops by less than 5%. This is the first numerical DNLS study on a tribonacci substitution chain. Corrected 2026-09-29: this result is not explained by η being critical at c*=3 — EtaChecks.lean proves η ≠ 3, that q=1 is not critical at c=η, and that no n-bonacci root (including φ) is critical at c*=3 either, so there is no subcritical/critical distinction between the two chains to explain the effect. The IPR gap is real and stands as an open, unexplained numerical result; the three candidate arithmetic mechanisms considered above (Pisot rate, conjugate modulus, Diophantine approximation) have each been checked and excluded.

What Version 4 adds. Finite-size scaling at T=10⁴ across N ∈ {500, 1000, 2000} shows the differential ratio IPRtrib/IPRfib generally growing with N — the effect persists toward the thermodynamic limit. Long-time evolution to T=10⁶ (N=1000, λ=1.5) finds the ratio peaking at 4.18× near T=10⁴, then both chains saturating to chain-limited IPR ≈ 0.002 — the peak is a transient finite-size feature. Pre-saturation spreading rates give αtrib > αfib uniformly for λ ∈ [0.5, 2.0]. Sharpest of all is the self-trapping threshold gap: the Fibonacci chain crosses into the self-trapped regime in λ ∈ [4, 8], while the tribonacci chain has not self-trapped by λ = 10 — a difference in regime, not magnitude, tied to η > φ. Companion criticality paper: λc(n) ≈ 0.958 Δₙ + 0.107 across n = 2…5 (10.5281/zenodo.20077205).

§ η.3 · Why c* = 3, and Why η Is Not At ItThe Fold at c* = 3

Corrected 2026-09-29. This section’s title read “Why η Is Critical” and its second paragraph asserted that η is the n-bonacci constant at c*=3. EtaChecks.lean proves the opposite: eta_ne_three (η ≠ 3, from η < 1.83929) and not_critical_at_eta (q=1 is not critical at c=η), and nbonacci_root_lt_two shows the whole n-bonacci ladder stays below 2 — no rung, including η or φ, ever reaches c*=3. The arithmetic below about V(q) and c*=3 itself is correct and untouched; only the identification of η with that threshold is withdrawn.

In the dm³ framework, the criticality parameter c* = 3 is the unique value at which q = 1 is a critical point of the potential V(q) = q³ − cq, and that critical point is non-degenerate (V″(1) = 6 ≠ 0) — the Whitney A₁ condition. V₃ itself has no root at q = 1 (V₃(1) = −2); the double root belongs to the shifted potential V(q) + 2 = (q−1)²(q+2). The falsifiable experiment this chapter implies — the Fibonacci/Tribonacci Peierls–Nabarro threshold comparison — is set out in WP-62 §3. No n-bonacci constant reaches c* = 3, so the fold operator F as defined here never fires for any rung of the ladder, φ or η included — both are, in this framework’s own terms, permanently subcritical.

Withdrawn: the claim that follows in the original draft — that η corresponds to c*=3 “because the Collatz map with c=3 is the unique convergent integer map, and η is its spectral signature” — asserted a specific identification between η and a threshold no n-bonacci root reaches. No replacement identification is offered here; whatever distinguishes the Tribonacci chain in the DNLS measurement (§η.2) is, as of this correction, unexplained.

§ η.5 · The Recurrence LadderWhere η Sits

The n-Bonacci Ladder — Click any rung

The ladder converges to τ = 2 as n → ∞ — the embodiment threshold of the dm³ framework, appearing in the canonical triple (T* = 2π, μ_max = −2, τ = 2). Complete Completeness (g⁶⁴) is the system that has traversed all six rungs and arrived at τ.

Three Falsifiability Conditions

F1. If a tribonacci DNLS chain is found whose mid-gap IPR drops more than 20% at λ = 1.5 under a different boundary condition — periodic rather than fixed-end — the 4× robustness claim requires domain qualification. Currently numerical for open chains, N = 50–400.

F2. If the Tribonacci companion matrix T₃ is shown to have a spectral interpretation inconsistent with the dm³ weight η⁻ᵏ — for example, if a lower-entropy weight produces tighter localization — then the identification of η with c* = 3 requires revision.

F3. If the Lean stub fold_central_charge (Level IX) is proved false — that is, if no functor F: C → V exists sending the rank-3 fold to the Virasoro c = 26 vacuum — then the structural claim connecting η to string theory dimensionality via Dcrit(13) = 26 is a coincidence, not a theorem. Either outcome is honest.

§ η.6 · The Two 26sAn Open Problem

One arithmetic observation that is not yet a theorem: Dcrit(13) = 2 · (13 − 1) + 2 = 26 — which, per EtaChecks.lean’s dcrit_eq_two_mul, is just 2·13 for every argument, not a special fact about 13. The Tribonacci sequence at index 7 is 13 (trib(7) = 13) under the OEIS A000073 indexing (0,0,1,1,2,4,7,13,…) — under the other common indexing (1,1,2,4,7,13,…), 13 sits at index 6, not 7 (noted 2026-09-29; both are checked as trib_seven and trib_six in EtaChecks.lean). The bosonic string critical dimension is also 26 — but computed by a completely different mechanism: anomaly cancellation in the Virasoro algebra, c_total = D·(+1) + (−26) = 0.

Two unrelated derivations. Both land on 26. No identity is claimed. The open problem — stated formally as fold_central_charge in AXLE — is whether a functor F: C → V exists sending the rank-3 fold structure to the c = 26 Virasoro vacuum, with det(T₃) = 1 mapping to modular invariance. This is Level IX of the series.
