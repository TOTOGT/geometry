A Contact Condition for the Fifth Dimension

An author's own derivation, hand-checked only: when is a Kaluza-Klein 5-manifold a contact manifold?

Everything in the earlier chapters is history and cited work. This chapter is different: a short calculation of mine, checked by hand, that links the Kaluza-Klein form of the metric to the contact geometry this series is built on.

Setup

Take Klein's five-dimensional metric with the fifth coordinate y on a circle. Its off-diagonal part defines a one-form on the five-dimensional manifold:

η = dy + κ A, A = Aμ dxμ on the 4D base

Differentiating, dη = κ dA = κ F, where F is the electromagnetic field strength pulled back from the base. Then

η ∧ dη ∧ dη = (dy + κA) ∧ κ² F ∧ F = κ² dy ∧ F ∧ F

The second term, κ³ A ∧ F ∧ F, vanishes because it would be a five-form built only from the four-dimensional base. So the top form is κ² dy ∧ F ∧ F.

[RESULT, HAND-DERIVED]η is a contact form on the 5-manifold exactly where F ∧ F ≠ 0. In vector terms F ∧ F is proportional to E·B, so the condition is that the electric and magnetic fields are not orthogonal at that point. The Reeb field is ∂/∂y, the fifth-dimension translation; Kaluza's charge is its conserved momentum.

What this does and does not claim

It does: give a precise condition under which the Kaluza-Klein circle bundle carries a contact structure whose Reeb flow is the hidden circle. It does not: say the physical universe has a contact structure, or that the series' operators C/K/F/U act on it. A pure electric field or pure magnetic field has F ∧ F = 0, so the simplest textbook configurations are not contact.

Two careful comparisons. First, the Boothby–Wang construction in the literature builds a contact circle bundle over a symplectic base (as I recall it, not rechecked here); here the base is spacetime and the condition is on F ∧ F, a different requirement that I have not looked up. I am not claiming novelty, and a literature check is on the to-do list. Second, Grabowska and Grabowski (arXiv:2207.04484) work in contact Hamiltonian mechanics and mention Kaluza-Klein in one sentence of analogy; they do not derive the condition above. Reduction of Kaluza-Klein by a circle gives a magnetic, symplectic coupling; contact reduction uses an R× scaling action. They resemble each other without being the same.
