The Names It Already Has

The dm³ corpus computes a gradient descent, a Jacobian rank condition, an eigenvalue at a fixed point and a chain rule over a composite map. It calls none of them by those names. This chapter is the translation table, and every address in it was located by a script rather than copied.

A scan on 2026-09-17 found pytorch in one tracked file of this corpus and reinforcement learning in none. That is a real gap and Chapter 4 will close part of it. But a second thing is true and cheaper to fix: much of what the corpus already does has a standard name in the machine-learning literature, and using the local name instead has cost it readers it did not know it had.

2 · What a translation table is and is not

It is not a claim that the objects are identical. That mistake is already on the record in this corpus: the TO hub asserted that sixteen domains sharing a normal form “are not analogies, they are exact mathematical identities”, which cannot hold when every row carries its own fitted parameters — identity would require them to agree. The audit of 2026-09-18 corrected it.

What the table claims is narrower and is the useful half: where the corpus performs a standard operation, the standard name is the one that travels. A reader who knows what a Hessian is does not know what “curvature at κ*” is, and will not look it up. The cost of a private vocabulary is not confusion; it is silence.

The one row that is a genuine identity, and why it is worth separating Similarity. Two matrices are similar exactly when they represent one linear map in different bases (Hefferon p. 274). That is the Coherence Bridge's intended claim, stated in a standard term with a standard test attached. The table's other rows are correspondences of practice; this one is a definition the corpus could actually check — and checking it would settle, row by row, which bridge entries are similar systems and which merely share a shape. Not done here. [OPEN]

3 · What this buys, measured

Nothing in the corpus changes because of this chapter. What changes is the measurement WP-82 taught the series to take of itself. Before: git grep -lic for the machine-learning vocabulary returned 1, 2, 0, 0, 0. That number was read as ignorance. Part of it was naming: gradient descent, Jacobians, Hessians and chain rules are present throughout and were counted as absent because they were spelled in-house.

The rest of the number is real, and Chapter 4 is where it gets paid: a search loop that actually trains, with a Lean gate on its output. This chapter does not pretend to have closed a gap it has only measured more carefully — which is the distinction WP-124 had to learn about its own Figure 1.3.1 count, where seventeen entries read as absent and the page saying so became the thing that filled them.
