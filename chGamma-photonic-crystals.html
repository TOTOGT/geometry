Photonic Crystals & the Graphene Lattice

Light can be trapped. Sajeev John proved it in 1984 — and built the materials to do it. Forty years later, graphene brings tuneability. This chapter reads the language of both crystals.

Section 2 Technical Vocabulary: Crystals and Waves
B2 – C1
photonic crystaln.

A structure with a periodic arrangement of dielectric material whose repetition length is comparable to the wavelength of light, creating interference effects that control which wavelengths can propagate.

“Structures that can confine and manipulate light at a microscopic scale.”Kalvapalle 2026

photonic band gapn.

A range of light wavelengths (or equivalently, frequencies) forbidden from propagating through a photonic crystal. The optical analogue of an electronic band gap in a semiconductor.

“A range of light wavelengths that are prevented from passing through and even existing in a material.”Kalvapalle 2026

graphenen.

A single layer of carbon atoms in a hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice. Isolated in 2004 by Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov (Nobel Prize in Physics, 2010). Its band structure has no gap at the Dirac points, but graphene combined with photonic crystals creates tuneable optical devices.

Anderson localizationn.

The absence of wave diffusion in a disordered medium. Philip W. Anderson showed in 1958 that electron waves in a sufficiently disordered solid are confined rather than conducting. John’s 1984 paper asked whether the same could happen to photons.

“A phenomenon in which electron waves existing in solids with an irregular internal structure are prevented from conducting current.”Kalvapalle 2026

dielectricadj. / n.

A material that is an electrical insulator but can sustain an internal electric field. In photonic crystals, alternating regions of high and low dielectric constant create the periodic index contrast that produces the band gap.

latticen.

A regular, repeating arrangement of points in space. Photonic crystals: typically three-dimensional with sub-micrometre periodicity. Graphene: two-dimensional with a carbon–carbon spacing of about 1.42 Å.

band structuren.

The relationship between a wave’s energy (or frequency) and its momentum (or wavevector) inside a periodic material. A “gap” in the band structure means certain energies have no allowed states.

Dirac pointn.

In graphene’s band structure, the point where the conduction and valence bands meet in a cone-shaped crossing. Electrons near a Dirac point behave as if they have zero effective mass.

interferencen.

The combination of two or more waves producing a new wave pattern. Constructive adds crests; destructive cancels a crest with a trough. The photonic band gap is a consequence of systematic destructive interference.

“When a crest and a trough meet, they cancel each other out.”Kalvapalle 2026, quoting John

defectn.

In a crystal, a deliberate or accidental break in the periodic pattern. In photonic crystals, designed defects create channels where light can propagate — the basis of photonic waveguides.

“By introducing deliberate defects, researchers can create singular pathways that channel chosen light in desired circuit paths.”Kalvapalle 2026

self-assemblyn.

A process in which pre-existing components form an organized structure without external direction. John’s group at U of T achieved self-assembling silicon photonic crystals (c. 2000) with a band gap near 1.5 μm — the wavelength of optical communications.

hollow-core fibren.

An optical fibre that guides light through an air or gas core using photonic band gap effects instead of total internal reflection. Permits higher intensities and wider wavelength range. First life-saving medical use: 2004, Boston, tracheal tumour surgery.

Distinguish: crystal · lattice · structure

Crystal — a solid whose atoms or constituent units are arranged in a highly ordered, repeating pattern. Graphene is a 2D crystal; a photonic crystal is a 3D (or 2D) engineered periodic structure, not necessarily atomic in scale.

Lattice — the abstract mathematical pattern of points that describes the periodicity. The lattice is the geometry; the crystal is the physical object sitting on it.

Structure — the most general term. “Band structure” is the energy-vs.-momentum map, not a physical shape.

Section 3 Research Register
C1 – D1

Terms from research papers and grant proposals. A presenter using these must define them for a mixed audience.

photonic band gap materialn.

The material class whose defining property is a photonic band gap. Named jointly by Sajeev John and Eli Yablonovitch, who published independently in Physical Review Letters weeks apart (1987) and agreed on the name over lunch at the Institute for Advanced Study.

Floquet–Bloch theoremn.

The foundational result for waves in periodic media: solutions of a wave equation with periodic coefficients can be written as a plane wave multiplied by a periodic function. Band structure is a consequence. The same theorem, under the name Floquet theory, governs the transverse exponent μmax in this series’ Vol II and the dm³ framework.

DNA aptamern.

A short, single-stranded DNA molecule folded into a specific 3D shape that binds to a target protein with high selectivity. Used as synthetic molecular receptors in John’s lab-in-a-photonic-crystal diagnostic chips.

“Various DNA aptamers that bind telltale proteins … an instant spectral fingerprint of the disease.”Kalvapalle 2026

spectral fingerprintn.

A pattern of absorption, emission, or transmission intensities across wavelengths that uniquely identifies a substance. In John’s biosensor, disease changes which frequencies pass through the crystal, producing a fingerprint.

Brillouin zonen.

The fundamental domain in reciprocal (momentum) space for a periodic lattice. Band structure is defined over this zone. In graphene’s hexagonal lattice, the corners of the Brillouin zone are the Dirac points.

tuneableadj.

Capable of being adjusted. A graphene layer added to a photonic crystal is tuneable because applying a voltage shifts graphene’s Fermi level and changes its optical response, modifying the crystal’s effective band gap without altering the geometry.

plasmonn.

A collective oscillation of electrons at a material’s surface, coupled to an electromagnetic wave. Graphene supports surface plasmons at terahertz and mid-infrared frequencies; these interact with a photonic crystal’s band gap to produce hybrid polariton modes.

Fermi leveln.

The energy level at which the probability of finding an electron is exactly 50%. In graphene, the Fermi level can be shifted by an external electric field (gate voltage) — which is why graphene is useful as a tuneable element inside photonic structures.

Section 4 Reading: “The Future Is Bright”
B2 – C1

Source Text

Rahul Kalvapalle, “The future is bright: Meet the physicist who discovered how to trap light,” University of Toronto News, 22 October 2026.

The article traces four decades of Sajeev John’s work: a single-author PhD paper at Harvard (1984) proposing that light could be trapped in disordered media; the invention of photonic band gap materials at Princeton (1987); self-assembling photonic crystals at the University of Toronto (c. 2000); and current applications in medicine (hollow-core fibre surgery, 2004; lab-in-a-chip biosensors), solar energy (ultra-thin silicon cells, 50× thinner), and optical computing (microchips with circuits of light).

“I didn’t start out with any plan to invent materials to microscopically control light on a chip. I was simply doing innocent, curiosity-driven physics.”

The article is structured as a profile: personal narrative first, technical content embedded in the story, applications at the end. Notice how the journalist moves from a human moment (“I thought, ‘OK, he’s sharpening his knives here’”) to the physics (“crests and troughs”) without losing the reader. That transition is the craft a researcher presenting to a general audience should study.

Comprehension & Discussion

  1. John’s 1984 paper was about trapping light in disordered media. His 1987 paper was about trapping light in ordered (periodic) structures. In your own words, explain why both approaches produce confinement but through different mechanisms.
  2. The article says photonic crystal solar cells can be “a factor of 50 thinner” yet absorb sunlight “more efficiently.” What property of photonic crystals makes this possible? Use the term band gap in your answer.
  3. Graphene has no band gap at its Dirac points. Researchers open a gap by placing graphene on hexagonal boron nitride, which breaks the sublattice symmetry. Compare this to how John introduces a defect in a photonic crystal to create a channel for light. What is analogous? What is not?
  4. Anderson localization traps waves by disorder. Photonic band gaps trap waves by order. John started with the first and moved to the second. Write a two-sentence paragraph a B1-level reader could follow, explaining why order is easier to engineer than disorder.
  5. Presentation exercise. Three minutes. One application of photonic crystals (medical diagnostics, solar cells, or optical computing). Mixed audience of engineers and business students. Write your opening sentence.
Section 5 Bridge to the Series
D1

Connection to the dm³ Framework

A photonic band gap is a forbidden zone in frequency space, enforced by the geometry of the lattice. The dm³ framework’s operator chain G = U ∘ F ∘ K ∘ C acts on a different kind of periodic structure — a contact manifold with a limit cycle — but the mechanism rhymes: periodicity creates interference, interference creates selection, and selection is what we mean by a “gap” or a “trap.”

The Floquet–Bloch theorem governs both. In photonic crystals it gives the band structure; in the dm³ toy model it gives the transverse exponents (μmax, Ch 0 of this book). The mathematics is the same theorem applied to different wave equations on different periodic domains.

Graphene’s Dirac cone — where the gap closes to a point — is the analogue of a critical parameter value in the dm³ system where the limit cycle loses hyperbolicity. Neither is a failure; both are phase transitions, and both are where the interesting physics lives.

What the series does not claim
Shared functional form (Floquet–Bloch) is not a shared invariant. Photonic band gaps and the dm³ transverse spectrum are governed by the same class of theorem for the same reason — periodicity — and that is all. The Coherence Bridge notes this as a dimensional-analysis coincidence, not an identity. √(J/λ) is the generic crossover scale for any coupling-vs.-localization competition; it does not require shared geometry.

Two kinds of “crystal”

Photonic crystal — an engineered periodic dielectric structure with sub-micrometre features, designed to create a photonic band gap. Periodicity is in the material’s refractive index.

Graphene crystal — a two-dimensional atomic crystal of carbon. Periodicity is in the positions of atoms. Its “band structure” refers to electrons, not photons, though graphene can be used inside a photonic crystal to tune its optical response.

When a presenter says “crystal,” the audience needs to know which one. The word carries different physics depending on whether the periodic thing is atoms or engineered dielectric regions, and whether the waves are electrons or photons.

The hexagons are real

In 2009, researchers at IBM Zürich used a non-contact atomic force microscope (nc-AFM) to image a single pentacene molecule — five fused carbon hexagons. For the first time, the textbook drawing matched a photograph. Individual atoms, the bonds between them, and the empty spaces inside the rings were all resolved. The honeycomb lattice that graphene extends to two infinite dimensions was visible in a single molecule.

The image above was shared by @scitechgirl, sourced from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), Atomic-scale imaging and measurement research.

ESL note · “Imaging” here means producing a picture of something too small to see. An AFM does not use light. It drags a needle one atom wide across the surface and measures the force. The hexagons in the image are force maps, not photographs — but the atoms are exactly where chemistry said they would be.

Section 6 Where They Meet: Graphene Photonic Crystals
C1

The two crystal types converge in active research. Graphene-integrated photonic crystals combine John’s band-gap engineering with graphene’s electrical tuneability.

The open question
Can a photonic band gap be made switchable — present at one gate voltage, absent at another — using graphene as the active element? If yes, the result is a light transistor: a device that lets light through or blocks it, controlled by an electrical signal, at room temperature, with no moving parts.
graphene photonic crystaln.

A photonic crystal in which one or more graphene layers are incorporated into the periodic dielectric stack. Applying a gate voltage shifts graphene’s Fermi level, changing its optical conductivity and the effective refractive index of the stack. The band gap becomes voltage-tuneable without altering the physical geometry.

Analogy to CatGT (Theorem 1)
In the CATGT self-trapping selectivity principle, a reaction pathway’s spatial extent is bounded by a self-trapping radius r*(λ) = a√(J/λ). In a photonic crystal, the band gap width is controlled by the dielectric contrast — the ratio of high to low refractive index. Both are selectivity from confinement: the parameter that determines what gets through is a ratio of coupling to localization. The Floquet–Bloch theorem is the shared mathematical ancestor.
