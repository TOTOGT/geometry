What a Collaborator Found

What a catalysis researcher studied, what one pass over her data did and did not show, and what her review changed.
What this is, and what is pending

A work sample from a physics and catalysis project: an account of a collaboration, written so a reader can judge the level of the work. It is not peer-reviewed. Permission to name her is pending as of 2026-10-22; the paraphrase of her review below should be read with that in mind. No table values are shown, and the experimental numbers stay with her until she agrees otherwise. The companion chapter, Chapter 5, runs the decision rule on invented numbers. This page is checked by book26/ch06-verify.py.

What she studied

Dr. Zilacleide Sousa and co-authors compared two zeolites, HZSM-5 and HMCM-22, converting ethanol to hydrocarbons (Sousa et al., Catalysis Today 234, 2014). Their abstract reports that HZSM-5 favors propene with coke strongly inhibited, while HMCM-22 forms coke in larger channels and favors ethene. The paper itself is paywalled and was not read in full by me; conditions and characterization figures come from her own summary of it. She holds a PhD and coordinates a chemical engineering school.

What I did with her data

The project proposes that one pore-size law with a single self-trapping radius r* explains both materials. If so, a ratio R built from each material’s selectivity and pore radius should equal 1. She sent product distributions versus time on stream for both materials. I computed R with every definition of selectivity and pore dimension I tried (ethene fraction, ethene against propene, and three choices of dimension for HMCM-22). R was never close to 1: it ran from roughly 1.4 to 4 and grew with time on stream. The direction agrees with the paper (more ethene in the larger-cavity material).

That is not a test. I looked at the numbers before fixing the definitions, so I could have found a definition that fit, and the fact that none did is informative but not decisive. It is a signal that the one-parameter law probably does not hold as it stands. There are no uncertainties on her figures, and the transcription of two table images was by hand, with the assignment of images to catalysts and two rows that do not sum to 100 still to be confirmed with her.

What she said, and what it changed

She said plainly that the mathematics is outside her field, and gave seven specific points, which I paraphrase. They changed the plan from “compute and see” to “define first, then test”.

  1. Say which pore dimension is meant. Each zeolite has several, and the answer depends on the choice.
  2. Say what the selectivity measure is. An ethene fraction and a coke weight percent have different denominators, and ethene is both a primary product and a coke precursor, so more ethene can mean less secondary conversion through site blocking.
  3. Compare at the same time on stream.
  4. Keep repeatability, rounding, and reading numbers off graphs separate, and do not simulate distributions without a stated origin.
  5. Define the selectivity measure and the geometry before computing r*, and do not call anything pass or fail at this stage.
  6. Write down in advance what to predict for HMCM-22 if a smooth-pore law does not apply to a cavity.
  7. Without an independent estimate of the energy ratio, the fit cannot identify the lattice spacing.

She also said the paper’s premise sentence overstated things: the two zeolites do not have “nominally identical acid chemistry” but similar nominal Si/Al ratios with different acid properties and pore structures. She pointed out a mislabeled line in a 2016 manuscript, asked that two experimental conditions be kept as separate datasets, and said her advisor must be a co-author on anything that uses her thesis data. Each of these is now in the plan. The proposed test fixes definitions and a decision rule on paper first, as described in Chapter 5.

What this does not show

Not established here
  • That the law is true or false. One exploratory pass is not a test, and the real test needs new runs.
  • That I did this work at a doctoral level. This is what I did and what a specialist said about it; the reader can judge.
  • Any measurement, since no table values are shown and the transcriptions are unconfirmed.
  • That any of the proposed new experiments is funded, scheduled, or agreed to by the people they depend on.
