Book XXVI · Chapter 20 · Reviewing a review

Two Routes, One Spine

A public critique of a Lean release of a Riemann Hypothesis proof, the two proof routes it touches, and what the dependency records do and do not show.
What this is

A record of a review, kept at the strength of its evidence. It is second-hand. The declaration names below come from three review notes written earlier and relayed to the author. For this page the notes were not re-opened and the pinned Lean source was not read. It gives no verdict on the Lean release, on the proof it announces, or on the critique. Nothing was sent to anyone.

What was reviewed

J. L. Nielsen, “The Invalidity of Alpöge’s Proposed Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis” (20 November 2026), criticises a Lean release attached to an announced proof. A mathematical reconstruction of the announced argument, “From theta lifts to zeta zeros” (revision 2, 20 November 2026), is in the author’s Downloads folder and was not read for this page. The release was inspected selectively, read-only, at a pinned mirror commit, 40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005. No archive was downloaded and nothing was cloned.

Principle. In reviewing other people’s work you sometimes find statements of your own. Those are labelled found in review, novelty not claimed, literature not exhaustively searched. This chapter claims nothing of the kind about the release.

The notes describe the Nielsen-associated route as parallel to the headline spine rather than part of it. They also report that this route was later pruned from main (21 November). Pruning a route does not show that an objection to it was resolved.

The reported census

Across the five headline theorems the census reports one dominant dependency spine, not several independently established proofs.

  • Outside the used closure: hypsE₂, everyE₂, plugAll, Hyps, DRS, and the more substantive geometric predicate chain including geoOf.
  • Inside the used closure: anyGeometry, geoCl, GeoCl.

So the critique’s route is not the one the headline proof uses, and the pattern it questions is. That keeps the question open and narrower: what mathematical content do the geometric assumptions actually used in the final proof supply?

Not established here
  • That the Riemann Hypothesis proof is invalid, or that it is valid.
  • That the critique is right, or that it is wrong. No fallacy is attributed to its author.
  • That any Claude session, working in the release’s own workflow, tried several proof strategies, or that anyone directed such attempts. The records show two routes in the code, not who tried them.
  • That the pruning of a route settled anything.

Why a critic could aim at an unused route

Hypothesis, not a finding. The two routes converge and share a pattern. A reader who follows the route named in a critique, rather than the route the headline theorem uses, can describe a real pattern in code the headline proof does not depend on, and still have found a question that applies to the code it does depend on. Nothing here shows that the critique was mistaken. The census points the other way: the pattern is inside the used closure.

What would make this source-anchored

Each step is read-only and selective. None needs an archive download or a clone.

  • A1Read EveryCharacterE.lean at the pinned commit and record the declarations of hypsE₂, everyE₂, plugAll as written.
  • A2Read the declarations of geoCl, anyGeometry, GeoCl and geoOf. Do not infer meaning from names.
  • A3Find the three call sites of geoCl anyGeometry and give file and line for each.
  • A4Audit the axioms of the headline theorems and of zeroFreeAll_of_topG. Only the standard three are acceptable.
  • A5Re-run the census on the pinned commit and compare it with the reported one.
  • A6Locate Nielsen’s second paper and say exactly what changed in it.

Until A1–A5 are done, this page is a pointer to where the question lives.

How we work, and where our own statements are

Each review here uses more than one kind of check on the same statement — a Lean theorem, a sympy re-derivation, a numerical recomputation — each with a control that can fail, and failures are recorded. That describes our process. Statements of our own that turned up in other reviews, such as the Euler 1747 adjacent results, are listed with their status in Chapter 8, none with a novelty claim.
