When Bail Is Not Posted

What a million New York court records can and cannot say about bail and early guilty pleas, including a $1 code that changes the reading and a judge check that fails.

Newark, New Jersey · 8 November 2026 · A work sample for economics research roles; no posting is named

What this chapter is not

It is a descriptive audit of public data. It does not show that anyone could not pay, that detention caused a plea, or that money is the mechanism. Individual judges are not named.

Among New York local-court misdemeanors with a recorded first cash-bail amount above $1, not posting bail at arraignment goes with a plea disposition rate about 7.6 percentage points higher within 30 days.

That figure compares cases with the same charge, court, year, criminal-history category and bail band, on the 4,778 not-posted and 977 posted cases with a match. It holds at +7.6, +7.7 and +7.6 under three sets of controls on that one fixed group.

1From a million rows to the comparison

The source is the Office of Court Administration's pretrial release extract. Each row is one defendant on one docket. Dockets from the same arrest can be linked, but one person cannot be followed across arrests.

2What the fields actually mean

  • Timing. Dates are month and year only, so the elapsed-days field is used, never subtracted dates. Same-day dispositions are recorded as 1, and results are reported at both 30 and 31.
  • The $1 code. The data dictionary says $1 is often set to credit time served when a person is remanded on another case. Of 43,803 misdemeanor bail cases, 24,913 carry exactly $1. That is why they are kept apart. The $1 amount is an imperfect marker of another hold, and an amount above $1 does not exclude one.
  • Criminal history. A blank means no matching record, not a clean record, so there are three categories: positive, all zero, unknown.
  • Plea date. Disposition is when all charges were resolved. The outcome is a plea disposition, not the day a plea was entered.
  • Later posting. Among not-posted cases above $1, 13,050 of 15,876 show no posting observed. That is not the same as never posted.

In 462 of 645 cells there is one posted case, so this understates uncertainty and is not a confidence interval.

5A judge check that does not support the design

The natural next step is to compare stricter and more lenient judges. The extract gives the judge but only the month, so judges can be compared only within a court and month, across 596,710 arraignments and 426 judges.

A judge one standard deviation stricter also hears a caseload 3.26 points more likely to be a class A misdemeanor, 1.39 points more likely to be assault and 1.07 points more likely to involve a Black defendant, and cases are 2.57 points more likely to continue past arraignment. These checks cannot separate judge assignment from other causes, so no causal judge result is reported.

§Limits

  • People who post bail differ: 53% of posted cases have a positive criminal history, against 74% of not-posted cases.
  • Bail amounts reflect a judge's view of the case, so they are not a dose of financial pressure.
  • The extract has no measure of wealth, work or obligations such as rent.
  • Court date and shift data would improve assignment controls, but would not establish a causal design on their own.
