Two Postings, One Employer?

Two listings for a recruiting coordinator in New York, read side by side. No employer is named.
What this is

A comparison of the text of two public job postings, as they appeared on 2026-10-26. It records what each says and where they differ. It does not show that they are the same role, and it does not say whether an application was sent. The employer is left out, as everywhere in this book.

What the two postings share

Both are titled recruiting (or recruitment) coordinator. Both were posted two weeks before the date above and close on the same day, 2026-11-09, at 12:59 AM. Both are full time, ask for a bachelor’s degree, ask for US work authorization, and list medical, dental and vision coverage with paid time off. The duties overlap: schedule interviews across several calendars, be the candidate’s point of contact, work with recruiters and business stakeholders, handle confidential information, and help improve the recruiting process.

The second posting names a financial services firm in its text. The first describes only “one of the world’s most respected investment and technology firms” and a “highly regarded global organization” and does not name it. The second is listed under a staffing firm; the first shows no employer line in the copy I have. So the match rests on the second posting’s text and on the shared dates and title. It is an inference, not something either posting states.

Each difference could mean two different teams, or two openings for one team, or one opening described by two writers. A staffing firm and a firm’s own recruiters would naturally write different copy for the same seat. Nothing in the text separates these readings.

What would settle it

  • The employer line on the first posting, if it names the same firm as the second.
  • A requisition or job number on either posting, matched to the other.
  • The recruiter or the firm saying whether there is one seat or two.

Until one of those exists, the sensible record is two postings, treated as possibly one role. If they are one role, the pay and schedule differ by who wrote the copy, and the stated figures disagree, so the posted range is not a single number.

What this does not show

Not established here
  • That the two postings are the same role, or the same employer. The match is an inference from one posting’s text.
  • That the pay is what an offer would be. These are posted figures, and the postings disagree.
  • That an application was prepared or sent for either one. The count in chapter 1 is not changed by this chapter.
  • Anything about the firm itself. No outside source was read for this chapter.
