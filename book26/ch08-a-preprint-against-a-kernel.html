A Preprint Against a Kernel

A 247-page theory cites five Lean-verified theorems. What the five say, what is still unchecked, and what a full check would cost.
What this is

A working record, partly done. The preprint is cited as literature (J. L. Nielsen, “A Complete and Unified Gauge Theory with Gravity on the Complex Hopf Fibration”, preprint dated 14 Aug 2026). This chapter is about its claims and its Lean file, not its author. Most of the checklist below is open. A checked box means a step was done and can be re-run, not that the theory is right or wrong.

What was received

The preprint claims that charge quantization and unification force one fibre bundle, and that the Standard Model gauge group, three generations, the particle masses and the cosmological constant then follow as theorems with no free parameter. Its abstract says five structural theorems are checked in Lean 4. It has about 300 lines containing the word “Theorem”; a count of distinctly numbered statements gives 140.

A full kernel check of everything cannot be done here or soon. Most of the statements concern objects the Lean library does not yet have: bundles with connections, spectra of the Beltrami operator, Ray–Singer torsion. So the work is sorted by what a kernel can reach.

The checklist

Five steps are done. Seven are open.

  • C1Compile and axiom-audit the paper's own Lean appendix. The appendix was transcribed with its symbols restored and run on 2026-10-26 (Lean v4.32.0, Mathlib build). It compiles. The audit lists 7 theorems, all within the three standard axioms: five depend on none, one on propext, one on propext and Quot.sound. This matches what the paper reports. Report: tools/verify-audit/2026-10-26/.
  • C2Read each of the five kernels for what it says. A reading, not a proof, with two of the points confirmed by short examples that compiled (see section 3). None of the five is about physics.
  • C3Census of the numbered statements. 140 in all: 83 theorems, 22 corollaries, 10 propositions, 10 lemmas, 13 definitions, 2 axioms. The tier labels (about 22 arithmetic or algebra, about 99 needing objects the Lean library lacks) come from keyword matching and are a first sort, not a reading of each statement. File: book26/nielsen-census.tsv.
  • C4Check the numbers in the abstract. Recomputed in Python (book26/nielsen-abstract-arithmetic.py) and checked in Lean (book26/NielsenAbstractArithmetic.lean; 6 theorems, standard axioms only, run 2026-10-26). Reproduced: 3/(4π) = 0.238732, which is 0.39σ from the Thomson-limit value the paper compares with; the neutrino masses sum to 59.282 meV; the boson masses sit within 0.23σ of PDG 2025; the cosmological-constant formula gives 2.940×10−122. Not reproduced as a prediction: at the Z pole, the abstract’s own masses give 1 − (mW/mZ)2 = 0.2232, which differs from 3/(4π) by more than 0.015, and the paper bridges the gap with a “dressing” equal to the residual. The larger neutrino splitting is compared with 2.453×10−3 eV2, which I believe, from memory and not yet verified, is the 3–2 splitting; against the 3–1 splitting from the one NuFit-6.0 block I could read it is −3.2σ. The H0 value is the cosmological-constant formula rearranged with the measured dark-energy fraction.
  • C5Check the algebra statements (about 6).
  • C6Hypothesis audit of the remaining statements (about 99). For each: what is assumed, what is derived, and what a kernel would need that does not exist yet.
  • C7Read the full 247 pages. Only the front matter, the contents, one table and the Lean appendix have been read.
  • C8Reconcile the stated inputs. The abstract says the one empirical input is the electroweak scale. A later table lists that scale, with c, ℏ and G, among the “predictions”.
  • C9Check the sin²θW claim. 3/(4π) is 0.238732. The paper reports agreement within 0.4σ; which scheme and which uncertainty that uses has not been checked.
  • C10Confirm the product-splitting reading of kernel 2. The reading in section 3 is a hand derivation and has not been tested.
  • C11Check the remaining arithmetic claims. Quark and boson masses, couplings and the anomalous moments, each tabulated from the paper’s own script as the lepton sector now is.
  • C12Lepton sector: formula, constants and outputs in one table. From the paper’s own appendix script, transcribed by hand and confirmed against what that script prints (book26/nielsen-lepton-table.py, run). The three masses come out 0.23% high, a uniform offset the script itself prints, which is more than 106, 105 and 40 standard deviations for the electron, muon and tau, so the abstract’s “within PDG uncertainties” does not describe them. The mass ratios agree to 0.002% and 0.006%, which is a one-parameter result anchored on one mass. The exponent has five terms; the one that distinguishes the tau, a trefoil “regularization”, acts on that lepton alone and moves it by about 1.1%. A Lean version of the deviation comparison is written (NielsenLeptonDeviation.lean) and was run on 2026-10-26: 363 s, three declarations, axioms within the permitted set, no sorryAx (receipt: tools/verify-audit/2026-10-26/geometry__book26__NielsenLeptonDeviation.axioms.txt). A clean axiom report is not a reading of the statement — per R20 a theorem can assume its conclusion and still report clean — so what this establishes is that three named declarations elaborate, not that they say the deviations exceed the quoted uncertainties.

What the five kernels say

KernelWhat the Lean provesWhat it does not
1. Charge quantization forces holonomyIf some charge is inadmissible, the holonomy is not trivial. A contrapositive in a setup where Charge, Phase and Loop are arbitrary types.Nothing about U(1), a charge lattice or a connection.
2. A domain has no product splitA ring with no zero divisors has only the idempotents 0 and 1, and a direct product of two nonzero rings has zero divisors.A direct product of rings corresponds to a disjoint union of spaces (connectedness). A Cartesian product of spaces corresponds to a tensor product (Künneth). My reading, untested: the Lean does not prove “no product splitting of the bundle”.
3. Completeness forces universalityAny two classifying objects for the same bundle functor are equivalent. The paper’s U1_forcing is this theorem renamed; an example that closes by rfl shows it.That the complex projective space classifies anything. That is a hypothesis.
4. Completeness as a theoremEvery bundle is a pullback of the universal one. This restates a field of the hypothesis Classifies, closed by that field.That nature’s fields satisfy the hypothesis.
5. Affine absorptionAdding a constant and a linear term does not change a second difference. Linear integer arithmetic.Anything about spectra or masses.

The Lean compiles and the axioms are standard, so these are real theorems. The gap is between what they state and what their names and the abstract claim.

What would make a claim like this refereeable

This section is about method, and applies to any large claim, including this project’s own. A kernel checks that conclusions follow from stated hypotheses. It cannot check that nature satisfies them. So the useful question is where each claim sits relative to the kernel.

  1. Split the work into pieces a referee can read. One paper on what the premises force, one on a fixed formula and its numbers, one on cosmology and neutrinos. A referee can check twenty-five pages; no one can referee 247.
  2. Freeze the formula before comparing. Publish the exact formula, every constant and every discrete choice, with a commit hash, and only then compare with the data. Count each discrete choice as a parameter and say how many there are.
  3. Add negative controls. Put the formula on a wrong case, such as a wrong lens space or a shuffled level, and show the fit gets worse. Without that, close agreement is not informative.
  4. Use the kernel where it is strong. Check that each number in a results table is exactly the output of the stated formula and constants. Give each Lean theorem a mutation test: negate a hypothesis and confirm it stops holding. Where the library lacks an object, state a named hypothesis and list it in a ledger.
  5. Reconcile the arithmetic with the prose. Compare each quantity with the matching reference quantity and report both uncertainties. Say which statements are predictions and which are rearrangements.
  6. Write at the strength of the evidence. Vary the strength of claims, let headings ask questions, and define terms where they first appear.

One claim in this preprint is pure mathematics and independent of the physics: that a lens-space determinant yields a ζ(3)n² term. A topologist can referee it as it stands, which makes it the natural first piece to submit.

What this does not show

Not established here
  • That the theory is false. Nothing here tests the masses, the gauge group or the number of generations.
  • That the Lean file is faulty. It compiles, and its theorems are what they say they are.
  • That the tier counts are right. They come from keyword matching.
  • That the product-splitting reading of kernel 2 is correct. It is a hand derivation.
  • Anything about the author, who is cited only because the preprint is public.
