Statistician review brief: CheXNet worked example

Review document · working version · for an independent statistician

Context

A short chapter demonstrates a method for checking medical-AI claims, using the CheXNet paper (arXiv:1711.05225 v3) as the worked example. The chapter recomputes what the printed numbers allow and labels each result as verified, partially supported, contradicted or not independently checkable. We ask for a review of three statistical statements only. Please do not review the clinical claims.

Please work from the source-verified figures that accompany this brief (the output of the separate source-fidelity checklist). If a figure there differs from the chapter, use the verified figure and tell us.

Figures in use (as printed in the paper, once verified)

  • 420 test images; four radiologists; bootstrap with 10,000 resamples; 95% intervals from the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles; the difference computed on the same bootstrap samples.
  • F1: CheXNet 0.435 (0.387, 0.481); radiologist average 0.387 (0.330, 0.442); individual radiologists 0.383, 0.356, 0.365, 0.442; difference 0.051 (0.005, 0.084).
  • Not available to us: the number of pneumonia-positive images, per-image predictions and labels, and bootstrap replicates.

Question 1: the difference (ledger row C2)

The paper prints a difference of 0.051. Subtracting the printed point estimates gives 0.435 − 0.387 = 0.048, and rounding both to three decimals cannot account for the gap. The chapter calls this an unexplained discrepancy, not an error. Could the bootstrap estimator explain it, for example a mean or median of the resampled differences, or a different way of averaging across radiologists? What evidence would establish the explanation?

Question 2: interval midpoints (rows C3a and C3b)

The chapter notes that each interval's midpoint is close to its point estimate, and says percentile-bootstrap intervals can be asymmetric for a nonlinear statistic such as F1, so this is only a weak plausibility signal. Is that description statistically accurate? Do the midpoint calculations carry any meaningful evidence?

Question 3: implied correlation (row C4)

Treating the percentile intervals as if they were symmetric normal intervals, we derived standard errors of about 0.024 (CheXNet), 0.029 (radiologists) and 0.020 (difference). These imply a correlation of about 0.72 between the two resampled F1 values. The chapter calls this an exploratory approximation, not a check of the bootstrap. Is that a defensible exploratory use, or is even that too strong given what is printed?

One more request

Please separate, in your answer, (a) conclusions that can be established from the published summary statistics alone from (b) conclusions that would need per-image predictions, labels or bootstrap replicates.
