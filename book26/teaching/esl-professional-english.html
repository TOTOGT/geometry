Professional English for credentialing and clinical-position applications

Output 2 of 2 · advanced (about B2 to C1) · for physicians who already hold a medical degree, some with specialty training · 90 to 120 minutes · all letters and people are fictional · pairs with the pathway comparison

Aim. The medicine is not the difficulty here. The language of recognition is: who decides, what is binding, what is only guidance, and how to ask in writing without overclaiming. Students should leave able to (1) read regulatory English closely, (2) draft an accurate credential inquiry, and (3) describe surgical experience truthfully in an interview.
Rule for every task: never write that you are licensed, certified or qualified to practise somewhere unless that is true. Use placeholders such as [number] when you do not know a figure.

Part 1. Credentialing vocabulary (20 min)

Match, then use each term in a sentence about your own situation. English glosses for German terms are the teacher's, not the source's.

Part 2. Reading regulatory English (30 min)

Short extracts from the pages used in the pathway comparison. Read each, underline the modal verbs, then answer.

Extract A (professional association summary, US)

"All state licensing jurisdictions require a graduate of a foreign medical school to complete at least one year of accredited U.S. or Canadian graduate medical education."

  1. Is this a law, or a summary of laws? Who published it?
  2. What does "at least" change about the meaning?
  3. Rewrite it for a colleague in your own words without using "jurisdiction".

Extract B (German federal chamber, original and gloss)

"nur empfehlenden Charakter" (of the model specialty regulation, for the state chambers). Teacher gloss: of recommendatory character only.

  1. What does this tell you about which text applies to you?
  2. Which English word pair marks the difference: advisory / binding or draft / final?

Extract C (draft professional guidance, US)

The draft says the automatic move from a provisional to a full licence is "neither ordinary nor typical".

  1. Is it safe to tell a colleague "after two years you will get a full licence"? Explain using the quote.
  2. The document is a draft. How does that change how you cite it in a letter?

Extract D (German orientation guide, teacher paraphrase)

A temporary permit may be limited to specific activities or employers and is valid only in the state that issued it.

  1. List the three limits. Which one matters most if you want to move between hospitals in two states?

Discussion (5 min): binding law, professional guidance, institution-specific policy. Sort these: a state licensing statute; a hospital's privileging policy; a specialty society's recommendation; a residency program's rule on graduation years.

Part 3. Writing a credential inquiry (30 min)

Situation (fictional). Dr. [Name] completed medical school and a [number]-year ophthalmology residency in [country], and has [number] years in cornea and refractive surgery. Dr. [Name] writes to a state medical chamber or board to ask how the training would be assessed.

3a. Fix the draft

Dear Sir,
I am ophthalmologist and LASIK surgeon with licence from Brazil. Please give me recognition so I can operate in your state next month. I have done many surgeries. What is the fastest way, without repeat residency?
Regards

Find at least six problems (register, accuracy, overclaiming, missing information, the assumption in "without repeat residency", the unknown addressee). Rewrite it in 120 to 160 words.

3b. A model structure

  1. Subject line naming the request and your profession.
  2. One sentence on who you are, with placeholders for facts you have not checked.
  3. Numbered questions, each answerable in writing (for example: "Which body assesses foreign specialty training, and does it credit periods already completed?" and "Is a supervised or provisional route available for graduates with [number] years of specialty practice?").
  4. Enclosures you can supply (diploma, transcript, certified translation, list of procedures).
  5. A close that asks for the governing rule, not for a favour.

3c. Reading a reply

"Your application will be reviewed on an individual basis. Credit for prior training, if any, is determined by the specialty department. Please note that this correspondence is not a determination of eligibility."

Questions: Did the board promise credit? Who decides credit? What should your next email ask? Which phrase protects the writer from giving a binding answer? (Fictional text, written to model typical hedging.)

Part 4. Speaking: describing surgical experience (20 min)

Role-play in threes: interviewer, candidate, observer. Scenario: an interview for a clinical or research position in a department that will not let the candidate operate independently.

  • Candidate describes [number] procedures using accurate verbs: I performed, I assisted, I observed, under supervision, independently. The observer checks that each verb matches the truth.
  • Interviewer asks: "What can you do here, and what would you need a licence for?" Candidate answers without claiming permission they do not have.
  • Candidate asks one question about supervision and one about how the department treats credential evaluation timelines.
  • Model hedges: as far as I understand, I will confirm with the board, subject to licensure.

Part 5. Reflection and next step (10 min)

Each student writes three questions that only an official source can answer for their own case, and names the body to ask. Teacher collects them. Do not give legal or immigration advice in class; point to the sources listed in the comparison and to the OPEN items.

Teacher notes and key

Part 2. A: a summary by a professional association (AMA, updated September 2023), not law; "at least" sets a minimum and some states ask for more (the summary says 12 require two years, 25 three). B: only the state chamber's own regulation binds you; advisory / binding. C: no. The draft calls automatic conversion neither ordinary nor typical; cite it as a draft recommendation, not as a rule. D: activity, employer, state. Moving states makes the state limit matter most.

Binding vs guidance: statute: binding; hospital privileging policy: institution-specific; society recommendation: professional guidance (such as the German KRC, which calls itself recommendations); a residency program's graduation-year rule: institution-specific.

3a problems: "Sir" assumes gender and addressee; missing article ("an ophthalmologist"); "licence from Brazil" is vague (name the registration); "give me recognition" and "next month" demand a result and a date; "many surgeries" is unquantified; "without repeat residency" presumes the answer. 3c: no promise; the specialty department decides credit; next email asks which criteria and documents the department uses and the expected time; the last sentence is the protective hedge.

Accuracy reminders. This resource claims nothing about a particular country's current rules beyond what the pathway comparison marks VERIFIED or PARTIAL. If a student's case needs OPEN items, they should ask the named authority. A fellowship, observership or provisional licence does not by itself grant permission to practise.
