What the Bridge carries

The Bridge is a translation tool. A scientist who knows how a transition works in one field uses it to ask whether the same questions can be put to another. This chapter says what it carries across, what it does not, and what has already been tried, withdrawn and kept.

How claims are marked

computed a script beside this book, or in tools/, produced the number, and ch01-verify.py re-checks it. sourced a source or an earlier page of this series was read for the statement. to confirm the statement comes from a secondary account or from the author and has not been checked against a primary source. author the author’s decision or identification, recorded as his, not derived here.

1 · A translation tool, at the size it can be defended

The Bridge carries two things from one field to another: a normal form, the fold, together with its exponents, and a fixed list of questions asked in the same order. It does not carry constants, units or mechanism. A rate measured in one field is not a rate in another, and nothing in the Bridge turns one into the other. author, 2026-09-25

That is a smaller claim than the first table in this series made. The rest of the chapter is about why the smaller claim is the one to keep, and what the larger one turned into when it was tested on its own numbers.

Why this has its own book

Until version 5 of the CATGT zeolite paper the Bridge sat inside that paper’s framing, as a claim that several domains share one invariant. It was cut there, deliberately. A length scale of the form √(J/λ) is what dimensional analysis gives for any competition between coupling and localisation, so finding it in several fields does not require shared geometry, and most of those domains had no derivation behind them. The Bridge continues here as a protocol with its own ledger, and no paper depends on it as a premise. from the project record

2 · The first attempt: six rooms

Book III, Chapter 20 printed a table of six systems (HPA stress, neural oscillations, the circadian clock, immune adaptation, plasma reconnection, market volatility), each with an exponent μmax, a frequency ω, an exponent β and a threshold κ*. Theorem 5.4 above it said the six are objects in one category, related by explicit contact morphisms. Book III Ch 20

On 2026-09-19 the chapter withdrew the identity claim. “Identity” has a standard test: two matrices are similar exactly when they represent the same linear map in different bases. Near its cycle each room is a two-by-two system with eigenvalues μ ± iω, so the test can be run on the table’s own figures. 3 · What survived: a test for the fold

Withdrawing the table left the question that was worth asking: in which systems does a fold actually occur? The protocol written on 2026-11-06 says the fold operator F is present only if two things hold together. First, the relaxation rate toward the new state vanishes like the square root of the distance from the critical value. Second, a control sweep shows hysteresis: a jump, and a different path back. The square-root law alone is not enough, because the amplitude of a supercritical pitchfork or a Hopf branch also grows like a square root; what separates a fold is the exponent of the rate, one half for a fold and one for the transcritical, pitchfork and Hopf cases. protocol doc, 2026-11-06

The algebra of those exponents is in Orthogenesis/Bifurcation/BifurcationDiscriminator.lean. It proves exact polynomial identities about explicit coefficients. It does not prove hysteresis and it does not classify any real system. status note, run on the author’s Mac 2026-11-06

The test was given controls before it was given data. tools/fold_classifier_controls.py runs it on three positives (a bistable cubic, a subcritical pitchfork, a toy immune model) and five controls (transcritical, supercritical pitchfork, Hopf radial part, linear damping, diffusion). The note of 2026-11-06 records every positive as fold-present and every control as fold-absent, with each gate alone failing one control, so only the conjunction passes. The thresholds were tuned to this set, the controls cover one-dimensional smooth cases only, and the note records the run as done in a cloud sandbox and not yet on the author’s machine. This book does not count that run as verified until a receipt is logged. to confirm

4 · The ledger

Every row in the Bridge’s machine-readable table carries a claim level, and the level says how much of the row came from the domain itself.

LevelWhat it means in the v8.2 table
empiricalMeasured parameters calibrated to physical data; all five questions answered from the domain’s own mathematics; any threshold derived from observation.
analogicalStructural similarity identified; three or four of the five questions answered from the domain’s own results; any threshold is a placed constant, not a domain result.
formally_statedStated as a Lean theorem with honest sorry; pure mathematics, no physical calibration claimed.
formally_verifiedProved in Lean with zero sorry. No row qualifies yet.

The validator, tools/bridge/validate_bridge_v8_2.py, run on the committed table, reports 18 rows, 13 passed, 5 failed, 8 warnings. computed The five failures are neural_microtubule, phyllotaxis, principia_vol2, generative_contact_mechanics and biological_transitions_togt. The reasons, from the 2026-11-06 status note: two rows are labelled empirical although their threshold of 33 is a placed constant or the author’s own paper; one has a threshold on a pure-mathematics row; one cites an asymptotic source; and phyllotaxis is labelled empirical with no calibrated parameters. That last row is the honest one, and it still fails, because the rule that an empirical row needs calibrated parameters was not loosened to let it through. The validator reports these; it does not decide what to do about them, and the decision is the author’s. status doc, 2026-11-06

A table in which several rows fail its own validator is the table working, not breaking. A reader can see which rows have earned their level and which have not.

Open · the author’s call

Which meaning each letter keeps in this book, and whether the others get new symbols. Until that is decided, a row that fills a slot must say in the row which vocabulary it is using. The Saturn row of Chapter 8 is a live case: its working notes of 2026-11-12 use C for the seed imbalance that starts the pattern, K for a correcting response and E for entropy identified with the rotation, none of which is any of the three definitions above. They are recorded there as the author’s identifications, not as a fourth vocabulary adopted.

6 · What one row has to say

For each operator a row records whether it is present, skipped or undetermined, and the type of evidence behind that: derived from a stated model, computed by a script or a kernel-checked statement about a stated model, asserted in text with no model behind it, or measured. The row’s status is the weakest evidence type among the operators it claims, and the ladder is established, then model, then analogy. protocol doc, 2026-11-06 A row that cannot reach established on data from its own field does not move up because it resembles a row that did.

A row that claims to be empirical is pre-registered before any data is read: the source, every analysis choice, the rival models and the outcome rule, committed and never edited, with the checker shown able to detect the effect on synthetic worlds first. Where the checker’s power is low, a failure on real data is uninformative and does not demote the row. repo rule R30

7 · The first new row: Saturn’s polygons

Saturn’s north polar jet carries a hexagonal wave pattern, observed since the Voyager images of 1981. A decagon has been reported at the south pole in 2023–2025 observations. to confirm: as relayed to the author; the primary papers have not been read for this book The shared form with the rest of the Bridge is symmetry breaking: a continuous rotational symmetry reduced to an n-fold pattern. That the two poles select different n says the mode number is chosen by conditions (jet speed, latitude, width, rotation, thermal structure) and is not a universal constant. No dispersion relation for that choice has been derived here.

The repository’s SaturnHexagon.lean models six sectors by construction (its vectors live in Fin 6 → ℝ); it proves statements about that model and does not derive the number six, and it says nothing about ten. Formalising a model does not test the hypothesis it models. file header read 2026-11-12; kernel status not re-run here

A draft row, run through the validator on a temporary copy of the table on 2026-11-12 and not yet in the committed table, fails on two grounds only: it has no calibrated parameters, and its fold test is untested because no control sweep exists. Its five operator slots are filled with the author’s identifications, labelled as such, and it stays at the empirical level in the draft because moving it to analogical would rest on those identifications and not on anything derived from Saturn data. What would move it is a measured n compared with a predicted n at each pole, or a measured hysteresis. computed: validator output

8 · What this chapter does not claim

It does not claim that the systems named here share a mechanism, a constant, or a geometry. It does not claim the fold test classifies any real system; it has been run on constructed cases only. It does not claim the five failing rows are wrong, only that they have not earned the level they carry. And it does not treat a Lean file as evidence about a physical system: a kernel-checked theorem about a two-by-two linear model says nothing about a clock, and a theorem about six sectors says nothing about why a planet chose six.
