Modular Equations and π

A modular equation is a machine with one dial. You set the dial to an integer $N$, and what comes out is a series for $1/\pi$ whose convergence rate you chose in advance. This chapter turns the dial at $N = 58$, slowly, and then says what changes at other $N$.

1 · The one object

Start with the elliptic modulus $k(q)$, a ratio of theta series — an infinite sum, transcendental, with no reason on its face to be nice anywhere. Evaluate it at a very particular place:

kN = k(e−π√N),   αN = kN2

At those points — and only at those points — the transcendental collapses into radicals. At $N = 58$:

$$\alpha_{58} = (13\sqrt{58} - 99)^2\,(99 - 70\sqrt 2)^2 \approx 6.506 \times 10^{-10}$$

That is not an approximation dressed as an identity. Computed from the theta series at 120 digits and from the radicals independently, the two agree to eighty places. The reason it happens at all is complex multiplication, and the reason it happens here is Vol XI's business, not this chapter's.

The trap, stated before you can fall in it $\alpha_N = k_N^2$, not $k_N$. The theta ratio $(\theta_2/\theta_3)$ gives $k$; you want its square, so the fourth power of the ratio. The producing script for this material got that wrong on its first run and the disagreement showed up at the fifth digit, which is exactly far enough in to look like a deep problem and exactly shallow enough to be a typo.

2 · The dial, and what it controls

From $\alpha_N$ build the class invariant

gN12 = (k′N)² / (2 kN),   k′ = √(1 − α)

and then the quantity that actually sets the series:

xN = 1 / (64 (gN12 + gN−12)²)

At $N = 58$ that is exactly $1/396^4$, which is why $396^{4n}$ is the denominator of Ramanujan's series and why it gains eight digits a term. The dial controls the convergence rate. Larger $N$, smaller $x_N$, more digits per term. Chudnovsky's series is the same machine at a larger discriminant, paying 14 digits a term instead of 8.

3 · Why you must add before you invert

This is the step that looks like bookkeeping and is not. $g_{58}^{12}$ is not an integer:

$$g_{58}^{12} = \varepsilon^6 = 9801 + 1820\sqrt{29}, \qquad \varepsilon = \tfrac{5+\sqrt{29}}{2} \approx 19601.99999489\ldots$$

It sits a hair under 19602 and a hair over 19601 and equals neither. But $\varepsilon$ is the fundamental unit of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{29})$ with norm $-1$, so $\varepsilon^6$ has norm $+1$, which says

$$9801^2 - 29\cdot 1820^2 = 1$$

— Pell's equation — and that says the conjugate is the inverse. So the irrational halves cancel on addition:

$$g^{12} + g^{-12} = (9801 + 1820\sqrt{29}) + (9801 - 1820\sqrt{29}) = 19602 \quad\text{exactly}$$

Use $g^{12}$ alone and you land within eight significant figures of the right answer and are wrong. Add first and the answer is an integer. The cancellation is the whole reason the constants in these series are integers at all.

4 · The recipe, so it runs at other $N$

Procedure
  1. Pick $N$. Compute $\alpha_N = k(e^{-\pi\sqrt N})^2$ from theta series at high precision. Do not look it up.
  2. Form $g_N^{12} = (1-\alpha_N)/(2\sqrt{\alpha_N})$.
  3. Compute $S = g_N^{12} + g_N^{-12}$. If $S$ is not an integer to your working precision, stop — either the precision is short or $N$ is not a discriminant where this closes.
  4. $x_N = 1/(64 S^2)$. The series denominator is $x_N^{-n}$; $\log_{10}(1/x_N)$ minus the growth of the factorial ratio is your digits per term, and you can read it off before computing a single term.
  5. The linear coefficients ($1103$ and $26390$ at $N=58$) are not produced by this procedure. See §5.

5 · What this chapter does not give you OPEN

  1. 1103 and 26390 are not derived anywhere here. Everything else reduces to 99: $9801 = 99^2$, $396 = 4\cdot 99$, $19602 = 2\cdot 99^2$. Those two do not, and no step above produces them. They come from the general theorem, which this corpus has tested at one point and not transcribed.
  2. Step 3's stopping rule is a practical test, not a criterion. It tells you when you have failed, not when you will succeed.
  3. Nothing here proves the series converges to $1/\pi$. It is verified numerically to 104 digits, which is evidence and not proof.
