§ 0.1 · The Cajueiro
Cajueiro de Pirangi · The Largest Cashew Tree on Earth 8,500 m² of canopy · one root system · one seed · Pirangi do Norte, RN, Brazil

From the ground, it looks like a forest. Dozens of trunks, hundreds of branches, a canopy the size of a city block. It is one tree. One root system. One organism that learned to send its branches to the ground, take root again, and rise again — each new trunk indistinguishable from an independent tree to anyone who does not know to look for the root.

This is not a metaphor for the Principia Orthogona. It is the Principia Orthogona. Each volume appears to stand alone. Each chapter appears independent. The student portal is called Cajueiro because the student who learns contact geometry and the student who learns linguistics are sitting under the same canopy — they do not yet know they share a root.

The G-operator — G = U ∘ F ∘ K ∘ C — is the formal description of what the Cajueiro does biologically. Compress to the essential seed (C). Build curvature until the threshold (K). Fold into a new topology (F). Unfold into the next stable form (U). Return — not to zero, but to g+1. The next octave. The branch that touches ground and rises again.

The reintegration of knowledge is like the tree of life gives all fruit. Not one discipline. Not one application. Every fruit from the same root.

G-operator as helix · each cycle returns to g+1, not zero · base layer permanently imprinted
H and Li are not analogous — they occupy the same position in consecutive turns

Its true shape is a helix — each row a complete cycle of the G-operator, each new row returning not to zero but to g+1, the next octave, heavier and more complex but formally identical in position. Hydrogen and Lithium do not resemble each other. They are the same operator position in two consecutive turns of the helix. Mendeleev knew this. He organized by periodicity, not by rows. The table is a projection. The helix is the truth.
