The Square Lattice Is a Saddle, the Triangular Lattice a Minimum

The real Epstein sum $E(z,s)=\sum_{(m,n)\neq 0} y^{s}/|m+nz|^{2s}$ over the standard fundamental domain, for $s>1$.

Status. Every number on this page is a NUMERICAL value, computed in your browser from truncated lattice sums (with a continuum estimate for the tail) and cross-checked against an independent Fourier–Bessel evaluation in Python/mpmath. Nothing here is a proof, and nothing here proves or implies the Riemann Hypothesis. The one theorem quoted, about the global minimum, is a published result; it is cited with its exact range and was not re-proved here. Not human-reviewed.

1 · What is plotted

For $z=x+iy$ in the upper half-plane, $E(z,s)$ is the Epstein zeta function (sum of $|v|^{-2s}$ over nonzero vectors) of the lattice $\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}z$ rescaled to covolume 1. Its value depends only on the lattice up to rotation and scale, so it is enough to look at the fundamental domain $|x|\le\tfrac12,\ x^2+y^2\ge1$. Two lattices sit on its boundary: the square lattice $z=i$ and the triangular (hexagonal) lattice $z=\rho=e^{i\pi/3}=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$. The slider is limited to $s\ge1.5$ because the plain sum converges like $R^{2-2s}$ in the cutoff radius $R$ and is too slow close to $s=1$.

2 · The same identity at both points

Because $E(\cdot,s)$ is an eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplacian $\Delta=y^2(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2)$ with eigenvalue $s(s-1)$, the Euclidean Hessian trace obeys $y^2(f_{xx}+f_{yy})=s(s-1)f$ at every point, including $i$ ($y=1$) and $\rho$ ($y^2=\tfrac34$). The table in the panel above shows both sides (the panel uses $R=200$, so the two sides agree to about $10^{-5}$ relative; Section 4 shows the residual shrinking as $R$ grows). What differs between the two points is how that trace is split between the eigenvalues: at $i$ it splits unevenly with one negative eigenvalue (a saddle), at $\rho$ it splits equally by the three-fold symmetry (a minimum). Signs of Hessian eigenvalues at a critical point are coordinate-independent; their sizes are not (the $(x,y)$ chart is not isometric to the hyperbolic metric).

3 · Local view: a saddle and a minimum

At $i$ the level-0 set is a pair of crossing curves (a saddle: lower along $x$, higher along $y$). At $\rho$ there is no blue at all (a minimum), and the contours are nearly circles.

4 · Reproducing the numbers at a larger cutoff

Each row sums every lattice vector with $|m+nz|\le R$. "f raw" is the truncated sum; "f + tail" adds the continuum estimate $\pi y^{s-1}R^{2-2s}/(s-1)$ of the omitted vectors. Derivatives are the truncated sums only. The raw error shrinks like $R^{2-2s}$, so rows should stabilise as $R$ grows; at $s=2$ the displayed digits settle by $R\approx160$–$320$. $\lambda_{\pm}$ are the Hessian eigenvalues in $(x,y)$ coordinates; "trace residual" is $|y^2(f_{xx}+f_{yy})-s(s-1)f|/(s(s-1)f)$.

5 · The same point on the critical line (the addendum)

This is a different situation, and the two should not be confused. On the critical line $s=\tfrac12+it$ the function $f=E^*(z,s)$ is real-valued, and the same eigenvalue identity reads $y^2(f_{xx}+f_{yy})=s(s-1)f=-(\tfrac14+t^2)f$. At the height of a zero of $F(t)=f(i)$ we have $f(i)=0$, so the trace vanishes, and the Hessian at $i$ is trace-free, $\operatorname{diag}(b,-b)$. If $b\ne0$ (this is the nondegeneracy hypothesis $a(t_0)\ne0$ of the addendum) the nodal set near $i$ is a pair of orthogonal lines; a transversal reconnection as $t$ varies additionally needs $F'(t_0)\ne0$. Neither is established at every zero. The saddle at $i$ for real $s>1$ above is a different phenomenon: the trace is positive, $s(s-1)f(i)>0$, and $f(i)$ is not a zero. The sign pattern of the Hessian at $i$ and $\rho$ was checked on the slider grid $1.5\le s\le8$ (step $0.05$) with cutoff $R=300$ and showed no exceptions; this is a numerical scan, not a proof for all $s>1$.

6 · The global minimum: what is actually known

Define $Z_L(s)=\sum_{v\in L\setminus0}|v|^{-2s}$ for a lattice $L\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ of covolume 1; then $E(z,s)=Z_L(s)$ for $L=(\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}z)/\sqrt{y}$. The result below is quoted as read in the published sources, and was not re-proved here:

  • Epstein zeta, every real $s>0$. Among binary quadratic forms of fixed discriminant, the hexagonal form minimizes the Epstein zeta function for every real $s>0$: Rankin (1953), Cassels (1959), Ennola (1964) and Diananda (1964) proved this between them; Montgomery (Minimal theta functions, Glasgow Math. J. 30 (1988), 75–85) proved the stronger theta-function version for every $\alpha>0$, from which the Epstein inequality follows, and showed the minimizer is unique up to integral equivalence. (Source reading note: in the text of Montgomery's paper available to me the displayed inequalities were garbled; the statement and direction are confirmed by Bétermin–Faulhuber, arXiv:2007.15977, Thm 1.4. Montgomery normalizes by discriminant, not covolume; the factor convention was not independently resolved.)

7 · Where this connects

The square lattice is the point treated in the Chapter 14 addendum; the square and triangular lattices are the two cases compared in Chapter 29 (GS-2), where their lattice-sum coefficients differ but the bulk-subtracted values at $s=0$ and negative integers agree. This page does not claim that the saddle/minimum picture explains that agreement.
