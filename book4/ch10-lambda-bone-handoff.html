1 · The system

Chapter 10 with a loading parameter

r′ = (r−1)[2e−z − r(r+1)],  z′ = λ r2 − 2(r−1)2e−z,  θ′ = 1

At λ = 1 the radial field equals Chapter 10’s r(1−r2) + 2(r−1)e−z (sympy confirms). The parameter λ is an extension introduced in this work. It is not in the chapter. The identifications of r, z and λ with bone density, loading history and gravity (scenario A), or with stiffness, healing support and stability (scenario B), are choices and not derivations.

One structural fact limits everything below: r = 1 is invariant for every λ. The system therefore cannot generate bone loss. A loss has to be imposed as a starting state (r0, z0), and the model speaks only about recovery from it.

T rises as λ falls, so the point of no return moves toward baseline. At r0 = 0.90, a start with z0 between −0.503 and 0.109 recovers at 1 g but exits at λ = 0.05.

Recovery is state-dependent. From r0 = 0.932 at 1 g, the time for the deficit to halve is 4.07 model units at z0 = −0.654 (just above T = −0.674), 2.90 at −0.574, 1.18 at 0, 0.726 at 0.5, 0.546 at 1, 0.425 at 2 and 0.380 at 4. The z0 → ∞ limit is 0.374, not ln2/2 = 0.347, because r0 = 0.932 is not small and the full rate is r(r+1) − 2e−z, not 2. A start at z0 = −1 lies below T and never recovers. From z0 = 0 the deficit remaining at t = 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 is 1.000, 0.915, 0.614, against 0.607, 0.368, 0.135 for a single exponential of rate 2. That lag is the prediction a single exponential does not make.

Delay near the separatrix. Passage time grows as ln(1/δ)/μu + O(1). The fitted slope times μu is 0.993 at λ = 1 and 1.011 at λ = 0.2. μu falls as λ falls, so lower stability lengthens the delay.

Unit calibration is a choice, not a fit. Setting the far-field rate 2 per model unit equal to a fitted recovery rate K gives 1 model unit = 609 days (femoral neck, K = 0.00329 per day, from the 2006 NASA manuscript’s half-time of 211 days) or 280 days (pelvis, 97 days).

4 · What Chapter 10 says that the equations do not support

Contested and stale statements

The A1 fold. Section 8, the Remark after Proposition B.6, and the summary box call r* the Whitney A1 fold threshold. Only section 8 adds “where attractor and repeller branches merge.” The equilibria never merge (section 2), so that clause is refuted. Proposition B.6 makes r* the z = 0 crossing of the inner saddle’s stable manifold, which the script reproduces. Nothing in the chapter defines an A1 singularity for that object, so the fold identification is unsupported; whether it can be rescued is open.

The helix. Section 1 calls the helix a Reeb orbit. For α = dz − r2dθ the Reeb field is ∂/∂z, so a curve with θ′ = 1 is not a Reeb orbit. On the helix α(tangent) = λ − 1: Legendrian at λ = 1, neither Reeb nor Legendrian otherwise. The flow has α(X) = (λ−1)r2 − 2(r−1)2e−z, which at λ = 1 is −2(r−1)2e−z and is nonzero off r = 1, so the contact structure does not constrain the flow. The helix attracts only where z > 0 and only asymptotically, so it is non-compact.

Stale statements. The abstract and section 6 say every r(0) > 1 converges. The chapter’s own correction note says it does not, and the script confirms it: (7.5, 0) is driven to z → −∞, while (6.8, 0) converges. Section 3(ii), “z′ → 1 monotonically,” is false: from (3, 0), z′ peaks at 2.41 near t = 0.114 before settling to 1.

5 · Biology

What was read, and what was not

Read: Perren, Physical and Biological Aspects of Fracture Healing (lamellar bone breaks at 2% elongation, granulation tissue tolerates 100%, interfragmentary strain is inversely proportional to the gap; the text read gives no percentage for fibrous tissue or cartilage). The nonunion definitions and risk factors from a Wikipedia summary: the FDA definition is a fracture at least 9 months old with no radiographic progress in the last 3 months; a delayed union is a nonunion that goes on to heal. LeBlanc and colleagues (NASA NTRS 20100030610): regional density loss of 1–1.6% per month on Mir, and recovery that “does occur, although slowly and sometimes incompletely.” The WHO T-score categories from a table in a BMC paper: normal T ≥ −1.0, osteopenia between −2.5 and −1.0, osteoporosis T ≤ −2.5. Earlier in the project: the 2006 NASA manuscript (half-times 97 to 255 days by site), the 2022 Gabel abstract, a 2012 NASA article, and the mechanostat page.

Not read: Claes and Heigele 1999 (the PubMed page returned a verification screen, so no threshold is quoted); the equations of Komarova et al. 2003 (the repository page had no equations); Wolff’s law and RANKL/OPG (not searched); critical-size defects (not searched). No public fracture-healing dataset was confirmed. The NASA Life Sciences Portal exists as a catalog entry; its access terms were not read.

None has been tested against data.

6 · Limits and first-run failures

What to check hardest

Finite windows

Separatrices are traced backward from the saddle with tmax = 40000 and stop at r = 0.9999, r = 10−3 or z = 14. Half-times are measured to the first crossing of half the initial deficit. The r = 0 exit is non-physical.

First-run failures, kept

Four checks failed on the first run and none was a model error. A float-equality test on the cubic. The λ = 0.2 delay slope was 3.1% off because linear interpolation on a grid gave the start point an error near 10−8; it was fixed by using the solver’s dense output and not by loosening the tolerance. My expected z0 → ∞ half-time used the linear rate 2 although r0 = 0.932 is not small. And z0 = −1 lies below the separatrix, so it has no half-time.

An auditor should independently check: that the stable-manifold branch with r increasing is the right one at every λ used; the proof sketch for the delay law; the λ = 0 treatment; and that the 3×10−13 agreement with the certified r* is not an artefact of the shared integrator settings (DOP853, rtol 10−13, atol 10−15), by rerunning with a different method.
