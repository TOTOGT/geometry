Everything below is public and can be checked. I list what each piece does not show next to what it does, so you do not have to find the limits yourself.

1

The Response Gap

An open-access working paper on the governance of automated credit liquidation.

Shows: the interval between a system detecting a shortfall and taking an irreversible action treated as an auditable governance object, four proposed controls, and every claim tagged as documented, modeled, prospective or open.

Does not show: peer review. It is a working paper and is not peer-reviewed. It is about credit systems, not enterprise data.

2

A Policy Brief on the Response Gap

The same paper read as an analyst would: each proposed control as a requirement, a test and a status, with one control run on the page.

Shows: turning a policy proposal into testable controls, with a worked check whose results were derived by hand first.

Does not show: use on a real system. The example numbers are invented, and the four tests are proposals made in the chapter.

3

The Forced Urgency Gap

The base working paper under the other two: why selling at a loss is not identified as a preference, and why small liquidity shortfalls cascade. It is Working Paper 32 in Book 6 of this series.

Shows: urgency defined as the shadow price on a liquidity constraint, which moves the diagnosis from the seller's preferences to the seller's balance sheet. That is the premise the response-gap controls rest on.

Does not show: peer review. It is a working paper published on this site and is not peer-reviewed.

4

Algorithmic Urgency

A governance, ethical, legal and social implications paper on AI credit and an invisible wealth transfer, published openly on this site.

Shows: the same argument developed in the AI-credit setting, with each claim tagged by its status.

Does not show: peer review, or any test of its predictions on a deployed model.

5

How the pages are checked

Each page has a verification script, for example the one for the policy brief, and every change is recorded in a dated audit log. Each script is written before the page and is tested against negative controls, known-bad copies it must reject, to show it can fail.

Shows: version control, change records, and a habit of testing the test.

Does not show: that every claim on a page is true. The scripts check structure and stated facts, not whether a conclusion is right.

What this page does not show

This is not data-privacy or data-protection experience. My formal experience is in records and compliance at schools and in credit governance at a bank, and I have not held a dedicated data-governance role. The work here is evidence of how I design and check governance material, and it is offered as that.
