§3   What the thing actually is

The gallery slate this paper floors needs one place to state the mathematics, so that four chapters cite it rather than restating it four times. What follows is description, not result. OPEN

The organising image is older than the program and belongs to a man who already has a chapter here. In 1940, writing from a prison in Rouen to his sister Simone, André Weil described an analogy in three columns: number fields, function fields over a finite field, and Riemann surfaces — three settings in which the same statements keep appearing in different dialects. The Langlands program has three strands for the same reason Weil's letter has three columns.

Strand one — arithmetic. For a global field F and a reductive group G over it, a conjectural correspondence between automorphic representations of G(AF) and Galois-side parameters valued in the dual group LG — a construction Langlands introduced. Class field theory is the abelian case, G = GL(1). Modularity of elliptic curves over Q — Wiles, and Taylor–Wiles, 1995 — is a case of GL(2).

Strand two — function fields. Where F is the function field of a curve over a finite field. Drinfeld proved the GL(2) case in 1983; Drinfeld and Gérard Laumon formulated the general GL(n) geometric statement in 1987; Laurent Lafforgue proved GL(n), for which he took a Fields Medal in 2002. The trace-formula machinery the arithmetic strand needs rested on a statement Langlands and Shelstad had conjectured and could not prove — the fundamental lemma — which Ngô Bảo Châu proved, Fields Medal 2010.

Strand three — geometric. Over a curve in characteristic zero, a correspondence between sheaves on the moduli of G-bundles and the moduli of LG local systems; the formulation is Beilinson and Drinfeld's. It was proved in 2024 by a nine-person team led by Dennis Gaitsgory and Sam Raskin — with Dima Arinkin, Nick Rozenblyum, Joakim Færgeman, Dario Beraldo, Lin Chen, Justin Campbell and Kevin Lin — in five papers. Gaitsgory took the 2025 Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics for it and Raskin a New Horizons Prize. Sources disagree on the length: Quanta reports more than 800 pages, Wikipedia more than 1,000. This paper does not pick one. VALUE

One detail belongs in this corpus more than the rest of it does. Beilinson and Drinfeld's conjecture could not be proved as stated. Quanta's account is that their best hope had been a bit too simplistic about how the relationships… should work, and that Gaitsgory and Arinkin made it into a precise conjecture in 2012 — twenty-five years after the formulation and twelve before the proof.

A conjecture whose statement had to be repaired before it could be true is the same object this project has now caught four times in its own Lean: a name that promised more than its statement said. The difference is only that theirs was repaired in public, by the people who would go on to prove the repaired version. That is the standard, not an anecdote. OPEN

§4   What this paper does not license

WP-82's correction of 2026-09-11 replaced rung 32's Langlands seed with the vertex-algebra route for Volume XV — Feigin–Frenkel, z(ĝ) ≅ W(Lg) — on the grounds that the W-algebra path was the one the corpus could reach. docs/part-ii-source-wants.md still carries Volume XV as The W-algebra route — BRIDGE. That judgement stands and this paper does not reverse it. The w-algebra row above, 0 → 7, is that judgement being vindicated.

So a Langlands slate in Book 7 goes past what WP-82 judged reachable, and needs a reason that is not the material is adjacent. The reason is that Book 7 is a gallery: its unit is attribution, and its established voice is naming what the corpus does not have. ch-weil is in the gallery, in its own words, for the half of the sentence about why his proof does not cross over. ch-grothendieck names K-theory as a rung this corpus has been standing on without having built. A chapter in that voice attributes mathematics without claiming the series reaches it.

ASSUME The distinction between attributing a rung and occupying it is a choice about what a gallery chapter is for, not a finding. It is recorded here so that a later reader can reject it in one place rather than in four chapters. The failure mode it is meant to prevent is the one this series reversed in V5 of the CATGT paper, where seven domains were claimed to share one invariant on the strength of a shared functional form. The Langlands program is described as a unification; this corpus is also described as one; those two facts are not a bridge between them.

§5   What the slate would be

Four chapters, in the gallery's existing format, each with a verify.py checking what is actually checkable. Named here so the slate is a commitment with a shape rather than an open-ended expansion:

  • ch-langlands — b. 6 October 1936, New Westminster, British Columbia. Princeton 1960–67, Yale 1967–72, IAS 1972–2007. Wolf Prize 1995/96 shared with Wiles; Shaw Prize 2007 with Richard Taylor; Abel Prize 2018. The seventeen-page letter of January 1967 was addressed to Weil and offered him a waste basket. The letter's recipient has had a chapter in this gallery longer than its sender has.
  • ch-drinfeld-beilinson — the geometric formulation, and the 2012 repair of its statement. Adjacent to ch-feigin, which already carries the vertex-algebra floor under it.
  • ch-the-provers — Laurent Lafforgue, Ngô Bảo Châu, Gaitsgory and Raskin. The closed cases, which is where a verify.py has something concrete to check.
  • ch-frenkel — extending ch-feigin rather than opening a thread. Vincent Lafforgue's description of the preparatory decades as a rising sea, in Grothendieck's sense, points at ch-grothendieck; the cross-link is already earned.

book6/wp130-verify.py recomputes every number in §1 and the composition table in §2 at the commit they name, and fails if either drifts. It also asserts the zero rows, which are the rows most likely to stop being true — and which this slate is intended to falsify.
