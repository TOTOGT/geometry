Instruments · a measurement of this corpus, by this corpus
The corpus has been good at one half of its own discipline. Every producing script recomputes the numbers in the prose beside it and fails loudly rather than rounding to agreement. What almost none of them do is record what they did not check — and the ratio is 23.4 to one.

The most thoroughly examined material has the least record of what its examination left alone. VERIFIED

2 · Why this is the same error as a near-integer

A script that reports only passes makes a true statement and an unearned one in the same breath. “All checks passed” is true. It says nothing whatever about the checks that were not written, and the reader has no way to tell those two things apart.

The same shape, twice in one afternoon Chapter R's $1/\pi$ work asserted $g_{58}^{12} = 19601$, then $19602$. The value is $19601.99999489847\ldots$ — near both, equal to neither. A tolerance chosen after seeing the number passes either assertion.

A near-integer asserted as an integer, and a check-list offered as a coverage claim, are the same move: a claim about the part you did not look at.

3 · What it costs

The narrow cost is that no reader can size the corpus's own uncertainty. The larger one is structural.

A gap list is a request. WP-124's companion in the Spine repository records, in machine-readable form, that it needs Neurospine 2026;23(3):526–35 and one sentence from its methods — that is a corpus naming its next input in a form a person or a fetcher can act on. Five hundred and fifteen assertions of what is known against twenty-two records of what is not is an instrument with almost nothing to ask for.

Whatever is built on top of these scripts later — a register, an agent, a loop — reads the gaps, not the passes. The passes are already spent.

WP-125 improves the statistic it reports. A reader shown only the HEAD column could not see by how much, which is the whole reason the baseline is printed beside it.

And the rule earned its keep immediately The first run of wp125-verify.py reported four fewer scripts at HEAD than at a commit HEAD postdates. Scripts do not vanish. The working-tree walk used one os.listdir per book while the baseline side used git ls-tree -r, which recurses into subdirectories. Nothing but the self-count would have surfaced it: every individual number looked plausible, and only the comparison was impossible.

tools/harvest.py had the same defect and was disagreeing with this script about the same corpus. Both now walk recursively.
And then a third, found the same way With the walks agreed, the two instruments still disagreed — 31 gaps against 21, on an identical tree. This script's gap counter added two overlapping patterns without deduplicating, and an entry inside a gaps = [...] list usually matches the standalone-tuple pattern as well. The headline ratio in the first draft of this paper was wrong: 16.6 where it should have read 23.4.

Nothing internal to either script could have caught that. Both ran clean, both exited zero, and each was individually plausible. Only running two instruments over one corpus and insisting they agree found it. They now agree to the entry, per script.

5 · What this does not say

  1. Three bugs in this paper's own instruments were found while writing it — a non-recursive walk, a double-counted gap, and a headline ratio wrong by 40%. All three were caught by comparison, none by inspection. That is evidence for the method and a caution about every number above. OPEN
  2. The detector recognises two syntactic shapes, not the idea of a gap. A script that discusses its limits in prose without a gaps list reads as zero. The count is a floor and the ratio an upper bound. OPEN
  3. “Named check” means a call to check() — a convention, not a law. Scripts using bare assert are undercounted.
  4. No claim is made that any particular script should have gaps. Some results are complete. This measures a habit across a corpus; it does not convict a file.
  5. The baseline is one commit, not a history. Whether the ratio has been worsening or improving over the corpus's life is unmeasured. OPEN
