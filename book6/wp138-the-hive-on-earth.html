Maxley

The Hive on Earth. An urban, family-inclusive city of about 100,000, built in about five years and linked across four New Jersey counties. A sketch to be tested, not a plan.

WP138 · 2026-10-26 · not peer-reviewed

What this is, and what it is not
A concept, not a nomination and not a prediction of where anything will be built. The Pentagon’s site-selection memo was not read by me (the Defense Department’s file server refused my fetch and I did not work around it), so the competition’s criteria, dates and rules for who may nominate are unknown here. Every number below is either sourced, or my arithmetic on stated assumptions, and is labelled. Not peer-reviewed. Companion script: book6/wp138-verify.py.

§1 · The prompt

On 30 September 2026 the Secretary of War signed a memo launching a nationwide competition to site the “Next Great American Base”: a “highly hardened joint military installation within the Homeland,” off the commercial grid, with its own power, autonomous systems and air defenses (reported by National Defense Magazine and My Base Guide). The second report gives 15,000 to 25,000 troops, from the Secretary’s speech at Quantico; that figure is secondary reporting and I have not seen the speech. The selection plan was due 14 October and, as of the 19 October report, was not public. The memo, as reported, names no site, cost or opening date.

The same report quotes a defense-communities executive: housing, child care, schools and health care are connected; communities should be in the planning from the start; and the department “may want to consider building a few smaller, more geographically dispersed installations.” Everything on this page follows from taking that seriously.

§2 · The thesis

  1. Urban, not underground. The threats named are drones, cyberattack and loss of the grid. Meeting them needs dispersal, independent power and protected cores, not burial; and families need schools and clinics that do not go underground well.
  2. Families are in the plan from day one. A base of this size is a small city, so it is sized as one (§3).
  3. About 100,000 people, built in about five years. The only precedent for a state-built capital at that pace is Brasília (§6), which is a warning as much as a model.
  4. Linked across four counties so that no single town absorbs the whole load (§4, §5).
  5. It becomes a hub. People and jobs gather around it, so 100,000 is a seed, not a cap.

§3 · Sizing the people

Newark as the core

Newark had an estimated 323,808 residents in July 2025 on 24.14 square miles (about 12,900 per square mile), with 24.4% of units owner-occupied and median rent $1,392 (Census). A Rutgers inventory found about 27 acres of city-owned vacant land suited to affordable housing, 21 for commercial use and 17 for green infrastructure (Rutgers CLiME), about 65 acres. At Newark’s density the 32,000 to 54,000 military residents need roughly 2.5 to 4.2 square miles and 12,000 to 21,000 households (at 2.62 per household). If the other counties take half, about 6,000 to 10,000 units fall in Newark: 95 to 158 units per acre on those 65 acres. That is tower density, and city-owned vacant land alone would not be enough.

§5 · A linked schematic

Reading the schematic: a hardened Newark core with dispersal outward. The labels on the outer districts are guesses from density alone (Passaic, at 2,818 people per square mile, is less dense than Essex at 6,850; those are the only two counties I have density figures for), not findings. The fifth hexagon is deliberately open. The point of the picture is the structure: hexagonal districts, a core, and links.

§6 · What comes from the moon habitat

The lunar page, WP136, Living in a Moon Habitat, designs for a small crew in a closed station. Two things carry over; the rest does not.

Carries over
Hexagonal districts. Wall or perimeter per unit area, from hand arithmetic (perimeter / sqrt(area)): hexagon 3.722, square 4.000, triangle 4.559. For equal cells the hexagon needs the least boundary to fence, patrol or service. A real city is not made of equal cells, so this is a heuristic, not a layout.

Sleep and noise rules, as hypotheses. Dark, quiet sleeping quarters, no beeping indicator lights, emergency-only wake signals and shared night watches were written for a lunar crew and are untested there; for dense family towers and barracks with shift workers they would need adapting.
Does not carry over
Radiation shielding, vacuum, closed life support and the lunar day-night cycle have no counterpart here. The Earth threats are drones, cyberattack and grid loss. The lunar claims that were cut or flagged in the audit (a crew-dose prediction and a diamond-hardness figure) are not brought over.

§7 · Brasília, and land

Brasília was built in 41 months, from 1956 to its inauguration on 21 April 1960, with residential areas designed for 500,000 people, a figure reached by 1970; the construction camps became the basis of the satellite cities, 27 of which surround it today, and the project is generally regarded as one of the most expensive in the world (History of Brasília). Three lessons for this sketch: house the construction workforce on purpose, plan the periphery from the first day, and control cost. The Congressional Research Service range quoted in the My Base Guide report, five to seven years or more for military construction, makes five years the optimistic end, and a city also needs teachers and clinicians, who arrive on a different clock from the buildings.

New payroll and new residents raise nearby land values; speculation follows; residents and workers can be priced out. Tools to consider: public or land-trust ownership with leases, reserved workforce housing, and a share of the value the base itself creates. How Brasília handled land tenure is not checked. Buying land near a likely site before it is announced invites conflict-of-interest problems even for a private concept, so the clean version is to assemble public land in the open and publish the plan.
