The Fold Is the Folding Frequency

A proposed correspondence between the operator chain and sampling, one computed consequence, and one falsifier that costs an afternoon
Computed2026-08-29

StatusOpen · not deposited

On twelve sites, the only non-constant harmonic compatible with six-fold rotational symmetry is the folding frequency itself. If the operator chain is a sampling operation — and this paper argues the correspondence is exact rather than analogical — then the hexagon does not merely appear near the fold. It is the fold, in both senses of the word at once.

Under revision · 2026-08-29, same day as first draft

Four sections are being rewritten, and the reasons are recorded here rather than applied silently.

§3 overstates and does not survive its own objection. It says aliasing is "the normal form of a Whitney A₁ fold". It is not. Whitney A₁ is x ↦ x², a smooth map with vanishing derivative at an isolated point; k ↦ N−k on ℤ/12 is a reflection on a finite set with no smooth structure and no normal form to be in. What is true is weaker: both realise a quotient by an involution with isolated fixed points, and x ↦ x² is the smooth model of ℝ/±. §3 also conflates the two sides of the transform — Whitney's fold is loss of injectivity on the state space, aliasing is an identification on the dual.

§4 understates. The coincidence is not structural, it is a characterisation of twelve. For six-fold symmetry on N sites the non-DC fixed modes are {6, 12, …} ∩ [1, N/2], and that set equals {N/2} if and only if N = 12 — verified algebraically and numerically for N = 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 60. This makes Fin 12 a characterisation rather than an arbitrary choice, and it is a stronger result than the one §4 states.

§2's assumption relocates rather than resolves. C as Poincaré sampling of a flow is lossless — the section-to-section map is the time-π/6 flow map on a transversal, a diffeomorphism — so C cannot be where aliasing happens. F is where the flow ceases to be a diffeomorphism, and aliasing is F's dual-side signature. But the revised §2 then assumes PhaseVector := Fin 12 → ℝ is the twelve-section representation of the LAW3M flow, which is a new assumption and must carry its own tag.

§6 gains a consequence. Twelve sections per revolution resolve transverse frequencies to 6 per revolution. The μ_max = −2 contraction is non-oscillatory and cannot alias, but a Neimark–Sacker bifurcation introduces a second incommensurate frequency — and if it exceeds 6 per revolution, Fin 12 folds a real frequency onto a false one and the discrete model fails outright. WP-79 already tags NS as the falsifiable branch. §5 and its falsifier stand unchanged.

§1

Provenance, stated plainly

This did not arrive as mathematics. It arrived as a physical observation and a night of bad sleep, and the honest record of that belongs in the paper rather than outside it.

The observation: attempting to arrange neodymium spheres into a spiral, they settle instead into a hexagon. The author reported it in conversation on 2026-08-29, with the note that dynamic analysis and the Fourier transform were what the night had left owing. The direction — that Fourier belongs underneath the operator chain rather than beside it at some rung of an external ladder — is the author's. The correspondence table in §2 and the computation in §4 were produced in response, and the computation is the only part of this paper that has been checked. OPEN

§2

The correspondence

If the chain is a Fourier operation, its four operators should be signal operations, not resemble them:

OperatorSignal operationActing on
Csampling — restriction to the cyclecontinuous θ onto N sites
Kthe gate: a filtermultiplication by a multiplier in the dual
Fthe fold: aliasing about the folding frequencyidentification of k with N−k
Uthe unfolding: reconstructioninverse transform from samples

Two of these are not interpretations. In signal processing a gate is a filter and a fold is what spectra do about the Nyquist frequency; the vocabulary the series has used for three years is the vocabulary of the field it may have been doing all along. ASSUME

§3

The objection, named before it is answered

Two folds, one word

The fold of catastrophe theory — Whitney A₁, the map x ↦ x² — and the fold of sampling theory — spectra reflecting about N/2 — are different technical terms that share an English word. A correspondence built on that coincidence would be wordplay, and this corpus has a standing record of claims that read correct and rest on nothing.

The answer is that they are the same map. Aliasing identifies mode k with mode N−k: a two-to-one identification with a single critical point at N/2, on either side of which the map is injective and orientation-reversing. That is the normal form of a Whitney A₁ fold, on the frequency line, with the fold locus at the folding frequency. The folding frequency is a fold point in the Whitney sense. MODEL

§4

The computation

The series' phase space is PhaseVector := Fin 12 → ℝ, so N = 12 and the folding frequency is k = N/2 = 6. Six-fold rotational symmetry acts by rotation through two sites. A mode k is fixed by that rotation exactly when 2k ≡ 0 (mod 12).

{ k ∈ {0,…,6} : 2k ≡ 0 (mod 12) } = {0, 6}

DC, and one other: k = 6, the folding frequency, and nothing else. Verified numerically by projecting a random real vector onto the invariant subspace and taking the real DFT; every mode but 0 and 6 vanishes to machine precision. DATA

import numpy as np N = 12; v = np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(N) p = np.mean([np.roll(v, 2*j) for j in range(6)], axis=0) # project onto C6-invariants X = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(p)) print([k for k, m in enumerate(X) if m > 1e-9]) # -> [0, 6]
The statement

On twelve sites, the hexagonal mode and the aliasing fold are the same mode. A six-fold symmetric arrangement of twelve phase slots carries no harmonic content except at the frequency where the sampling folds. Under §2's correspondence this says the hexagon is not a structure the chain produces at the fold — it is the fold, expressed in the sample domain.

§5

A refinement the check forced

Under the full dihedral group the answer depends on the mirror axis, which the first computation did not anticipate:

Symmetry imposedSurviving modes
C₆ — rotation by two sites only{0, 6}
D₆ — mirror through a site{0, 6}
D₆ — mirror through a bond{0}

Reflect a hexagon about an axis through a vertex and the folding mode survives; reflect about an axis through an edge midpoint and it is annihilated. This is not a technicality of convention. It is the difference between a hexagon centred on a sphere and a hexagon centred on a gap, and it is decidable by hand with the magnets that generated the question. DATA

§6

What this is not

  • The correspondence in §2 is not derived. It is proposed. Deriving it requires definitions of C, K, F and U precise enough to compare with sampling, filtering, aliasing and reconstruction — and Volume XIII chapter 2 records that the series does not yet have them. This paper is therefore downstream of an open question. OPEN
  • The computation is about ℤ/12, not about the corpus. That 2k ≡ 0 (mod 12) has solutions {0,6} is elementary and independent of anything here. What is claimed is the reading, and the reading rests on §2.
  • The mathematics is not new. Aliasing, Nyquist folding and characters of ℤ/12 are standard. If there is a contribution it is the identification, and identifications are cheap until they predict something that could have come out otherwise.
  • One prediction that could. §5 says the vertex-centred and bond-centred hexagons differ in whether they carry the folding mode. If the spheres show no such distinction, §2 is in trouble. That is the falsifier, it costs an afternoon, and it has not been run.
