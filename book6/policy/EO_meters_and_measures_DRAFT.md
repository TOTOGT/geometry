## EXECUTIVE ORDER

### Meters and Measures for Metered Digital Services

By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the laws of
the United States of America, including the Federal Property and Administrative
Services Act [40 U.S.C. ch. 1 et seq. — *counsel to confirm*] and section 301 of
title 3, United States Code, it is hereby ordered as follows:

---

### Section 1. Policy

For as long as the Republic has existed, a person who sells by quantity has been
required to measure honestly and to show the measure. A merchant may set any
price; the merchant may not conceal the scale. That principle is administered
today through the weights and measures system, and it is among the least
controversial functions of government.

A growing class of services is sold by consumption but delivered without a
meter. A subscriber pays a recurring fee for an undisclosed quantity of an
undefined unit, cannot observe how much remains, and learns of the limit only
upon reaching it — frequently in the middle of work that is then lost. Because
the provider must measure the quantity in order to enforce the limit, the
measurement already exists; it is simply withheld from the party being measured.

This concealment imposes a real and avoidable cost. A subscriber who cannot see
remaining capacity cannot plan around it, and the only available hedge is to
purchase duplicate subscriptions — paying two or three times for a single
workload. That cost falls on buyers, including small businesses, sole
proprietors, and Federal agencies, while the resulting revenue accrues to
providers. A provider therefore has no commercial incentive to install a gauge.

It is the policy of the United States that when the Government purchases a
service sold by measured use, it shall receive the measure. This order concerns
the accuracy and disclosure of measurement only. **Nothing in this order
regulates the design, capability, content, or price of any service.**

---

### Section 2. Definitions

(a) **"Metered digital service"** means a digital service furnished for a
recurring or prepaid fee that limits the quantity a subscriber may use within a
defined period, or that varies charges according to quantity used.

(b) **"Usage unit"** means the quantity in which a provider measures a
subscriber's use for purposes of applying a limit or computing a charge.

(c) **"Enforced measure"** means the quantity computed by the provider and
actually used to apply a limit or compute a charge.

(d) **"Displayed measure"** means the quantity made available to the subscriber
under section 3(b).

(e) **"Covered contract"** means a contract, order, or agreement of an executive
agency for a metered digital service, including purchases through the Multiple
Award Schedule and other Governmentwide vehicles.

---

### Section 3. Metering Disclosure Requirements

Executive agencies shall require, as a condition of a covered contract, that the
provider:

**(a) Declare the unit.** Publish a plain-language description of the usage unit
and of the actions that consume it, available before purchase and throughout the
term. Where usage depends on more than one factor, publish the conversion among
them.

**(b) Display the measure.** Make available to the subscriber, at no additional
charge and through the interface by which the service is ordinarily accessed, in
both human-readable and machine-readable form: quantity consumed in the current
period; the applicable limit; and the time remaining until the limit resets.

**(c) Signal before the limit binds.** Provide notice before use reaches the
applicable limit, at a threshold sufficient to permit the subscriber to conclude
or transfer work in progress. Notice at the moment the limit binds does not
satisfy this subsection.

**(d) Give notice of changes.** Provide notice to affected subscribers before any
change to the usage unit, the applicable limit, or the method of accounting takes
effect. Renaming or re-partitioning a usage unit does not restart or extinguish
any obligation under this section.

**(e) Furnish a reconcilable record.** On request, furnish a record of usage for
any period within the preceding twelve months in sufficient detail to permit
verification of the quantity charged or limited.

**(f) Correspondence of measures.** Certify annually, by an officer of the
provider, that the displayed measure corresponds to the enforced measure.

**(g) Safe harbour.** A provider that satisfies subsection (a) and exposes
subsection (b) through a documented application programming interface shall be
presumed to satisfy subsections (c) through (e) to the extent that interface
supports them.

---

### Section 4. Limits Not Disclosed Are Not Enforceable Against the Government

Agencies shall include in covered contracts a term providing that a provider may
not throttle, suspend, degrade, or charge for use in excess of a limit unless
the disclosures required by section 3 were furnished with respect to that limit.
Agencies shall further provide that:

(a) the Government shall not be required to procure multiple subscriptions for a
single user or workload in order to obtain capacity that could be furnished and
disclosed under a single subscription; and

(b) prepaid or committed capacity shall not expire or be forfeited absent clear
prior disclosure of the terms of expiration.

---

### Section 5. Technical Standard

(a) Within 180 days, the Secretary of Commerce, acting through the Director of
the National Institute of Standards and Technology, shall publish technical
guidance for the disclosure and verification of digital usage measurement,
drawing upon the Institute's existing responsibilities for weights and measures.

(b) The guidance shall address, at minimum: the form of the machine-readable
disclosure under section 3(b); the content of a reconcilable record under section
3(e); and a procedure for determining whether the displayed measure corresponds
to the enforced measure. The verification procedure shall be limited to that
correspondence and **shall not require disclosure of model architecture,
training data, source code, or infrastructure cost.**

(c) The guidance shall be technology-neutral. It shall not prescribe a particular
usage unit.

---

### Section 6. Acquisition Implementation

(a) Within 120 days, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall
issue guidance to agencies implementing sections 3 and 4, including model
contract language and a schedule for incorporation at the next renewal or
modification of covered contracts.

(b) Within 270 days, the Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council shall consider
appropriate amendments to the Federal Acquisition Regulation.

(c) The Administrator of General Services shall incorporate the requirements into
Governmentwide acquisition vehicles.

(d) Agency heads may waive a requirement of section 3 for a specific procurement
upon a written determination that no compliant provider can meet a critical
mission need. Waivers shall be time-limited, shall not exceed one year, and shall
be reported to the Director of the Office of Management and Budget, who shall
publish an annual summary.

---

### Section 7. Small Business and Consumer Effects

(a) Within 270 days, the Administrator of the Small Business Administration, in
consultation with the Chair of the Federal Trade Commission, shall report on the
effect of undisclosed usage limits on small businesses and sole proprietors,
including the prevalence of duplicate subscription purchasing undertaken to
compensate for the absence of usage disclosure.

(b) Nothing in this order limits the authority of the Federal Trade Commission
under section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act, or of any State, with
respect to unfair or deceptive acts or practices.

---

### Section 8. Scope and Construction

(a) This order applies to the Government as a purchaser. It does not, of itself,
impose requirements on transactions to which the Government is not a party.

(b) Nothing in this order shall be construed to authorize regulation of the
content, design, capability, or price of any digital service, or to require the
disclosure of trade secrets or confidential commercial information beyond the
correspondence described in section 5(b).

(c) Nothing in this order shall be construed to impair or otherwise affect the
authority granted by law to an executive department or agency, or the functions
of the Director of the Office of Management and Budget relating to budgetary,
administrative, or legislative proposals.

(d) This order shall be implemented consistent with applicable law and subject to
the availability of appropriations.

(e) This order is not intended to, and does not, create any right or benefit,
substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity by any party against
the United States, its departments, agencies, or entities, its officers,
employees, or agents, or any other person.

## Drafting notes (not part of the order)

**Why an EO and not a bill.** The Government buys these services at scale. As a
purchaser it need not assert regulatory jurisdiction, which avoids preemption,
dormant-commerce, and First Amendment arguments entirely. Federal procurement has
repeatedly established commercial norms this way — the standard becomes general
because compliance is easier to build once than to maintain in two versions.

**The load-bearing section is 4, not 3.** Disclosure requirements without a
consequence are frequently absorbed as paperwork. Making an undisclosed limit
*unenforceable* removes the benefit of concealment rather than taxing it, and is
self-executing — no agency action required, and the provider's own counsel prices
the risk on day one.

**Section 5(b) is the political survival clause.** The single audited question —
does the displayed measure equal the enforced measure? — is mechanically checkable
and reveals nothing proprietary. Every attempt to widen it should be resisted; it
is what keeps this metrology rather than AI regulation.

**Deliberately absent:** any specified unit (freezes technology, advantages
incumbents), any new agency or licensure gate (slow, capturable), any capability
or content provision (invites the fight this proposal does not need), and any
price control.

**Open items for counsel.** `[OPEN]` Precise authority citation in the preamble ·
whether FAR amendment should be directed rather than "considered" · waiver
duration · whether section 7(a) should carry a data-collection instrument ·
interaction with existing agency AI acquisition guidance.

**Evidence still needed.** The prevalence of duplicate-subscription purchasing —
section 7(a) is drafted to generate exactly this figure, which is the number any
legislator or reviewer will ask for first, and which does not presently exist in
public data. `[OPEN]`
