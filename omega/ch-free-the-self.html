Movement IV · Show the Way
The ginga · the cleared circle · the guard that is not fear

Free the Self from the Self

Attitude as a method, and why the open hand is the strong one

The base movement of capoeira is not a strike. It is a triangle you keep walking, and everything the game can do is generated out of it.

Under the cajueiro
came the jacu —
tried to take me,
met my heel.

Broken air.
Cracked like a whip.
He opened his mouth,
never saw the foot —

only his gun,
flying.

Pablo Nogueira Grossi

The Cleared Space

A roda is a circle that somebody cleared. That is the first fact about it and the one most easily missed: before there is any play there is an act of bounding, a decision about where the edge runs, and the ring of people standing on that edge and singing is the boundary. Nobody plays capoeira in an open field. The game exists because a limit was drawn around it.

Which is this book’s thesis wearing a body. A generative system is as much a boundary as an engine — Pāṇini’s grammar generates every well-formed Sanskrit sentence and generates no others, and the second clause is the whole idea. The roda is that, done with people. The constraint is not what stops the play. The constraint is what there is to play inside.

The Ginga

The base movement is a triangle. You stand at the forward point; a foot slides back along the diagonal, and as it goes the opposite arm comes up across the face, and the other hand stays low and ready. Then it reverses, and reverses again, and does not stop for as long as the game lasts. [MODEL] Nothing in it is an attack. Every attack the game contains is generated out of it, and — the part that matters — from any given stance certain strikes are simply not available. Which ones depends entirely on which foot went back. Order first, then the fold. The same non-commutation, carried in the hips.

The author slides the left foot. That is a fact about one body and not about the art, and this book has been careful all week about which is which.

What the ginga does to a person is stranger than what it does to a fight. It runs some forty times a minute and it cannot be run by deciding. A practitioner who thinks each transition is already too late; the movement has to be handed over to the body entirely, and the mind that keeps trying to hold it is the thing that has to go. Not silenced. Superseded — given nothing left to do.

The Self That Was Generated

Here is the claim this chapter is for. [MODEL] If a self is what a rule set produces under constraint — a language, a family, a century, a body, a set of accidents arriving in an order nobody chose — then it is not an essence. It is a trajectory. It went where the valley took it, the way water does, and it could not have gone elsewhere without a different valley.

The consequence is not that the self is unreal or that nothing matters. It is narrower and more useful: there is nothing there that needs defending. Freedom is not escaping the constraint — that offer was never available, and every philosophy that promised it has been selling. Freedom is dropping the defence of the particular self the constraint produced. Water does not free itself by refusing to run downhill. It frees itself by not insisting it is this water.

Which is what the witnesses did, and it is why their behaviour looked so unremarkable from outside. At the moment when despair was the fully rational response, each of them went back to work. Levi wrote a report. Ramos wrote a memoir. Dos Anjos wrote the poems. That is not courage as a feeling. It is the self standing out of the way of the work, which is a thing a body can learn to do and a mood is not.

Three Things the Self Is Not

The claim is easy to state and hard to hold, so it is worth taking apart. Three descriptions of the self do most of the defending, and each fails in a different place.

The self as story. A narrative with a protagonist, a consistent character, and an arc that must be honoured. The trouble is that the story is written backwards. Nobody chose the language they think in, the century they arrived in, the body they were issued, or the first thousand facts they took to be obvious. A narrative assembled after the fact from constraints that were never selected is a description of a course, not evidence of a driver.

The self as property. Something owned, and therefore something that can be taken — which is why insult wounds and why a threat to reputation registers in the body like a threat to the body. But an owner has to be separable from the thing owned, and here there is nothing standing behind the possession to hold it. The proprietor and the estate are the same object, which is a strange arrangement to spend a life defending.

The self as debt. The most respectable of the three and the most corrosive: a ledger of what was promised, what was wasted, who was disappointed, what should have been done by now. This one is hardest to release, because letting it go feels exactly like absolving yourself. It is not the same act. A debt says I owe because of what I am. Responsibility says this needs doing and I am here. The second survives the release of the first, and works better without it, because it is not spending anything on the accounting.

The Objection That Has to Be Answered

Every argument of this kind arrives at the same doorway, and most of them walk through it and never come back: if the self is a trajectory and nothing needs defending, why do anything at all?

It is a serious objection, and quietism is a real destination — there is a long history of release-from-self doctrines arriving at withdrawal from the world, and some of them meant to. This book is arguing the opposite and owes the distinction plainly.

[MODEL] What drops is the defence, not the agency. Look at what the defence actually consumes. Am I the right person to do this. Do I have standing. Will I look foolish. What if it fails and the failure is mine. Will anyone credit me. Each of those is a question about the self, none is a question about the work, and together they account for most of the interval between a person seeing what needs doing and doing it. Remove them and the agency is not diminished. It is unblocked.

The released self is not the self that does nothing. It is the self that does the next real thing without first negotiating with itself about whether it is entitled to.

The witnesses are the evidence and it is worth seeing precisely what they did not do. None of them wrote about whether they had the standing to testify. Levi did not open by establishing his authority, Ramos did not apologise for the plainness of his prose, dos Anjos did not defend his vocabulary against the critics who had already refused it. They wrote. That is what an unblocked agency looks like from outside, and it is characteristically mistaken for confidence, which is a different thing and much more expensive to maintain.

The Loop, and Why the Release Is Not a Loss

There is a mathematical form to this and it belongs in the book that ends at a limit.

A bounded system that preserves its measure returns — Poincaré’s recurrence theorem, which says that almost every state comes arbitrarily close to itself again, given enough time. Not the same state. Arbitrarily close, and then again, and again. A limit cycle does the same thing more obviously: the trajectory is not going anywhere new, and it is not therefore going nowhere.

This is what makes the release survivable rather than bleak. If the self were an essence, letting go of it would be a subtraction — something gone that was there before. If it is a trajectory in a bounded system, the release is not a subtraction at all. The pattern recurs. It comes back around, close enough to recognise, differing in the ways the passage made it differ. You do not lose yourself by stopping the defence, any more than the water at the bottom of the valley has lost something the water at the top possessed. It has a course now. It did not have one before.

[CONJECTURE] And this is the point at which the traditions in this book’s gallery all say something and say it in incompatible vocabularies: that the release of the particular self is the condition of arriving anywhere, and that what is on the other side of it is not less than what was given up. Whether their accounts of that are describing a common structure or converging by cultural transmission is exactly the sort of question this series marks as checkable-in-principle and unchecked. It is flagged here rather than claimed.

The Guard Is Not Fear

Now the practical half, and it is the reason this belongs in a book of method rather than a book of consolation.

The hand comes up across the face and an opponent reads it as fear. He charges. It is a mistake, and it is the most common one in the roda, because the raised hand is the only hand not yet doing anything — and the weight is already back, and the foot that trailed away from him is not retreating. It is loaded. He is reading the front of a body whose power is being kept at the back. The stingray lies flat and apparently does nothing, and the sting comes from the part that trailed behind.

This corpus has been doing that all along without naming it. It prints the defect list rather than the green badge. It marks the timelines [OPEN] before anyone thinks to ask. It publishes five of its own theorems filed under claims they do not support, on the front page of its own journal, the same week it built the machine that found them. To a reader looking for weakness, every one of those is a guard going up, and in they come.

What they have walked into is a body that has already conceded everything concedable. There is no blow to land on a man who published the list of his own errors this morning. The only ground left to attack from is ground he cleared himself — and he cleared it, and he is standing on the edge of it, singing.

It is not a trick, and that is exactly why it works. The openness is real. It simply happens to be unassailable, and a posture can be both honest and undefeatable without the honesty being in service of the undefeat. [FAITH] This book bets that the same holds at larger scale: that a civilisation which can say plainly what it does not know is in a stronger position than one that cannot, and not only a more admirable one. That is a bet. It is stated as one. Nothing proves it.

But it is what the ginga has been demonstrating, four hundred years, in cleared circles, without writing any of it down: the guard goes up, the weight is already back, and the part they are not looking at is the part that moves.
