**Title:** Is Hodge-type positivity on algebraic classes of a product of two surfaces over F_p known beyond the product-generated part?

Let T, S be smooth projective surfaces over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, X = T × S with ample h = h_T + h_S, and P^2 the primitive algebraic classes in codimension 2 modulo numerical equivalence. Grothendieck's Hodge-type standard conjecture predicts (−1)^2 deg(z² ) > 0 on nonzero z in P^2.

On the subspace generated by products of divisors the form is a product of two negative definite forms (Hodge index on each factor), hence positive definite, and I can write an LDL^T certificate over Q for any given Gram data. What I cannot find is a statement covering algebraic classes in H^2(T) ⊗ H^2(S) (and the H^1 ⊗ H^3 parts) that are not sums of products of divisors.

1. Is positivity on these classes known, for example via results on abelian varieties (Milne, arXiv 2611.00244, Thm 3.5 as I read it), and how far does that reach?
2. If not known, is there a known reason the Frobenius action alone cannot decide it? (A matrix F = q²I with J = diag(1,−1) satisfies FᵀJF = q⁴J with J indefinite, so a purely formal argument fails.)

I am not claiming a proof of anything; scripts and exact computations for P²×P², P³×P³ and the surface-product setup are in the standard-conjectures folder of https://github.com/ (repo link to be filled by the author).
