Only the third number is evidence

Project 1080 is this corpus’s attempt to say, in public and in numbers, how much of its mathematics a machine has actually checked. The name comes from the page that carries the registry. This paper reports the first sweep of that registry across every repository rather than whichever one happened to be open, and it reports something the earlier counts could not: the three numbers the registry publishes are not three views of one quantity. They are three different quantities, they differ by an order of magnitude, and only the smallest of them is evidence of anything.

Scope, stated first

This paper makes no claim about whether any dm³ result is true, no claim that a kernel-checked theorem is an interesting theorem, and no claim that the corpus is more verified than the figures below state. It is a counting paper. It says what exists, what has been through Lean’s kernel, what has not, and which published figures do not survive contact with the filesystem. VALUE PREMISE A framework that publishes a verification count without publishing the command that regenerates it is asking to be trusted rather than checked.

§ 1 · The occasion

The corpus outgrew hand-counting

The registry’s figures were produced by a text scan of one repository. The corpus is thirteen repositories. Files are duplicated across them at different vintages, so the same file name resolves to different content depending on which checkout is read, and a total quoted from one checkout is not a total.

The sharpest illustration is a single file. CatGT_Main.lean and its neighbours yield 227 declarations if counted in AXLE/CatGT/, 36 in a working copy under Projects/io, and 19 in TOTOGT/io itself — the repository with the git remote, and the only one of the three whose contents are under continuous integration. The 227 is inflated by six Main_v* versions of the same material that exist in no repository of record. The 36 is a stale working copy. The 19 is the number.

Three answers to one question, all obtained honestly, differing by a factor of twelve. That is a property of a corpus this size, not an accident, and it does not improve with care. It improves with a map.

§ 2 · Disambiguation

Three words that are not synonyms

The registry publishes three tiers. They have been read as three levels of confidence in one quantity. They are three different quantities, and the gap between them is where every overstated claim in this corpus has lived.

TierWhat it meansWhat it rules out
WrittenA theorem or lemma declaration exists in a file.Nothing. It does not even mean the file compiles.
Sorry-freeNo sorry appears in the proof body. That the author left an explicit hole. Nothing else. theorem X : True := by trivial is sorry-free; so is a biconditional whose hypothesis is assumed on both sides. Both have been found in this corpus and both were withdrawn.
Kernel-audited#print axioms was run on that named declaration and reported nothing outside [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. That the proof is admitted, and that it rests on an undeclared axiom. It still does not rule out that the statement says nothing.

Two consequences follow, and both have cost corrections here. Compiling is not proving: sorry raises a warning, so lake build exits 0 on an admitted proof. Printing is not gating: #print axioms emits info, so a probe reporting sorryAx scrolls past inside a build that still reports success. A gate has to read the output and refuse.

The limit of the third tier, stated by the corpus itself

From vol1-proofs/tools/run.sh: “Neither lake build exiting 0 nor grepping for ‘sorry’ can decide anything — a theorem can be sorry-free and vacuous. Only #print axioms sees both.” And from OPEN_QUESTIONS.md: “A kernel check certifies the proof, not the interest of the statement.” OPEN Vacuity is a fourth question the third tier does not answer. One repository scans for it; four do not.

What the ladder result does and does not give

The theorem bounds the sequence, not the constant: a(m+k) < 2m+k for all m yields ηk ≤ 2. OPEN The strict inequality ηk < 2 needs a limit argument no file in the corpus contains. And the fractal dimension dn = log(n)/log(ηn), with dn → 1, has no Lean statement anywhere: a search across every repository for a dimension declaration returns nothing. Where that formula appears in prose it is MODEL, not a result.

§ 6 · The gap

Four of five checkers print without gating

Five verification setups exist across the corpus. Only one of them would fail if a proof were replaced by sorry tomorrow.

RepositoryStateWould a new sorryAx fail it?
vol1-proofsFive stages: gate self-test, build, probe, axiom gate, vacuity scan whose fixtures must fireYes
GTCTProbe list generated from source; axiom step ends || trueNo — though the badge would turn red, since it parses the registry CSV
iolake env lean prints; nothing parses the outputNo
geometryGate written and tested; workflow file uncommittedNot yet running
AXLEGates exist as local scripts; no CI on any branchNo

OPEN The repair is small and identical in each case: parse the probe output and exit non-zero on sorryAx or on any axiom outside the allowlist. The gate already exists, tested, in two repositories. It is a copy, not a design problem.

VALUE PREMISE A check that runs and cannot fail is worse than no check, because it produces a green mark that a reader is entitled to believe.

§ 7 · Claims with no repository behind them

Two results that cannot be re-run

The Jacobian result. book7/jacobian-verification.html carries the axiom line for not_injective_despite_constant_jacobian pasted into the page. No .lean file with that declaration exists in any repository in the corpus. The output is real; the source is not present, so the claim cannot be regenerated by anyone, including its author. OPEN This is the failure mode WP-78 named: an artifact whose only copy lives inside the document that cites it.

The Galois result. chGal-galois.html carries #print axioms Theorem_Galois — expect sorryAx: this theorem is OPEN. It is correctly disclosed, contributes nothing to the ledger, and needs no repair. It is recorded here because a disclosed open problem and an unreproducible closed one look similar in a bibliography and are opposites.

§ 8 · The duplication tax

The same file at several vintages

Of 127 Lean files in AXLE, grouping version-suffixed copies reduces 1,407 declarations to 765 — forty-six per cent of the raw count is duplicate material. AutophagyDm3_v2.lean exists in four places, two of them byte-identical and two divergent. certify_rstar.py exists in seven. A corpus-wide total that does not group these is not wrong so much as meaningless.

The tax is not only in counting. A verifier was written this month to probe PrincipiaVol1 and AutophagyDm3_v2 — work vol1-proofs had been doing, gated and with a vacuity stage, for two days already. AXLE even contained a pointer script placed there to prevent exactly that duplication, reading: “AXLE is too large to build for a single check — that is the whole reason the little repos exist. This script exists only so that anyone who looks for the Volume I verifier in AXLE finds the pointer instead of a dead end.” The pointer was not read. CLOSED 2026-08-27 The duplicate verifier is retired; the pointer now carries the current count.

§ 9 · What this paper does not claim

Four disclaimers

It does not claim the corpus is verified. 173 of 2,234 declarations have been through the kernel. The other 2,061 have not, and most of them are in files no build target compiles.

It does not claim the 173 are interesting. A kernel check certifies a proof, not a statement. One repository scans for vacuous conclusions; the rest do not, and vacuous statements have been found and withdrawn in this corpus before.

It does not claim the map is complete. Five repositories were not reachable when the sweep ran, including DM3-lab, the third most-referenced in the corpus. Their declarations are absent from every figure above.

It does not claim these numbers will hold. They are dated. The point of the tooling is that re-deriving them costs one command rather than one afternoon.
