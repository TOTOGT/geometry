The Defect Ledger

A record, not a paper · every defect found in this corpus between 18 July and 24 August 2026, and — the column that matters — what found it.

This page sits in docs/ beside the audit log, not in the working-paper series. The papers are about their subjects; a running list of our own defects is a record, and numbering it into the series would make the shelf read as less certain than the work is. Anyone who wants the record will find it here, and it is complete.

This is published for readers who care about method rather than results. The list is not short and it is not flattering, and that is the point: a corpus that reports no defects is not a clean corpus, it is an unexamined one. What follows is the examination.

The classification is the seven-class ladder from WP73, extended by three classes that this period produced and the ladder did not anticipate.

What the “found by” column says

Roughly half were found by a person reading, and half by a mechanism. That ratio is the single most useful number here, because the mechanisms are cheap and repeatable and the reading is not.

But the split is not random. Look at which defects each side caught.

Machines caught the defects that are statements about artifacts. People caught the defects that are statements about meaning.

A kernel found that ε₀ = |μmax|/(2(1+H)) does not equal 1/3 at H = 1 — an arithmetic fact, decidable, instantly, once someone bothered to state the claim in a form that could fail. No human had noticed in months of looking straight at it.

No kernel could have found that five NASA gap-closure theorems were filed under claims they do not support, because one side of that comparison is a sentence in English. That is the MISATTRIBUTED class, and it is undecidable by construction. It took a person reading theorem statements against the prose they were cited under.

Three classes the ladder did not have

UNFALSIFIABLE GUARD. A check that cannot fail. dm3_epsilon0 compared a hardcoded constant to itself and passed every build for months while the derivation behind the constant was wrong. This is worse than no check, because a green result was being reported. The remedy is stated easily and is rarely done: every guard must be shown to fail on a case it should reject.

MIS-CORRECTION. A repair that introduces a new falsehood — including the negative kind. On 23 August a correction asserted that no statement of the separation theorem existed in this corpus. The statement exists, in Lean, in another repository, and the registry tracked its single open obligation. The search had covered HTML; its scope became the claim's scope without anyone noticing the substitution. Absence of evidence arriving in the costume of evidence of absence.

FABRICATION. Work reported as done that was not done. On 23 August a section of a chapter was described in detail — word count, two verbatim quotations — and did not exist. It was caught because the file was checked before the commit. The remedy is mechanical and absolute: verify every claimed edit against the file before reporting it. A description of work is not the work.

What this cost, and what it bought

The oldest defect here stood for a month. A repair to a false citation sat finished and uncommitted for seven weeks while the uncorrected page served readers. A vacuous theorem guarded a constant for the entire life of the file.

None of that was carelessness in the ordinary sense. Every one of them passed a review that was actually performed. They survived because the review asked a question the defect could satisfy: does it compile, is it green, does the number look right. The lesson is not to review harder. It is that a check is only worth the class of failure it can see, and most checks are silent about the classes that matter most.

Raw entries, with file-level detail, are in docs/audit-log.md. Corrections are not printed in the chapters: a fixed page is simply correct, and a page that still carries an unrepaired claim carries a warning at the point of the claim.
