## Qualifications (2026-11-15; these supersede the wording of the rows above)

**E13 Wronskian.** Definitions: s = sigma + i t; -zeta'(s)/zeta(s) = c - i g (c = Re, g = -Im; for sigma > 1 these are the Lambda-series of the page; for sigma <= 1 they are the meromorphic continuation, because the series do not define them there); W = c dg/dt - g dc/dt with d/dt of the analytic function = i H'(s). Explicit values (docs/evidence-wronskian-points-2026-11-15.txt; 3 routes: analytic, central differences, numerical derivative of zeta): (0.99, 286/3): c = -0.445808448983, g = -0.0240931376565, |zeta| = 0.461193, W = -0.454179456877189; (0.9, 60): W = -0.0439451856640713; (0.25, 59.35): W = -81.0525444479808. Spread across 30/50/80 digits <= 4e-29; central differences with h = 1e-3, 1e-5, 1e-7 converge to the analytic value (|diff| 2e-6, 2e-10, 2e-14 at the first point); none of the three points is near a zero (|zeta| 0.46-0.50). One regular point with W < 0 refutes "W > 0 for all 0 < sigma < 1, sigma != 1/2", and (0.99, 286/3) lies in 1/2 < sigma < 1. The sampled critical-line values (495 points, positive) establish only those samples. The reduction of "dalpha|ker alpha positive-definite" to the sign of W is the page's own and was not re-derived here (orientation conventions untested).

**E8 positive mean vs sign changes.** These are separate arguments. (i) A positive mean (sum Lambda(n)^2 log n / n^(2 sigma), checked numerically at sigma = 2: 0.052619 vs 0.052549 at horizon 4000, and the double-series closed form W = sum Lambda(m)Lambda(n) log n (mn)^-sigma cos(t(log m - log n)) matches the analytic W at sigma=2, t=7.3 to 6e-6 with truncation at 3000) shows only that W is not identically zero; it does not exclude sign changes, and W has them at sigma = 2. (ii) W is real-analytic on sigma > 1 (zeta has no zeros there), so if not identically zero its zeros are isolated: "dense sign changes" is impossible, and non-vanishing on an open dense set of t is what follows. Only log m != log n for m != n is used, not the Q-linear independence of the log p. For 1/2 < sigma <= 1 W is real-analytic wherever zeta(sigma + i t) != 0; the same conclusion needs W not identically zero on an interval, which is not proved.

**E9 Dirichlet series.** Justified: the series for c and g converge absolutely for sigma > 1. For sigma < 1 the complex series sum Lambda(n) n^-s diverges for every t, because convergence at s0 with Re s0 < 1 would give convergence and analyticity on Re s > Re s0, which contains the pole of -zeta'/zeta at s = 1. That concerns the pair: at least one of c, g diverges at each such (sigma, t); at t = 0 every term of g is zero (g converges to 0) while c diverges. sigma = 1 is not addressed. The partial-sum run at (0.75, 20) (0.194, 0.750, -0.348, -0.500 for N = 1e3..2e5; continuation 0.324) is consistent with divergence and is not a proof; the earlier remark about growth like N^0.25 was not verified and is withdrawn.

**E1 neighbour counts.** Neighbour = tile sharing a boundary segment of positive length. The aligned rectangular grid gives every tile 4 neighbours. The 8-neighbour tiling is a specific arrangement of congruent 1x3 rectangles: period 3 columns x 4 rows, one row of horizontal bricks, then three rows of vertical bricks in each column; horizontal bricks 8 neighbours, vertical bricks 4 (mean 5.00; checked on a 12x8 torus). The mean-at-most-6 statement assumes a periodic tiling by finite-area discs, so that the contact graph is a simple graph embedded in the torus quotient (Euler); it is cited, not proved here, and the script checks only this example.

**E4-E6 acoustics.** "No uniform stair gives an ascending chirp" is restricted to the model analysed: straight flight of equal steps, reflections from point reflectors at the step edges P + n v, source and listener together. In that model the non-strict statement (delay increments never decrease) follows from the triangle inequality for every listener position; the strict statement holds when the listener is off the line through the edges (sampled, 0 exceptions in 20000; not proved). Not covered: reflections from extended tread/riser surfaces, separated source and listener, curved or non-uniform stairs, diffraction. Lubman source (read 2026-11-15 via a page reader, paraphrased): 1310 Hz is described as "about" the maximum chirp starting frequency, from the average tread 26.2 cm; 922 Hz is the minimum chirp ending frequency, from the 37.3 cm hypotenuse (average riser 26.4 cm); both are predictions from f ~ c/T with c about 343 m/s; no listener position is given; the page says measured frequencies agree "reasonably well" and gives no measured values; the page does not state an interval in octaves; log2(1310/922) = 0.508. The "about an octave" figure comes from the sonicwonders.org page. **Open:** Lubman's f ~ c/(tread) and the chapter's f0 = c/(2L) differ by a factor 2 in absolute pitch for the same spacing (for a distant listener the round trip per step is about 2 x tread, which favours c/(2 tread), about 655 Hz for a 26.2 cm tread, against 1310 Hz); the octave fall is unaffected; not resolved, and the chapter states no absolute pitch.

## ch22 (The Gauss Map) — audit 2026-11-15, findings only, no page edits

Evidence: `book4/ch22_audit_check.py` (exit 0, sympy). The page has no script of its own; its footer box lists only `discriminant` (book21/Spiral.lean:75, a 2x2 spiral-matrix identity), which warrants nothing in this chapter.

- **F22-1 (false for non-hypersurfaces, §22.5-22.6).** gamma is defined as X -> (P^n)^v sending x to "its embedded tangent hyperplane", and Con(X) is called "the closure of the graph of gamma". Tangent space is a hyperplane only when dim X = n-1. In general Con(X) has dim n-1 (page says so) while the graph of the tangent-space map has dim X: P^3 curve 2 vs 1; P^4 surface 3 vs 2. Con(X) is a P^(n-1-k)-bundle over X_sm, not a graph; X^v is the image of Con(X) under pi^v, not the image of gamma. Statement is correct for hypersurfaces. Also edge case: X = P^n gives Con(X) empty, so "any irreducible X" needs X proper.
- **F22-2 (cylinder, §22.3).** "Where the map folds — where det dN = 0 — the surface is parabolic." Parabolic is right (K=0), "folds" is not implied: the page's own cylinder has det dN = 0 everywhere with dN of rank 1 everywhere (verified), so the Gauss map is degenerate, not a fold. Fold along the parabolic curve and cusps at isolated points are generic behaviour.
- **F22-3 (attribution, §22.7 and inventory).** "Precisely the setting of Whitney's theorem" / "Classical, Whitney". Whitney's theorem is about generic maps R^2->R^2. Gauss maps are not generic maps (they are gradient/Lagrangian-type); the fold/cusp result for generic surfaces is the Thom / Banchoff-Gaffney-McCrory result (cited from memory, unchecked). Conclusion for generic surfaces is true; the cited warrant is the wrong theorem.
- **F22-4 (overstated, §22.7).** "The classification of such singularities is ADE is a theorem": Arnold's ADE list classifies simple (finite-modality) Legendrian singularities, not all of them.
- **F22-5 (overstated, §22.4).** "Drift from zero is a discretisation error, not a discovery": int K dA = 0 needs a closed, embedded, C^2 torus (verified exactly for a torus of revolution, K = cos v/(2+cos v), integral 0). A computed non-smooth or self-intersecting invariant set, or a truncated patch, can also drift. Neimark-Sacker for a map gives an invariant circle; the torus is the flow case.
