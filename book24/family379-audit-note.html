Which hypothesis supports which step: an audit of the matrix-multiplication papers in OpenAI’s Family 379

A partial technical audit, dated 2026-11-25 and revised 2026-11-28, of the Euclidean-geometry companion, the circuit lower-bound companion and the main nine-fourths paper. It tracks dependencies between hypotheses. It does not verify, refute or re-derive the theorems.

Scope and limitsSources were read at commit 92b516ef8e2abf66c3db7aa31438d3010bc7956f of openai/math. Page numbers are those of the PDFs at that commit. Bracket numbers differ between the papers, so this note names them EC (Euclidean geometry in the asymptotic spectrum of tensors), CC (Circuit lower bounds for Hamiltonian cycle and the permanent) and MP (The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths). Not audited: the proofs of CC Theorem 5.1 and Theorem 6.1, CC Theorem 14.1 and the Section 13 compressor, the compactness step in EC Lemma 11.3, MP Section 11 (the analytic core), and the October paper’s Appendix A. Nothing here has been compiled or reviewed by a third party.

1 · Cube exclusion implies ports; the converse is not established

Definition (EC 11.1, p. 54). A field has ports if there are a family of regular symmetric multigraphs of unbounded order and bounded degree and a constant γ with V(x) ≤ γ·EG(x) for every graph in the family and every assignment x into a spectral space over the field. This depends only on the characteristic (EC Lemma 2.3, scalar extension).

Proposition 11.2 (EC, pp. 55–58). If a finite field F has a largest spectral flat dimension k, then F has ports. The inputs are cube exclusion (EC Section 7, p. 30) and Lemma 11.3, which gives η > 0 with Bm ≤ (m−η)B1 on the Grassmannian of m-subspaces of F22m, for m a power of two above k+1.

What was found. One direction. The audit found no statement in EC or CC of the form “no ports if and only if flats of every dimension exist,” and no converse of Proposition 11.2. Reading ports as “the cube-exclusion case” goes beyond what is proved; cube exclusion is sufficient for ports, not known to be necessary.

2 · Theorem 12.1: the field in the statement, the ports in the heading

Printed statement (CC Theorem 12.1, p. 55). “Fix a finite field F of characteristic χ. There are constants σ, δ > 0 such that, for all sufficiently large n and every 1 ≤ k ≤ 2nσ, every trivalent bit network over F realizing the batch (Un*)⊗k has volume greater than k·n1+δ.” The statement begins with a finite field and prints no ports hypothesis.

Where the hypothesis is supplied. Part II of CC opens (p. 54) “Fix a characteristic χ admitting ports, in the sense of Section 2,” and Section 13 (p. 56) repeats “Assume throughout this section that the characteristic admits ports.” Section 13 and Theorem 14.1 are the compression step on which the proof of 12.1 rests (proof in Section 15: a diagonal argument comparing two evaluations of one pinned contraction modulo p). So Theorem 12.1 holds under ports, even though a reader of the displayed statement alone would not see this.

Where it is invoked. MP Section 4.3 begins “Fix a finite field with ports” and then cites CC Theorem 12.1 for constants δ, ξ with volk((Uℓ*)⊗j) > jℓ1+δ for j up to 2ℓξ, and EC Proposition 14.1 (p. 71, stated for a finite field with ports in the sense of EC Definition 11.1) for the descent constant. The invocation therefore sits inside the ports hypothesis. The earlier worry, that MP might cite Theorem 12.1 without saying ports, is not borne out by the passage read; the heading-level placement is a presentation issue in CC, not a gap in MP’s use.

Dependencies not audited. Theorem 14.1, the Section 13 compressor identities, and the bit counts for the copy step. Theorem 12.1 is only as good as the port compression it uses. The no-ports side (MP Lemma 4.1, which begins “Let the field have no ports,” with CC Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 5.2) is the other branch of MP Theorem 2.2.

3 · Does any characteristic admit ports? An unresolved existence question

Ports versus no ports is a dichotomy by definition, and CC says that either outcome gives a circuit lower bound with no classification of characteristics required. Beyond that, the audit found no example of a characteristic that admits ports and no argument that none does. The only geometric route to ports found in the sources is Proposition 11.2, and EC Section 16 derives a contradiction from the assumption of a finite largest flat dimension, giving Theorem 1.2: spectral flats of every dimension exist over every finite field. So the hypothesis of Proposition 11.2 is never satisfied. It is used inside a proof by contradiction.

Bearing on the conditional results. Everything stated under “fix a characteristic admitting ports” (CC Part II including Theorem 12.1, EC Proposition 14.1, MP Section 4.3) is a conditional statement. If no characteristic admits ports these statements are true but vacuous. That does not weaken the main theorem, because the no-ports branch then covers every field, and the logic is sound either way. It does mean that a reader cannot tell from the text which branch is populated, and that the correctness of the main theorem rests on the no-ports machinery in the case where the ports branch is empty. The question is open as far as these sources go.

4 · The sorting exponent p < 2 depends on the shared-price hypothesis

In the Lean library at the pinned commit, the theorem OAI.LinearAlgebra.MatrixExponent.Routing.sorting_of_shared_price (file MatrixExponent/Routing/Dual.lean, line 319) has the statement (hs : Prices.SharedPrice (AlgebraicClosure F)) : ∃ p : ℜ, 1 < p ∧ p < 2 ∧ Lookup.Sorting F p. The exponent p < 2 exists only under the shared-price hypothesis. That hypothesis is the one the overall proof argues against, so p < 2 is a conditional output from a proof by contradiction and not an unconditional sorting algorithm. It enters network compression through compresses_of_stages in Obstruction.lean. On the paper side, EC Lemma 14.2 (Leighton column-sort lifting) and Theorem 10.1 supply the sorting machinery; the MP derivation of p < 2 and its use in MP Section 27, where ρ = p − 1 < 1 appears, were checked only for internal arithmetic, conditional on the hypothesis.

5 · What the Lean files establish

Read from source, not compiled. The headline theorem omega_eq_and_rigidity states omega F = 9/4 ∧ Rectangle.Rigidity F for every field in a formal rational-circuit model, where omega is the infimum of s such that n×n products are computable by straight-line programs over the fraction field of a polynomial ring with at most C·ns+ε steps. Rectangle.Rigidity is a disjunction, weaker than the full rectangular formula. The library contains no notion of CAT(0) space, Poincaré inequality or ports, so it does not formalize the ports split or MP Theorem 2.2; it reaches the lower bound by a case split on Prices.SharedPrice (contact_of_not_shared) and a descent route (Processor.hard_of_descent). The repository’s catalogue lists as unformalized, among others, the n9/4/2 gate bound at each side length and the infinite-dimensional Hilbert extension. The library therefore would, if it compiles, support the exponent in its formal model; it does not verify the geometric companion theorems as written.

Source scan (this release, informational). Over the 468 files of the lean/ tree and the 1,092 files of the extra import closure: no axiom declarations and no native_decide. sorry occurs in one file, ComparatorChallenges/MatrixExponent.lean (lines 108, 119, 132), where it marks the three challenge statements; unsafe occurs once, in lakefile.lean line 313 (a build-script line). The scan cannot tell a real proof from a vacuous one.

Kernel status: unverified. The pinned toolchain is Lean v4.34.1 and the Mathlib revision is d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612. A job in this repository compiles the files and prints #print axioms for each declaration listed in the release index. Until it has run, no declaration is reported as verified.

6 · Corrections to earlier readings

An earlier reading in the audit equated ports with cube exclusion and no ports with flats of every dimension. It is withdrawn; the right statement is the one-directional implication in section 1. A concern that MP cited Theorem 12.1 without its ports hypothesis was raised on 2026-11-25 and resolved on 2026-11-28 by the passage quoted in section 2.
