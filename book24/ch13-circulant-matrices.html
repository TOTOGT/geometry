Circulant matrices: the Fourier basis diagonalises them

If every row of a matrix is the row above it shifted one place, the matrix depends only on the gap i − j, and its eigenvectors are sines and cosines. That one fact explains why a ring of masses has the normal modes it has, why a ring of oscillators linearises the way it does, and why the months sit on a circle inside a word-embedding model. This chapter proves it, checks it, and keeps apart what the three cases share (an equation) from what they do not (anything else).

1 · The statement

Intuition

Shift a pattern around a ring and it looks the same, only moved. A wave that fits the ring evenly, one crest, two crests, three, is moved too, but stays a wave of the same kind. A circulant matrix treats every place on the ring alike, so it can only stretch such waves, never mix one with another. Each wave is an eigenvector, and its eigenvalue says how strongly the matrix stretches it.

Statement
Circulant matrixFix n and numbers c0, …, cn−1, indices taken mod n. The circulant C has entries Cij = c(i−j) mod n.
Theorem (standard)Let ω = e2πi/n and v(k) the vector with entries v(k)j = ωjk, for k = 0, …, n−1. Then C v(k) = λk v(k) with
λk = Σd=0..n−1 cd ω−dk
The n vectors are orthogonal, so C is diagonalised by a unitary matrix, and C is normal. If the numbers are real and symmetric (cd = cn−d), then λk = Σ cd cos(2πkd/n) is real and λk = λn−k.
Proof

Direct. (Cv(k))i = Σj ci−j ωjk. Put d = i − j; as j runs over all residues so does d, and ωjk = ωik ω−dk. So the sum is ωik Σd cd ω−dk = λk v(k)i.

Through the shift. Let S be the cyclic shift, (Sv)i = vi−1. Then C = Σd cd Sd, a polynomial in S. Since Sv(k) = ω−k v(k) and the n numbers ω−k are distinct, S has n distinct eigenvalues, so its eigenvectors form a basis (Chapter 7). Every polynomial in S has the same eigenvectors, which gives the theorem a second proof.

Orthogonality. ⟨v(k), v(l)⟩ = Σj ωj(l−k) is n if k = l and a full geometric sum, equal to 0, otherwise. Dividing by √n gives a unitary matrix U with C = U diag(λ) U*, hence C*C = U |λ|² U* = CC*.

Symmetric case. cd = cn−d pairs the terms d and n−d, and ω−dk + ωdk = 2cos(2πkd/n). And cos(2π(n−k)d/n) = cos(2πkd/n).

Remarks

The pairing λk = λn−k means the eigenspace for 0 < k < n/2 is two-dimensional, spanned by a cosine and a sine of the same wavelength. Two equal eigenvalues are what make a picture a circle and not an ellipse (Book XVI, Chapter 2). Because C is normal, Chapter 11 applies: the norm of C is its largest eigenvalue modulus.

2 · What the script checked

Checks

All of these are in ch13-verify.py (standard library only).

  1. The eigenvector relation C v(k) = λk v(k), with the formula above, holds for all 343 integer circulants with n = 3 and entries −3..3, all 2401 with n = 4, and 300 pseudo-random ones for n = 5 to 12 (3044 matrices; worst error below 10−12).
  2. C CT = CT C holds exactly, in integers, for the 343 + 2401 exhaustive cases.
  3. The 2-norm of 300 random real circulants equals max |λk| to within 10−9.
  4. For 100 symmetric circulants with n = 12, λk = λn−k.

3 · Three rings, one equation

The same theorem appears in three settings. In each, only the equation is shared.

A ring of masses

n equal masses m on a ring, joined to neighbours by springs of stiffness K, obey m ẍ = −K C x with c = (2, −1, 0, …, 0, −1). Then λk = 2 − 2cos(2πk/n) = 4 sin²(πk/n), and the normal-mode frequencies are ωk = 2√(K/m) |sin(πk/n)|. With wavenumber q = 2πk/(na), that is ω = 2√(K/m) |sin(qa/2)|, the chain dispersion law (with ends joined into a ring instead of fixed). The script checks the eigenvalue formula for n = 3 to 12.

A ring of nonlinear oscillators

Take n = 8 Kuramoto-type oscillators, θ̇i = K [sin(θi+1 − θi) + sin(θi−1 − θi)]. At the synchronised state (all θi equal) the Jacobian, computed numerically by central differences, equals the circulant with c = (−2K, K, 0, …, 0, K), to within 10−6. Its eigenvalues are −4K sin²(πk/n): every mode decays, the k = 0 mode (all phases moving together) does not. This is one worked example, not a theorem about all nonlinear rings.

They decrease from k = 1 to k = 6, so the dominant non-constant mode is the single turn around the ring. Its two equal eigenvalues (cosine and sine) are why the months appear on a circle.

What is shared, and what is notThe three settings share one equation, a circulant diagonalised by Fourier modes, and nothing else: one is mechanics, one is dynamics near a synchronised state, one is statistics of a text. That is a shared functional form, not a shared invariant. The mathematics does not say that a ring of springs, a ring of oscillators and a vocabulary of words have a common structure, only that any system whose rule depends on the gap alone has these modes.

4 · The ends of the ring

Remarks

Cut the ring open and the matrix depends on the gap but is no longer circulant: it is a Toeplitz matrix. Its eigenvectors are no longer exact Fourier vectors. In Book XVI, Chapter 2 the Toeplitz matrix exp(−|i − j|/6) on 30 points has eigenvectors with 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 sign changes, like waves fitted into a segment, but this chapter proves nothing about them.

5 · What is not here

Remarks

There is no Lean file for this chapter, although this book intends chapters from Chapter 3 on to carry one. The theorem is standard and could be formalised, but I have not written the statements, so nothing here is machine-checked. The numerical checks are floating point, with the tolerances stated, except where integers are compared exactly. The Toeplitz case, the convergence of finite rings to the open line, and the nonlinear dynamics beyond one worked example are not developed.

6 · Exercises

  1. Compute the eigenvalues of circ(1, 2, 0, 0) by hand, and check them against the formula λk = Σ cd ω−dk with ω = i.
  2. Show that the product of two circulants is a circulant, and that circulants commute. (Hint: polynomials in S.)
  3. Find the eigenvalues of circ(0, 1, 0, …, 0), the shift itself, and say which of them is real.
  4. Show that for the ring of masses the k = 0 mode has frequency 0 and say what motion it is. Why is it excluded when the ring is fixed to a wall?
  5. For which symmetric circulants with n = 12 is λ2 > λ1? What would the months look like if it were?
